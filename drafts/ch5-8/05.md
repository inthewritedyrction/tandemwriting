# CHAPTER 5: SILAS

Three hours on the step, and the only thing that had tried to kill Silas was the rain.

It came off the cavern ceiling in long, steady ropes, soaked through his collar, and found every place Jerrick had hit him. His left shoulder locked up a little more every hour. Gutterlight roared somewhere behind the rooftops whenever a match ended, and in between, the street in front of the dark house stayed empty enough that his hand kept drifting toward his knife without being told.

He'd counted everybody who walked past. A drunk Ring fan arguing with his own shadow. Two girls in work coats sharing an umbrella with a hole in it. A boy with a cart of empty bottles who looked at Silas's face, looked at the knife hand resting on his knee, and found a reason to cross the street. Not one of them had been worth getting up for.

His phone buzzed against his thigh.

Remy: You home?

Silas: Working.

Remy: That ain't a place.

Silas: It is tonight.

Remy: Dante?

Silas didn't answer that. Remy knew what no answer meant. Three dots showed up and went away twice before the next message came through.

Remy: Eat something. I'm bringing food in the morning.

Silas: You don't know where I am.

Remy: Lorraine do.

Naturally, Lorraine did. Silas put the phone away before he had to think about how many people in this city knew more about his night than he did.

Jude hadn't texted at all, which meant the token was still on his bench and Jude was still bent over it with his glasses on top of his head, ignoring everybody, including God. Rhea hadn't texted either. That was normal. She texted when she wanted to know if he was alive and went quiet when she didn't want him knowing she cared.

Behind him, the lock turned.

He was on his feet with the knife out before Camille's thread finished loosening. The door opened about four inches. Melena stood in the gap with the hood down and her hair out of its last pins, red as a warning lamp in the dark of the hall. She looked at the knife.

He put it away.

"You said you'd sit," she said.

"I was sitting."

"You've been sitting for three hours."

"That's what sitting is."

She didn't think that was funny. Rain blew in across the threshold and spotted the hem of the mourning dress, and she didn't step back from it. Down the street, the Ring went up in another roar, and her eyes cut toward the sound.

"What *is* that?"

"Gutterlight. Somebody's getting hit."

"All night?"

"Till somebody stops getting up."

She took that in without blinking. He was starting to notice that about her. Whatever you handed her, she held it and looked at it before she decided what to do with it, like she'd been raised in a house where you didn't get to drop things.

"I want to say something," she said. "Before I lose my nerve."

"Go ahead."

"In your father's office. You grabbed my wrist."

"You were falling."

"I'm aware of what I was doing."

"You'd rather I let you eat the floor?"

"I'd rather you asked." Her hand had found the edge of the door and was holding it tight. "I have one rule. Nobody touches me without asking. Not you. Not anyone."

Silas looked at her. "And if you're on fire?"

She thought about it longer than he expected. "Ask quickly."

He almost laughed. "There a two?"

"I'll let you know."

The door shut. The lock turned, and the thread drew tight against the frame. Silas stood in the rain and looked at that door for a second, the way you'd look at a dog that had just told you its name.

He sat back down.

His shoulder settled against the doorframe, and he closed his eyes for exactly one breath, and Pell's lane came back before he could stop it. The girl from Ashvein with her hand jammed in her skirt like she had a whole bank in there, looking up at him like he'd personally dumped her salt in the puddle on purpose. Silver down the lengths of her locs. A ring in her nose. Those two Ironcross rings, heavy enough to split a lip if she ever decided to swing.

*I ain't trying to have no debt tied to someone in Shadowmere.*

Most people who got knocked into by a Graves in Gutterlight said sorry first and figured out why later. She'd cussed him out and turned down his money.

He hadn't stopped thinking about it all night. That was a problem, and he didn't have time for problems with silver locs and a smart mouth. He had a girl from seven tiers up behind a door with rules, and a shoulder that wasn't going to be worth a damn by morning.

An hour later, the step was warm.

Silas sat up off the doorframe. It wasn't his body heat. He'd been sitting on cold stone for four hours, and it had stayed cold the whole time. Now it was warm under his thighs and getting warmer, and a thin skin of steam was lifting off the wet stone around his boots.

Behind him, Camille's thread snapped.

He felt it in his back teeth, a hard twang like a string on a bass going all at once. He was on his feet with the token out before he'd decided to stand. The lock plate took the brass. The ward checked him, slower than it should've, like something had already been through it and left it confused.

Smoke hit him in the face when the door opened.

The front room was empty. The cot was untouched. Down the hall, the back-room door hung open six inches, and where the lock used to be, there was a lump of brass the size of his thumb, still glowing at the edges and dripping down the wood in a bright little line.

"Melena."

Nothing.

He shoved the door open the rest of the way. The back room was exactly what Camille had built it to be: four walls, a bed, a lamp, and no windows. That was the point. No windows meant nobody could look in. It also meant the only other way out was the Mercer service door in the back wall, iron, with a Mercer lock and a double line of funeral thread sewn across the seam.

The thread was ash. The lock had run down the iron like candle wax. The door stood open to the back lane, and rain was blowing in across the floor and hissing where it hit.

Silas stood there for one second and let himself think it.

*Dante's gonna kill me.*

He could hear it already. *I gave you one job, son.* And Camille wouldn't say anything at all, which was worse.

He went out the back door.

---

She wasn't hard to follow. She wasn't trying to be.

Rain hit everything in Shadowmere and ran off it, but it didn't run off the places she'd touched. On the brick at the mouth of the back lane, two handprints steamed like somebody had pressed hot irons there. Ten steps on, a drainpipe had a black scorch shaped like a palm. Farther down, in a puddle, the water had gone warm enough to smoke.

She'd gone left at the dye vats, away from the Ring. Toward the market, toward the open lanes, toward the part of Gutterlight where the street widened and you could actually look up and see the cavern ceiling through the mist.

She was looking for sky. Same as in the alley outside Dante's.

"Damn it," Silas said, and ran.

His shoulder screamed every time his left foot hit the stone. He held his arm against his ribs and kept going, past the shuttered pawn counter, past the tailor row, through a gap between two buildings so narrow his coat dragged both walls. A woman leaning out a window above him yelled something about his mama. He didn't stop to answer.

He came out onto Lamp Street and the noise went away.

It didn't fade. It went all at once, like a door shutting. One second there was rain hitting tin and a tram grinding somewhere south and the Ring roaring behind him, and the next there was nothing. The rain kept falling. He could see it coming down in the lamplight and bouncing off the stone, and it didn't make a sound. His own boots landed silent. His breath went in and out of him without a single noise he could hear.

The lamps on Lamp Street dimmed to blue.

Everybody on the street knew what that meant, and everybody moved. A man selling skewers pulled his cart into a doorway. Two kids who'd been splashing in the gutter disappeared like somebody had erased them. A woman yanked her shutters closed without a sound. In ten seconds, the whole street was empty except for the rain.

And Melena.

She was standing in the middle of the street with her face turned up and her hood down, the rain running through her hair, and her hands were lit. She wasn't on fire yet, but the black under her nails had climbed to her knuckles, and a dull orange glow was coming through the skin of her palms like a lamp behind a curtain.

At the far end of Lamp Street, the Shrouds came around the corner.

There were two of them. Tall, taller than him, in layered grey veils that hung to the ground and didn't move right in the wind. Bone marks stitched down the front of them. Strips of paper hung from their heads and shoulders, hundreds of them, fluttering, and every strip had writing on it. Names. Silas had seen Shrouds before, the way everybody in Shadowmere had, from a distance, from behind a door, through a crack. He'd never once wanted to see one closer.

They moved down the street without walking. Their veils never touched the ground.

Melena hadn't seen them. She was still looking up.

Silas had about four seconds to think about her rule.

He couldn't ask. Nothing anybody said under Silence came out, and even if it did, the Shrouds would hear it before she did. He couldn't call her name. He couldn't do anything at all except what he was about to do, and she was going to hate him for it, and he was going to do it anyway.

He crossed the street in six silent strides and took her.

His right arm went around her waist. He pulled her backward off her feet and into the recessed doorway of a closed threshold shop, and she fought him the whole way. She was stronger than she looked and she didn't fight like a princess. She drove her elbow back at his ribs, his bad side, and found exactly the spot Jerrick had found, and his vision went white for a second. He held on.

Her hands were the problem. They were glowing brighter now, and the light was spilling out across the wet stone in front of the doorway like a sign that said *here*.

Silas grabbed both of her wrists in his left hand and pressed her palms flat against his own chest, under his coat, and closed the coat over them.

It burned.

It burned like grabbing a pot handle you forgot was on the stove, except you couldn't let go. The heat went through his shirt and into his skin and into his palm where it was clamped over the backs of her hands. He felt the skin on his palm go tight, then wet. He bit down on the inside of his cheek and didn't move.

Melena had gone still against him. Her back was pressed to his chest. Her pulse was going so hard he could feel it through her wrists, through his own burned hand.

The Shrouds passed the doorway.

Silas could see them over the top of her head. Up close, the name strips weren't paper. They were thinner than paper and they moved like they were breathing. One Shroud stopped. It turned its veiled head toward the threshold shop, slow, and Silas watched the name strips across its face lift and settle, lift and settle, like something under there was tasting the air.

Melena's hands got hotter.

He pressed them harder against his chest and put his mouth next to her ear even though she couldn't hear him, and breathed out long. Longer than he breathed in. Same as at Pell's. *Count it.*

He didn't know if she felt it. He did it again anyway.

On the third breath, the heat dropped. Not all the way. Just enough that the glow under the coat went from orange to red to almost nothing.

The Shroud turned its head away and moved on.

He held her there until both of them were gone around the far corner, and the lamps on Lamp Street went from blue back to amber, and the sound came back all at once like somebody had kicked a door open. Rain on tin. A tram. The Ring, roaring for somebody who'd just gotten hit in the face. Somewhere above them, a shutter opened and a woman said, "Lord have mercy," to nobody.

Silas let go.

Melena turned around and shoved him hard enough that his back hit the door behind him.

"I said *ask*."

"Couldn't."

"You absolutely could have—"

"Under Silence?" He held up his left hand. The palm was red and shining, a blister already lifting across the middle of it where the backs of her hands had been. "Nothing you say comes out under Silence. That's the whole point of it. Nobody hears you scream, and nobody hears you ask permission either."

She looked at his hand.

He watched the fight go out of her face, fast, and something worse come into it. She looked at the blister like she'd been the one holding the pot.

"I didn't—" she started.

"I know."

"I didn't mean to—"

"I know." He closed the hand and put it in his coat. "You can be mad at me later. Right now, I need you to tell me why you burned through two Mercer locks and a Graves ward to come stand in the middle of the street under the Synod's nose."

Her chin came up. A second later, it dropped. She looked past him, back up at the ceiling of the cavern, where the rain came out of the rock and the mist glowed violet and there wasn't anything else up there at all.

"There are no windows," she said.

Silas waited.

"In that room. There are no windows." Her voice cracked down the middle. "I lay down and I couldn't — I couldn't breathe. It's a box. You put me in a box and you sat on the lid."

He thought about the back room. Four walls and a lamp. Camille had built it like that on purpose, for people running from things, people who wanted walls.

Melena wasn't running from things. She'd lost somebody, and she'd just come down seven tiers to a place with no sky.

"Sky's six tiers up," he said. "You won't find it in Gutterlight."

"I know that."

"You were looking anyway."

She didn't deny it. She pulled the hood back up over her hair with hands that weren't glowing anymore, and when she spoke again her voice had gone flat and careful, like her voice in Dante's office.

"Is your hand going to be all right?"

"Been worse."

"That isn't an answer."

"It's the one I got." He stepped out of the doorway and checked the street both ways. The skewer man was rolling his cart back out like nothing had happened, which was exactly what he'd do tomorrow if anybody asked. "Come on. Walk on my left. If I stop, you stop."

"You said that this afternoon."

"Yeah. You didn't listen this afternoon either."

He started walking. After a second, he heard her boots behind him. Rhea's boots, a size too big, slapping wet stone.

---

Camille was standing in the front room of the dark house when they got back.

She was in her house shoes. She'd come across Gutterlight in the rain in her robe and her house shoes, which meant she hadn't stopped to change after her thread snapped, which meant she was furious. She had her glasses on her face instead of on top of her head. That was worse.

She looked at Silas. She looked at Melena. She looked at the back-room door hanging open with a brass tear running down it.

"Ma," Silas said.

"Sit down."

He sat down on the cot.

Camille turned to Melena, and her face changed. All the fury went somewhere Silas couldn't see it, and what was left was the face she used for folks who came to her door after a funeral. She didn't step closer. She didn't reach.

"You're soaked through, baby," she said. "May I take your coat?"

Melena stared at her.

"I'll hang it by the stove. I won't touch you."

Melena unbuttoned the coat herself and held it out at arm's length. Camille took it by the collar and hung it on the hook by the cold stove, then came back and stood in front of Silas with her arms folded.

"Hand."

He held out the right one.

"Other hand, Silas."

He held out the left. She turned it to the lamp, looked at the blister, and made a sound in her throat he hadn't heard since Jude broke his arm jumping off the tram platform at nine.

"Who did this?"

"Shrouds came through Lamp Street under Silence," Silas said. "She was out in the open. I had to get her out of it."

"I asked who did this."

Melena said, "I did."

Camille didn't look away from his hand. "Did he grab you?"

"Yes."

"Did you tell him not to?"

"Yes."

"Mm-hmm." Camille opened the medical tin from his coat, the one Dante had given him that afternoon, and pulled out a jar of something grey that smelled like river mud. "So you both did something you're gon' have to live with. Hold still."

She packed the burn. It felt like sticking his hand in snow. He hissed through his teeth, and she ignored him and wrapped it in clean cloth until it looked like he was wearing a mitten.

"Shoulder."

"It's fine."

"Your shoulder been fine since last night, I know. Take the shirt off."

He looked at Melena. Melena turned around to face the wall without being asked, and he would've thanked her if he'd had the energy. He got the shirt over his head. Camille pressed two cold fingers under his shoulder blade, and he swore.

"Jerrick?"

"Elbow."

"He's dead and still getting his licks in." She put the shirt back in his lap. "You'll soak it again when you get home. If you ever get home."

She went to the back room.

Silas pulled his shirt on and followed her as far as the doorway. Camille stood in front of the service door with her hand flat on the iron, not touching the slag where the lock had been, just the door. She had her eyes closed. Her lips moved.

When she opened her eyes, she looked older.

"I sewed this ward myself," she said. "Double-stitched it. Three Graves knots at the seam."

"I know."

"She went through it like it was paper."

"I know, Ma."

Camille pulled a spool of funeral thread from her robe pocket. She always had one. Silas had seen her pull funeral thread out of her bra at a wedding. She started laying a new line across the seam of the door, fast, her fingers moving in the old pattern, and the thread hummed as it went down, low enough that he felt it more than heard it.

"It'll hold tonight," she said, still sewing. "It'll hold tomorrow. Maybe the day after, if she sleeps." She tied off the knot and bit the thread. "It won't hold a week."

"Dante said till Rae says."

"Rae better say soon, then." She turned around. "Whatever's in that girl don't answer to Graves thread. It don't answer to death at all. I don't know what it answers to." She looked at him over her glasses. "And neither do you. So stop acting like you do."

He opened his mouth.

"Don't," Camille said.

He closed it.

She went past him into the front room. Melena had turned back around. She was sitting on the very edge of the sofa with her knees together and her hands in her lap, and between her hands was the envelope from Dante's office, the crushed one with the broken red seal.

Camille stopped in front of her. "I'm going to go home and sit up and listen to my thread," she said. "If it moves, I'm coming back. In my house shoes. And I'm too old to be crossing Gutterlight in my house shoes twice in one night, so please, baby. Stay in the house."

Melena looked up at her for a long moment. "I'll try."

"That's more honest than he's ever been with me." Camille picked up her umbrella from by the door. "Silas. Eat something."

"Remy's bringing food in the morning."

"Lord, at least somebody in your life got sense." The door closed behind her. The thread on the frame drew tight.

Silas stood in the middle of the front room and listened to his mother's house shoes slap away down the wet street until he couldn't hear them anymore.

---

He should've gone back to the step.

He didn't. He leaned against the wall by the cold stove with his mitten hand against his chest and watched Melena not open the envelope for a while.

She turned it over. She ran her thumb along the broken seal. She set it on her knee and looked at it like it might get up and leave. He knew that look. He'd seen it on people at the Ring, holding the last thing somebody had given them before the Quiet Staff carried the body out.

"You don't have to read it tonight," he said.

"I've read it."

"So you don't have to read it again."

"I want to." She didn't look up. "I just haven't decided if I want you watching."

"I'll look at the stove."

He looked at the stove. It was a cold iron stove that hadn't been lit in months, and there wasn't a single thing about it worth looking at, and he looked at it anyway while paper rustled across the room.

She read for a long time. He could hear her breathing. It went ragged twice, and both times the room got warmer, and both times she pushed out a long breath, longer than she took in, and the heat backed off.

She'd learned it. Two tries and she'd already learned it.

He glanced over without meaning to.

She'd unfolded the letter all the way across her knees, three pages in a looping, slanted hand, and her head was bent over the bottom of the last page. From where he stood, the top of the first page was right side up and facing him, and the lamp was right on it.

It didn't start *Melena*.

It started *My dearest Elias.*

Silas looked at that name a second too long.

Melena felt it. She looked up, and she saw where his eyes were, and her whole face shut like a door. She folded the letter in half. She folded it in half again, fast, and the edges started to smoke between her fingers.

"Who's Elias?" Silas asked.

Melena held the smoking letter against her chest, both hands, the way she'd held it in Dante's office before anybody had told her where she was going.

"Ask me something else," she said.
