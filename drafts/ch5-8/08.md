# CHAPTER 8: SILAS

Remy came back at final bell with a sack of borrowed clothes and a door Silas had never seen in his life.

The clothes were for Melena. Work pants, a grey sweater with a hole in one elbow, a brown coat that came to her knees, and a dark wrap for her hair. Remy said they were his cousin's. Melena went into the back room with them and came out ten minutes later looking like any girl from any tier who'd had a long week, except for the way she stood. She still stood like the room had been built for her to walk into.

"Better?" she asked.

Remy looked at her a beat too long. "Yeah. Better."

"You can see the hair at the back," Silas said.

She reached up and tucked it. "Now?"

"Now you look like you work for a living."

"I'll take that as a compliment."

"It's the nicest thing he's ever said to anybody," Remy told her. "Come on. Ora don't like waiting."

---

Blue Circle sat under an old bathhouse three streets east of Gutterlight. Everybody in Shadowmere knew where the front was: a blue lantern over an iron gate, a set of tiled stairs going down, and a doorman with a blue cord around his wrist who looked at your face and decided how much you were worth before you hit the bottom step.

Remy didn't take them to the front.

He took them down an alley behind a noodle counter, through a gap in a fence, and down a short flight of stairs that ended at a plain wooden door in a plain brick wall. No lantern. No route marks cut into the frame. Remy put his palm flat on the wood, said something under his breath that Silas didn't catch, and the door opened.

Silas stopped on the bottom step.

He knew every Mercer passage in Shadowmere. He'd grown up with Dante's map-room glass under his elbows, watching the threads move, learning which doors opened to which tokens and which ones you never touched. He knew the safehouses and the drop points and the service routes behind the tailor rows. He knew every door his family owned and most of the doors his family wanted to own.

This one wasn't on Dante's map. Not anywhere.

"Si," Remy said from inside. "You coming?"

Silas looked at Remy's face in the dark of the doorway. Easy. Normal. Like this was just a door.

He filed it away and went in.

---

The den was bigger than he'd thought it would be.

The bathhouse had been built back when Shadowmere still had hot springs, and somebody had drained the pools and left them. Three of them, one deep and two shallow, all tiled in blue and white that had gone grey with age. People lay in the shallow ones on cushions and blankets and piles of coats. Twenty, maybe thirty. Most of them had their eyes open. All of them were smiling, the same small, empty smile, like they'd just remembered something nice and couldn't remember what.

Low blue lamps hung from the pipes overhead. Steam came up from somewhere and hung in the air. And all along the walls, sewn into the old tile grout in long careful lines, ran thread. Grey thread. Dozens of lines of it, crossing and crossing again.

Silas felt it hum as soon as he walked past it.

Grief thread. Lament work. Somebody had taken the grief of everybody who'd ever come through here and spun it into thread and sewn it into the walls. It hummed low, like a crowd of people crying in the next room. Camille would've burned this place to the ground if she ever saw it.

Melena had stopped walking. She was looking at the people in the pools.

"They look happy," she said.

"They look gone," Silas said.

"Is there a difference?"

Remy touched her elbow, then caught himself and took his hand back. "Sorry. Can I?"

She looked at his hand. "Yes."

He put two fingers on her elbow, light, and steered her toward the deep pool.

Miss Ora sat at the bottom of it.

Somebody had put a velvet chair down there on the drain grate, and Ora sat in it like a queen in a well. She was old. Older than Camille, older than Rae, thin as a broom handle, with her head wrapped in blue silk and a long pipe between her fingers. The smoke that came off it was blue too. A clerk stood at a counter by the steps with rows of tiny glass vials behind him, and two runners with blue cords on their wrists leaned against the tile, watching everybody who came in.

"Remington," Ora said. Her voice was soft, like somebody singing a baby to sleep. "You brought me a Graves."

"I brought you a friend, Miss Ora."

"Mm." Ora's eyes went over Silas, slow, from his boots to his face, and stopped at the bandage on his hand. "Dante's boy. The one who fights." She took a pull on the pipe. "Your mother sewed the threshold on my old house in Lowtorch, baby. Did you know that? Before your time. Best ward I ever had. Burned anyway."

"Didn't know that," Silas said.

"Not many do." She looked at Melena last. She looked at her the longest. "And this one."

Melena stood very still.

"This is who I told you about," Remy said. "One drop. Measured. In tea. We sit with her and watch."

"And then you go."

"And then we go."

Ora smoked for a while. The blue smoke rose up past the lip of the pool and spread out under the pipes.

"One drop," she said. "On the house. For you, Remington." She waved the pipe at the clerk without looking at him. "Make it clean."

---

The clerk measured it in front of them.

He was young, younger than Micah's age, with a thin face and nervous hands and a blue cord wrapped three times around his wrist. He took one vial off the shelf, uncapped it with his thumb, and let a single drop fall into a cup of tea he'd already poured. The drop was clear. It didn't change the color of the tea. It didn't change anything. He handed the cup to Remy, and Remy handed it to Melena, and Melena held it in both hands and looked at Silas.

"You don't have to," Silas said.

"I know." She drank it.

For about a minute, nothing happened. She sat on the steps of the pool with the empty cup in her lap and her shoulders up around her ears, and Silas stood two steps above her with his good hand near his knife and watched her face.

After that, her shoulders came down.

It was slow. Like watching somebody lower a heavy box to the floor. Her shoulders came down, and her hands, which had been holding the cup so tight her knuckles were pale, loosened. Her breath went long without her trying to make it long. She blinked a couple of times and looked around the den like she was seeing it for the first time.

"Oh," she said softly.

"How you feel?" Remy asked. He'd sat down on the step next to her. Not touching. Close.

"Quiet." She said it like a word in a language she was just learning. "It's quiet."

"Good quiet or bad quiet?"

She thought about it. "I don't know. I haven't had quiet since—" She stopped. She put her hand flat on her chest, where the letter was, under the borrowed sweater. "I can think about her. I can think about her and it doesn't—" She held her other hand up in front of her face. Normal. Brown. No black under the nails at all. "It doesn't come."

Remy smiled at her. It was the realest smile Silas had seen on him in a year. "Okay," he said. "Okay. That's good. That's real good."

Silas let out a breath he hadn't known he'd been holding since Dante's office.

It worked. It actually worked. One drop, and the fire was down, and she was sitting on a tiled step in a Hush den talking about her dead mother in a calm voice. They could do this. They could measure it, keep her steady, keep her hidden until Rae said, and nobody had to get burned and nobody had to know.

He looked up at the clerk's counter.

A girl was standing at it.

He hadn't seen her come in. She had her back to him, and she was in a long dark coat, fitted at the waist, a coat nobody in Gutterlight could afford, with a stiff-sided bag over her shoulder. She set a package on the counter. Wardcloth, tied with blue cord, about the size of a brick. The clerk picked up a black strip from a stack beside him, punched it with something that clicked, and handed it back to her without a word.

She left fast. Up the far stairs, toward the front gate, without looking back.

Silas watched her go. Something about the way she stood nagged at him. Weight back. Head turned, counting the turns.

He didn't know her. He turned back to Melena.

The clerk set the package on the shelf behind him, between the vials.

"Remington," Ora said from her chair. "Come here, baby. Let's talk about what you owe me now."

"I thought it was on the house."

"The drop is on the house. The door ain't."

Remy sighed and got up. He looked at Melena. "You good?"

"I'm good," she said, and she sounded it.

He went down into the deep pool. Silas watched him crouch next to Ora's velvet chair and start talking low, his hands moving, and Ora smiling her soft lullaby smile and smoking and shaking her head.

Silas turned back to the room.

The two runners against the far wall had stopped leaning. One of them was looking at Melena's head. The wrap had slipped again, just at the back, and there was red showing. Bright red under blue lamplight, the brightest thing in the room.

Silas moved. He went up two steps to put himself between the runner's line of sight and her, the same half step he'd done at Pell's, and stared at the runner until the runner found something else to stare at.

It took maybe fifteen seconds.

When he turned back around, Melena wasn't on the step anymore.

She was at the clerk's counter.

Silas saw it all in one piece, the way you saw a punch you couldn't stop. The clerk had a vial in his hand. A full one, capped, the clear liquid all the way to the top. Melena was holding something out to him across the counter, and the something was dark and caught the blue light, and it took Silas a second to understand it was one of the stones off her collar. He could see the loose thread where she'd torn it free.

The clerk was looking at Melena's face, not at the stone. He had a strange, slack look, like he was listening to something far away.

He took the stone. He gave her the vial.

"*Hey*—"

She uncapped it with her thumb, the way she'd watched him do it, and drank the whole thing before Silas got down the first step.

"*Melena*!"

Remy's head came up in the bottom of the pool. He saw the empty vial in her hand, and his face went grey.

"No," he said. "No, no, no—"

Melena turned around. She was smiling.

It was the den smile. The empty, gentle one, the one on every face in the shallow pools. She looked at Silas coming down the steps toward her, and she looked at Remy climbing out of the deep pool, and she held up the empty vial like she'd won something.

"It's so quiet," she said. "Silas. It's so *quiet*."

Her hands were normal. Her breath was slow. There was no black under her nails, nothing climbing, nothing going ragged. None of the signs. Nothing.

The air went white.

There was no warning. That was the thing Silas would remember later, lying awake with his hand throbbing. In Dante's office there'd been a warning. On Lamp Street there'd been a warning. This morning, with the letter, there'd been a warning. The Hush had taken every one of those away and left the fire sitting there in her with no door to knock on first.

It came out of her all at once.

It didn't come from her hands this time. It came from all of her. Heat rolled off her body like a furnace door kicked open, and the cup in the clerk's hand shattered, and every vial on the shelf behind him burst in a line, *pop pop pop pop*, like firecrackers. The clerk screamed. The blue cord on his wrist caught and he slapped at it.

The grief thread in the walls lit up.

It went like a fuse. Silas watched it happen and couldn't stop it. The first line caught by the counter and the fire ran along the grout, orange, fast, and hit the next line where they crossed, and that one caught, and that one, and in four seconds every line of thread in every wall of the den was burning, and the hum of all that grief turned into a scream.

The package on the shelf behind the clerk blew.

It went with a crack that Silas felt in his chest, and something inside it sprayed out in a bright silver mist that caught the fire and burned blue-white. Something hot and small hit Silas in the cheek. He touched it. A coin, or half of one, melted, stuck to his skin. He tore it off and kept moving.

The people in the shallow pools didn't run.

That was the worst part. The fire was running along the walls above their heads and the steam pipes were starting to scream, and they were lying there on their cushions with their gentle empty smiles, watching it like it was pretty.

"*Get up*!" Silas yelled at the nearest one, a man about Dante's age. "Get up, get out, *go*!"

The man smiled at him.

Silas grabbed him by the coat with his good hand and hauled him off the cushions and shoved him toward the stairs. The man stumbled, caught himself, kept walking, still smiling. Silas grabbed the next one. And the next. The runners were running, the clerk was running, Ora was standing up out of her velvet chair at the bottom of the deep pool with her pipe in her hand and her soft voice gone hard and loud, shouting at the clerk, and the clerk was shouting back.

"She got in my head! Ora, I swear, she got in my *head*, I didn't—"

Remy had reached Melena.

She was standing exactly where she'd been, by the counter, in the middle of it. Heat was coming off her in waves. The borrowed sweater had started to smoke at the hem. Her eyes were open and she was looking at the fire on the walls with that empty smile, and Remy stood in front of her, as close as the heat would let him, which wasn't close.

"Melena." His voice was shaking. "Melena, look at me. What color's my coat?"

She looked at him. Her smile didn't change.

"*What color is my coat*?"

"Green," she said, dreamy. "Ugly green."

"Good. Good. Can I pick you up? I need to pick you up and carry you out of here. Can I?"

She thought about it. Silas, dragging a woman out of the shallow pool by her arm, watched her think about it while the den burned down around her.

"Yes," she said.

Remy picked her up.

He did it fast and he did it right, one arm behind her back and one under her knees, and the heat coming off her must have been like hugging a stove, because Silas watched Remy's face twist and his jaw lock. He didn't put her down. He turned for the stairs.

"Not the front!" Silas shouted. "They'll see her!"

"The door!" Remy yelled back. "Our door!"

Silas shoved the woman up the stairs after the others and turned around one more time.

The clerk was gone. Ora was gone. The counter was on fire. And sitting on the counter, right in the middle of the flames, was a dark stone with a loose thread still hanging off it. Melena's mourning stone. The one she'd bought the vial with.

He didn't think about it. He grabbed it.

It burned his good hand, and he didn't care. He shoved it in his coat and ran.

---

The plain door in the plain brick wall was standing open when they got to it. Remy went through first with Melena in his arms, and Silas came through after, and behind them, Blue Circle came down.

He heard it more than saw it. A deep groan from the bathhouse above, and then the crack of the tile roof giving way, and then a rush of hot air up the stairwell behind them that knocked him into the wall. Smoke came boiling up out of the gap in the fence. Somewhere on the street side, people were screaming. A bell started ringing somewhere, then another.

Silas pulled the door shut. He didn't know if it would lock behind them. He didn't know if it locked at all.

They came out of the alley behind the noodle counter into the rain.

It was chaos. People pouring out of the front gate of the bathhouse onto the street, some of them burned, some of them still smiling. Runners with blue cords scattering in every direction. A man in a work coat standing in the middle of the road with his hands on his head, saying "*My package, my package*," over and over. Steam rising off everything. Blue Circle's lantern lay shattered in the gutter, still burning.

And somewhere out there, one of those runners was going to remember a red-haired girl at the counter.

"Where?" Remy said. He was breathing hard. Melena was limp in his arms now, her eyes half closed, still smiling a little. The heat had dropped enough that Remy's face wasn't twisted anymore, but steam was rising off both of them in the rain. "The dark house?"

Silas thought about the dark house. Camille's thread, already sewn twice. *It won't hold a week.* Every runner in Blue Circle had seen Remy and Silas walk in with her. Half of them would know Silas's face from the Ring by morning. Some of them had probably watched him sit on that step all last night.

And whatever was in Melena right now, full of Hush and burning without a warning, Camille's thread wasn't going to hold it till morning.

There was one threshold in Shadowmere he knew would hold. Camille had built it with her own hands the year he moved in. She'd sewn it with his blood in the thread, so it would know him. Every door in this city answered to somebody. His answered to him.

"Home," Silas said.

Remy looked at him. "*Your* home?"

"You got a better one?"

Remy didn't.

---

They took the last south tram.

Silas paid for the whole back car. It cost him most of what he had on him, and the tram man didn't ask questions because the tram man had seen the smoke rising over the Gutterlight rooftops and wanted no part of whatever these three were. They sat in the back. Remy held Melena across his lap with Silas's coat over her to hide the steam. Nobody else got on. At every stop, the doors opened onto empty platforms and closed again.

Silas sat across from them and watched the rain go by.

His burned palm was throbbing. His other hand had a fresh burn across the fingers from the stone. His cheek stung where the melted coin had hit him. He reached into his coat and took out the mourning stone and turned it over.

It was smooth and dark and heavy for its size, and when he tilted it toward the lamp, something moved inside it, deep down, like smoke trapped in glass. He'd never seen a stone like it. Neither had anybody in Gutterlight, he'd bet. She'd ripped it off her own dress and paid it to a boy with a blue cord for a vial of Hush, and the boy had taken it like he didn't know what it was.

*She got in my head.*

He put the stone away.

Across from him, Melena stirred. Her eyes opened a little. She looked up at Remy holding her, and she didn't pull away.

"You smell like smoke," she said.

"So do you," Remy said.

"I'm sorry."

"I know."

"I wanted it quiet." Her eyes closed again. "Just for a little while. I wanted it to be quiet."

Remy didn't say anything. He looked out the window at the rain with his jaw tight, and Silas watched his best friend hold a burning girl on a tram and try not to cry.

---

Number 3 Shadowmere Lane was dark when they got there. Silas went up the steps first and put his hand on the frame of the outer door.

The threshold knew him. It always did. He felt it the way he felt Camille's thread, a low hum under his palm, and when he pressed the burned hand flat to the wood and leaned his weight into it, the hum went deeper. Camille's work. Graves knots in every corner of the frame and his own blood stitched into the line.

"She's with me," he said to the door.

He didn't know if it understood words. He said it anyway.

The door opened.

Remy carried Melena across the threshold, and Silas held his breath and watched. If the ward didn't take her, it'd throw her back out into the street like the Mercer lock had thrown off his token last night. It'd fight her.

It didn't fight her. The hum under his hand went tight, then settled. Like it had decided.

The second floor had been empty since spring. Silas had a key, because Camille had a key to everything in this building and had given him copies of all of them in a jar labeled *DON'T LOSE THESE*. The apartment was bare. A mattress on the floor. A table. A radiator under the window that knocked every time the tram went by underneath the street.

It had a window. A real one, looking out over the lane. Silas noticed it as soon as he walked in and felt something in his chest unclench.

Remy laid Melena on the mattress. She curled onto her side and was asleep before he'd taken his arms back. The heat was coming off her slower now, steady, like a banked stove.

Remy stood over her and didn't move.

"I'm staying," he said.

It wasn't a question. Silas didn't treat it like one.

"I'll be upstairs," Silas said. "Bang on the radiator if something happens. I'll hear it."

"Where you going?"

Silas was already at the door. He turned around.

"That clerk sold her a full vial for a rock," he said. "And somebody's package blew up on his shelf. Somebody in that den knows who she is, or they're gonna find out by morning. I want to know who before they do."

"Si. Your hands."

"I got two more burns than I had this morning. I'll live."

"Dante's gonna find out."

"Dante already knows." Silas held up his phone. It had been buzzing in his pocket since the tram.

Remy looked at the screen. His face did something complicated.

The message was from Dante. Four words, no punctuation, sent eleven minutes after Blue Circle came down.

*Where is she Silas*
