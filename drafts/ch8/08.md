# CHAPTER 8: SILAS

Rae was waiting at the corner by the noodle counter when final bell rang, and she didn't say a word about Camille's shoes.

His mother had put on her real ones. Black, low heel, the pair she wore to stand at the front of a funeral. She had her gold clasp in her locs and a spool of thread in her coat pocket, and she walked down the lane beside Silas like she was going to collect on a debt. Melena walked on his left in the clothes Remy had brought her that afternoon: work pants, a grey sweater, a brown coat to her knees, a dark wrap over her hair. She'd kept the mint. Silas could see the silver wrapper pinched between two fingers of her right hand.

Rae turned without a greeting and led them into the alley behind the noodle counter, through a gap in a fence, and down a short flight of stairs that ended at a plain wooden door in a plain brick wall. No lantern. No route marks in the frame.

Silas stopped on the bottom step.

He'd grown up with his elbows on Dante's map-room glass. He knew every Mercer passage in Shadowmere, every safehouse, every drop, every service route behind the tailor rows. He knew the doors his family owned and most of the ones they wanted.

This one wasn't on the glass.

Rae didn't touch it. She looked at Remy.

Remy stepped past his mother, put his palm flat on the wood, and said something under his breath. Silas didn't catch the words. The door caught them fine. It opened onto a stairwell full of blue light and a sound like a room full of people crying on the other side of a wall.

Silas looked at Remy. Remy didn't look back.

He filed it with the rest and went down.

---

The den was built in an old bathhouse from back when Shadowmere still had hot springs. Somebody had drained the pools and left them, three of them, tiled blue and white gone grey with age, and filled the shallow two with cushions and blankets and bodies. Twenty people, maybe thirty. Most of them had their eyes open. All of them were smiling the same small smile, like they'd remembered something nice and couldn't remember what.

Low blue lamps hung from the pipes. Steam came up through the floor grates. And all along the walls, sewn into the grout between the tiles, ran thread. Grey thread, line over line over line, crossing and crossing again until the walls looked woven.

It hummed. Silas felt it in his back teeth the second he stepped off the stairs.

Beside him, Camille stopped walking.

"Lord," she said quietly.

He looked at her. She was staring at the nearest wall the way she'd stared at Jude's arm the day he broke it jumping off the tram platform, like she was deciding how much it was going to cost to fix and who was going to pay.

"Lament work," she said. "Every line of it. Somebody's sorrow, pulled and spun and sewn." She didn't touch the wall. "Three hundred lines, she said. That's three hundred people who walked out of here lighter and don't remember why."

"They paid for it," Rae said behind her.

"I know they paid for it." Camille turned her head. "I'm wondering who got the change."

Miss Ora sat at the bottom of the deep pool.

Somebody had set a velvet chair on the drain grate, and Ora sat in it like a queen in a well. She was old, older than Rae, thin as a broom handle, with her head wrapped in blue silk and a long pipe between two fingers. Her smoke came off blue too. A clerk stood behind a counter at the top of the pool steps with rows of little glass vials on the shelf behind him, and two runners with blue cords knotted around their wrists leaned on the tile by the far stairs, watching every face that came in.

Their eyes went to Silas first and the wrap on Melena's head second. Silas moved half a step to the left and stayed there.

"Renae." Ora's voice was soft, like somebody humming a baby down. "You brought me the whole family."

"I brought you a girl," Rae said. "The rest of them came on their own."

"Mm." Ora took a pull on the pipe. Her eyes went over Camille, over the shoes, and came back with something like amusement in them. "Mrs. Graves. In my house."

"Don't get used to it," Camille said.

Ora laughed, low. She looked at Silas last and longest before she let her eyes drift down to Melena. "So this is the one burning holes in Dante's houses."

Melena's fingers tightened on the mint.

"One drop," Rae said. "In the walls. You measure it."

"I always measure it." Ora lifted the pipe toward the clerk without looking at him. "Make it clean."

---

The clerk did it in front of them. He was young, barely older than Teo, with nervous hands and his blue cord wrapped three times around his wrist. He uncapped a vial with his thumb and let one drop fall into a cup of tea, and the tea didn't change color, and nothing changed at all. He handed it to Remy. Remy handed it to Melena.

She held the cup in both hands and looked at Silas over the rim.

"You don't have to," he said.

"I know." She drank it.

For a minute, nothing happened. She sat on the pool steps with her shoulders up around her ears, and the walls hummed, and Silas stood two steps above her with his good hand near his knife and watched her face.

Her shoulders came down.

It was slow, like watching somebody set down a box they'd been carrying for miles. Her hands loosened on the cup. Her breath went long without anybody telling her to breathe. She blinked twice and looked around the den like she'd just walked in.

The walls changed pitch. Silas felt it before he heard it, the hum dropping lower in his teeth, steadier, like something had sat down to eat.

"Oh," Melena said.

Remy had sat down on the step beside her. Not touching. Close. "How you feel?"

"Quiet." She said it like a word she was learning. She put one hand flat on her chest, over the letter under her sweater, and held the other up in front of her face. Brown skin, clean nails. Nothing climbing. "I can think about her. I can think about her and it doesn't come."

Remy smiled at her, and it was the realest thing Silas had seen on his face in a year.

Silas let out a breath he'd been holding since Dante's office.

Behind him, Camille didn't say anything. She was still watching the walls.

---

A girl came down the far stairs while Ora was talking.

Silas wouldn't have noticed her except that she didn't look at anything. Everybody who came into a den looked at the pools first. She looked at the counter and nothing else. Long dark coat, fitted at the waist, a cut nobody in Gutterlight could afford. Stiff-sided bag on her shoulder. She set a wardcloth package on the counter, tied with blue cord, about the size of a brick. The clerk picked up a black strip from a stack beside him, punched it with something that clicked, and handed it back to her.

She was up the stairs and gone in under a minute. Weight back. Head turned just enough to count the turns.

Silas watched the stairs where she'd been. The clerk put the package on the shelf between the vials.

"Remington." Rae said it from the edge of the deep pool, where she'd gone down two steps to stand beside Ora's chair. "Come here."

Remy looked at Melena.

"I'm good," Melena said, and she sounded it.

Remy went down into the pool. Rae put her hand on his arm, and Ora leaned forward in her chair, and the three of them started talking low enough that the walls swallowed it.

Camille had walked to the nearest wall. She was standing close to it with her hands in her coat pockets, reading the thread the way she read the crossword, lips moving.

The runner by the far stairs had stopped leaning. He was looking at Melena's head. The wrap had slipped at the back, and there was red showing under the blue lamps, the brightest thing in the room.

Silas went up two steps and stood where the runner would have to look through him to see her, and he looked at the runner until the runner found something else to look at.

It took fifteen seconds.

When he turned around, Melena wasn't on the step.

She was at the counter.

The clerk had a vial in his hand, full, capped, clear to the top. Melena was holding something out to him across the wood, dark and catching the lamplight, and it took Silas a second to see it was one of the stones off her mourning collar. She'd worn the dress under the borrowed clothes. He could see the loose thread hanging where she'd torn the stone free.

The clerk wasn't looking at the stone. He was looking at her face with his mouth a little open, like he was listening to a song from far away.

He took the stone and gave her the vial.

Silas came down the steps. "*Melena.*"

She thumbed the cap off the way she'd watched the clerk do it and drank the whole thing before he got to the bottom.

---

Remy saw it from the pool. His face went grey.

Melena turned around with the empty vial in her hand, and she was smiling. The den smile. The one on every face in the shallow pools.

"It's so quiet, Silas." She held up the vial like she'd won it. "It's so *quiet*."

Her hands were clean. Her breath was slow. No black, no smoke, nothing climbing. Every warning she'd had in Dante's office, on Lamp Street, at Camille's kitchen table, was gone.

The air went white.

It came out of all of her at once. No knock on the door first. Heat rolled off her like a furnace kicked open, and the cup on the counter shattered, and the vials on the shelf behind the clerk burst in a line, one after another, like knuckles cracking. The clerk screamed and slapped at the blue cord on his wrist as it caught.

The walls lit.

The first line of grey thread caught by the counter and ran along the grout, orange and fast, and hit the next line where they crossed, and that one caught, and that one. Three hundred lines of somebody's sorrow went up like a fuse. The hum in the walls turned into a scream Silas felt in his skull.

The package on the shelf blew.

It went with a crack he felt in his chest, and something inside it sprayed out silver and caught the fire and burned blue-white. Something hot and small hit him in the cheek. He pulled it off. Half a coin, melted, stuck to his skin. He threw it down and kept moving.

The people in the shallow pools didn't run.

Fire was running along the walls over their heads and the steam pipes were starting to scream, and they lay on their cushions smiling at it like it was pretty.

"*Up.*" Silas hauled the closest one off his blanket by the collar, a man about Dante's age, and shoved him at the stairs. The man stumbled, caught himself, kept walking, still smiling. Silas grabbed the next one.

Camille was already in the shallow pool on the other side. She'd kicked off her funeral shoes. She had a grown woman under each arm and she was walking them up the steps like she was taking them to church, and every time one of them tried to sit back down, Camille said "*No ma'am*" in a voice that made the woman stand up straighter.

At the bottom of the deep pool, Ora was on her feet with her pipe still in her hand, and the soft voice was gone. She pointed the pipe at the counter like a knife.

"*Who sold her that?*"

"She got in my head!" The clerk was backing up the far stairs with his wrist smoking. "Ora, I swear on my mama, she got in my *head*—"

Remy had reached Melena.

She was standing where she'd been, by the counter, in the middle of it. The borrowed sweater was smoking at the hem. Her eyes were open and she was watching the walls burn with that empty smile on her face, and Remy stood in front of her as close as the heat would let him, which wasn't close.

"Melena. Look at me." His voice was shaking. "What color's my coat?"

She looked at him. Her smile didn't change. "Ugly green."

"Can I pick you up? I need to carry you out of here. Can I?"

She thought about it. Silas, dragging a boy out of the shallow pool by the back of his shirt, watched her stand in a burning room and think about it.

"Yes," she said.

Remy picked her up.

He did it fast and he did it right, and the heat coming off her had to be like holding a stove, because Silas watched his jaw lock and his eyes water and he didn't put her down.

"Not the front," Silas said.

Remy was already turning with her. "*Our* door."

Silas shoved the boy up the stairs after the others. He turned around once.

The counter was burning. The clerk was gone. Sitting on the wood in the middle of the flames was a dark stone with a loose thread hanging off it.

He grabbed it. It burned his good hand. He put it in his coat.

He looked for Rae.

The deep pool was empty. Ora was climbing out of it on the far side with two runners pulling her by the arms. The velvet chair was on fire. There was no black dress anywhere in the room.

---

The plain door was standing open when they reached it. Remy went through with Melena, Camille went through barefoot with her shoes in her hand and her coat over her mouth, and Silas went last.

Behind them, Blue Circle came down. He heard the tile roof go, a deep groan and a crack, and a rush of hot air came up the stairwell and put him into the wall. Smoke boiled out through the gap in the fence. Bells started ringing somewhere on the street side. People screaming.

He pulled the door shut. It didn't lock behind him. It didn't need to. When he looked back from the top of the stairs, there wasn't a door in the brick at all.

They came out behind the noodle counter into the rain.

The street in front of the bathhouse was chaos. People spilling out of the front gate, some of them burned, some of them still smiling. Runners with blue cords scattering in every direction. Blue Circle's lantern shattered in the gutter, still burning. And one runner standing in the middle of the road with his cord burned black, yelling at nobody.

"*The package. The package was in there. Somebody gonna pay for that package.*"

He looked up the street at the four of them coming out of the alley. His eyes went to the steam coming off Remy's arms. He turned and ran toward the rail line.

Silas marked which way he went.

"Where?" Remy said. He was breathing hard. Melena was limp against his chest, still smiling a little, steam coming off both of them in the rain. "The dark house?"

"Dark house won't hold her now." Camille was putting her shoes back on one at a time, standing on one foot in the street like it was her kitchen. "Every runner in that den saw her face. And whatever's in her, Graves thread won't hold it till morning."

"So where?" Remy said.

Camille straightened up and looked at Silas.

"There's one door in Shadowmere I sewed with your blood in the line," she said. "It don't answer to Dante, and it don't answer to Renae. It answers to you."

---

Silas paid for the whole back car of the last south tram. The tram man took one look at the smoke rising over Gutterlight and the burned girl in Remy's arms and didn't ask a single question. Nobody else got on. At every stop the doors opened onto empty platforms and closed again.

Camille sat beside Silas with her hands folded in her lap. She hadn't said a word since the street.

He took the stone out of his coat. Dark, smooth, heavy for its size, and when he tipped it toward the lamp, something moved deep inside it like smoke caught in glass. The skin across his good hand had blistered where he'd grabbed it.

Camille looked at the stone. She looked at his hand.

"Renae was gone before the roof came down," she said.

Silas kept his eyes on the window. "I saw."

"She brought that girl into those walls, and she was gone before they burned."

Silas put the stone away. He didn't say anything. He didn't have to.

Across the car, Melena opened her eyes. She looked up at Remy holding her, and she didn't pull away.

"You smell like smoke," she said.

"So do you." Remy's voice came out rough.

"I wanted it quiet." Her eyes closed again. "Just for a little while."

---

Number 3 Shadowmere Lane was dark. Silas went up the steps first and put his burned hand flat on the outer door.

The threshold knew him. It always had. Camille's knots in every corner of the frame, his blood in the line, a low hum under his palm that went deeper when he leaned into it.

"She's with me," he said.

The door opened. Remy carried Melena across, and Silas held still and waited for the ward to fight her the way Camille's thread had in the dark house.

It didn't. The hum went tight under his hand, and held, and settled.

The second floor had been empty since spring. Silas had the key because Camille had keys to everything in the building. It was bare: a mattress on the floor, a table, a radiator that knocked every time a tram went by under the street. Remy laid Melena down on the mattress, and she curled onto her side and was asleep before he got his arms back.

There was a window over the radiator. A real one, looking out over the lane.

Silas looked at it for a second longer than he needed to.

Camille sat down on the floor with her back against the wall and took her spool of thread out of her pocket. "I'll sit," she said. "Remington, go home and put something on that arm."

"I'm staying," Remy said. He didn't look up from the mattress.

Camille looked at him for a long time. "All right, baby."

Silas went to the door.

"Si." Remy finally looked up. "Where you going?"

"Runner from the den went toward the rail line yelling about a package." Silas checked the knife at his hip. "Somebody sold her that vial, and somebody's package blew. I want to know whose before morning."

Remy's eyes went to the blisters. "Your hands."

"I got two." Silas opened the door. "Lock it behind me."

His phone buzzed on the stairs. He took it out under the hall lamp.

*Dante: Where is she.*

Silas read it once. He put the phone back in his coat without answering and went out into the rain.
