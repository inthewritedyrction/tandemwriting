# 3SL — PACING AND SILAS'S VOICE

Read this together with the handoff prompt. It covers the two places drafts of this book have gone wrong: rushing the synopsis, and writing Silas without his voice.

## PART ONE: PACING

### The synopsis is an estimate, not a checklist

The chapter synopses tell you roughly where the story is going. They don't tell you how fast to get there. A synopsis line like "Asha comes home late, the thread goes slack, Isola finds the route strip, Micah laughs about Silas" isn't four beats to cram into one chapter. It might be two chapters. Some beats belong somewhere else entirely, and some happen differently once the characters get into the room.

**This is fantasy. Romantasy.** The story will get told. What I need is for the pacing to make sense and for the scenes to breathe.

### What rushing looked like in earlier drafts (don't do this)

- **Ch5:** the draft ended on Melena's letter revealing "My dearest Elias" because the synopsis mentioned it. I cut it and gave the back half of the chapter to Camille interrogating Melena at the kitchen table. That one scene made a better chapter than three plot beats.
- **Ch6:** the draft packed in the name wall, the route strip, Remy and Rae coming home. I told the drafter to slow down. The final chapter is one scene: Isola in Rae's kitchen asking her godbrother for help for the first time in her life. Nothing else.
- **Ch7:** a draft sent everyone to Ora's and the Hush den in the same chapter as the confrontation. The final version spends the night on Camille and Rae, the morning on Silas working his city, and ends with Camille deciding they're going. Ora's waited for Ch8.
- An early batch drafted four chapters at once and hit every synopsis beat on schedule. It read like a summary with dialogue, and every chapter got rewritten.

### How to pace a chapter

1. **Find the chapter's one job.** Every chapter has one emotional or relational turn that matters most: Isola asks for help; Camille forces Rae's hand; Silas finds out the salt girl is Rae's. Build the chapter around that turn and give it room.
2. **Choose the fewest scenes that can do that job.** One long scene is often better than three short ones. My chapters are usually one continuous scene, or two connected ones, moving through time inside the paragraphs, not across scene breaks.
3. **Let conversations run.** Real people circle, deflect, joke, eat, change the subject, and come back. A scene where two characters talk for 3,000 words is fine if something shifts between them.
4. **Spend time on what's lived in:** food, kitchens, family shorthand, how people move through a room, who sits where, what somebody does with their hands while they lie. That's where the book's Blackness and warmth live.
5. **Move plot beats that don't fit.** If a synopsis beat would crowd the chapter, push it to the next one or the one after. Say so in chat: "I'm holding the route strip for later because…"
6. **Don't resolve too fast.** Mysteries stay open. Rae doesn't explain. Remy keeps his promise. Silas doesn't get Isola's name until she gives it to him.
7. **End on a turn, not a recap.** The last line is an action, a choice, a threat, or somebody else's line that changes what happens next.

### Before you draft each chapter, tell me in chat (short)

- The chapter's one job.
- The scene or scenes you plan, and where it starts and ends in time.
- Which synopsis beats you're **holding for later**, and why.
- Anything you're planning to invent.

## PART TWO: SILAS'S VOICE

### The core idea

Silas is Xaden-coded, through his own life: an older Black man from Shadowmere who runs it. Study the Xaden bonus chapters (9 and 16) before every Silas chapter.

**His head is loud, but it's never a lecture.** It's contempt, cussing, self-argument, wanting and denying. It is **not** step-by-step reasoning. My biggest correction on Silas chapters has been cutting the drafter's logic chains out of his head.

### What NOT to write, with real examples

**Don't narrate his reasoning.**
- ❌ *Melena had come down Rae's routes. Rae's routes had Quietcasters walking their edges. And now somebody had eyes on the people who carried Rae's brass.*
- ✅ *Rae's goddaughter. / The Ashvein girl was Rae's. / Fuck.*
- If he figures something out, he says it out loud, short, as a claim: *"I'm starting to think it's about to become my family's business too."*

**Don't narrate other people's faces for him.**
- ❌ *It took Remy a second. Silas watched him run the question back, watched him notice it wasn't the question he'd been ready for…*
- ✅ Show the fact and move on: *Remy turned his head and looked at him.*

**Don't make his orders into briefings.**
- ❌ A full paragraph telling a runner to watch for red hair, long dark coats, and anybody in grey.
- ✅ *"Anybody asks about me and a girl, you remember their face."*

**Don't make his humor cute.**
- ❌ *"Put it on Camille." / "She will."*
- ✅ *"You got a problem with the difference, take it up with Dante." / "You trying to get me killed?" / "You do good at that all by yourself."*

**Don't make him wistful about Isola.**
- ❌ *"What'd she say?"*, or Remy teasing him while he blushes. That's an early-2000s rom-com.
- ✅ He's already a step ahead (he got it from Pell before Remy could tell him). He asks one protective question: *"She all right?"* He threatens Remy when Remy pushes: *"Ask me that one more time and I'm putting you in the canal."*

### What TO write

- **Contempt in a few words:** *Ora had the softest voice in Shadowmere and the hardest ledger.*
- **Threats as decisions he's holding back:** *He'd let him keep the eyes. Tonight.*
- **Cussing reactions:** *Fuck.* / *Oh, hell no.* / *Since when, motherfucker?*
- **Self-argument about Isola:** he denies thinking about her, then lists exactly what he's not thinking about. He calls her "Salt." Control is who he is, and she keeps knocking it loose.
- **His body reacts first:** heat up his neck, something twisting in his gut, his jaw locking. He names the feeling last, if ever.
- **Questions in his head in real time:** *So what the fuck is Rae playing at?*
- **His city moves around him.** People lift two fingers, refuse his money, take his coin, get out of his way. Show it; don't explain it.
- **Loyalty as names:** Remy, Jude, Rhea, and the fifteen-year-old runner nobody found.
- **Rhythm:** a long sentence of action or thought, then a short hit. The hit is always a reaction, never a summary.
- **Dialogue:** short, flat, an order or a threat. He never explains himself to people he's paying.

### Silas self-check before you send a chapter

- Did I write any paragraph where Silas walks through logic? Cut it, or turn it into one spoken line.
- Did I describe what someone else's face "meant"? Cut the meaning, keep the fact.
- Is any order longer than one sentence? Shorten it.
- Is any joke cute instead of dangerous? Sharpen it.
- When Isola comes up, is he wistful? Make him deny it, threaten somebody, or get caught.
- Does anyone repeat information the reader already has? Cut it.
- Is the chapter one continuous run with no scene breaks? It should be.

Use my Ch1, Ch3 and Ch7 as the Silas standard. Use the Xaden bonus chapters as the voice standard.
