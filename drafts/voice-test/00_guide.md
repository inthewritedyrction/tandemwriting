# 3SL Voice Guide (working draft)

The test chapters are written to these rules. Mark anything that's wrong.

**1. The narration is the character talking.** Close third and past tense stay. But Silas and Isola get to react in the middle of things: a dry aside, a cuss, a judgment, a joke at their own expense. These are grown people, so the humor is drier than Violet's and slower to panic. *Fourth Wing:* "I'm so fucked." *Your Ch. 3:* "Kids like that were always getting into something in Shadowmere." *Test version:* "Fourteen. Two more than she'd budgeted, which meant two more than they had."

**2. Short lines are for voice, not drama.** A one-line paragraph is fine when it's a reaction, a joke or a hard turn. It isn't fine as a solemn fragment for effect ("Nobody had." "It held."). If the line wouldn't come out of the character's mouth, it gets folded back into the paragraph.

**3. Attraction lands in the body first.** They notice and react, maybe roast themselves, then get back to work. Nobody explains why they noticed. *Kingmaker:* "it's bad timing to notice this guy is gorgeous. And that he smells really good." *Test version:* Isola clocks that he's fine, is annoyed about it, and moves on because her cousin is getting away.

**4. Name feelings plainly.** "Her stomach dropped" is allowed. Being honest and fast beats being clever.

**5. Tell when the pressure earns it.** World facts come with attitude or a stake, or because somebody in the scene needs to hear them. The narration doesn't stop to give the reader a tour.

**6. People say the hard thing.** Family members fight out loud. Love and cruelty are both blunt.

**7. Scenes move.** Each chapter opens on stakes and ends on a turn. Things happen on the page instead of being remembered.

**8. No refrains.** No repeating a phrase across paragraphs as a style move ("He thought about… He thought about…").

**9. Blackness lives in the social fabric.** It shows in who checks whom, family shorthand, aunties, money, manners and the jokes. Dialogue carries the dialect, and narration stays mostly standard with occasional slides.
