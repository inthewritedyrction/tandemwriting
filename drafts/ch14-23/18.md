# CHAPTER 18: SILAS

Rhea cleared his table with one arm.

The crossword went. The mail went. Camille's ward ledgers went, and his unpaid tram notices, and the broken pencil he'd left lying beside *WRIT* three nights ago, all of it swept off onto the floor in one long motion without her even looking at what she was moving. She spread a clean white sheet over the wood. She'd brought it with her, folded under her arm. Funeral linen. Silas knew it by the weave.

"There," she said. "Down."

He laid Isola on his kitchen table.

He did it slowly. He didn't want to let go. He knew how that sounded, even in his own head, and he didn't care. His arms had been around her since the Halflight stairs. His good one ached and his bad one had gone past aching into something he didn't have a word for, and his right hand under her thigh had been dead for an hour, and he still didn't want to let go. When he finally slid his arms out from under her, his whole body felt wrong, like a coat with the lining torn out.

She didn't wake. Her head turned a little on the linen, toward him. Her hand was still closed around the strip on her chest.

Rhea pulled Isola's skirt up past the knee without asking anybody's permission and looked at the leg.

Silas made himself look too.

It was worse in the light. In the station, in the grey of the funeral road, it had just been black, lines, a shape he could read. Here, under the lamp over his own table, he could see what it had done to her. The skin around the black had gone grey and dull and strange, like something cooked. The three branches above the ankle had sunk in, the way a road sinks after a flood, and the one behind the knee had left a trench in her flesh that he could've laid a finger in. The one that had gone toward the bone, he couldn't see at all. He could only see where it went in, a single dark point on the inside of her shin, like the tip of a nail.

And the black was moving again.

He saw it before Rhea said anything. He saw it the way he'd seen Jerrick's knee. A thin line, the thinnest of them, the one that had been closest to her hip in Halflight, sitting dead and still against her inner thigh. It had moved. Not much. A hair's width. A hair's width up.

"It's climbing," Silas said.

"I see it."

"I took the claim off. I *felt* it come off."

"I know you did." Rhea didn't look up. She had both hands flat on the linen on either side of Isola's leg, not touching her, and she was looking at the black the way Camille looked at a stitch somebody else had done wrong. "You took the claim. You didn't take what it left. Nothing's pulling it now, but nothing's stopping it either. It's like a fire after you kill the man who lit it." She finally looked up at him. "It doesn't need him anymore."

"Then stop it."

"What do you think I'm doing?"

She put her hands on Isola's leg.

Not on the black. Above it. On the warm skin of the thigh, just past where the highest line ended, her fingers spread. Her Underchant marks were shining wet across the backs of both her hands. She took a breath.

Then she started to speak.

Silas had heard Underchant his whole life. Camille used it on doors and on thread, low and steady, the way you'd talk to a dog you were teaching to stay. Rhea used it differently. She always had. When she was small, she'd done it like singing, and Camille had smacked her hand for it and told her it wasn't a song. Rhea had done it like singing anyway. She did it like singing now. Low and even and very fast, words he knew and words he didn't, words that weren't words, words that were agreements, terms, promises made to things that couldn't be lied to. *Here and no further. This is the line. This is the door. This is the place where the road stops.* Funeral terms. The words you said over a body to tell whatever was in it where it was allowed to go.

Under her hands, the black went still.

Silas watched it stop. He watched it hold. He let himself breathe.

Then he felt it come off.

It wasn't anything he could see. It was something he felt, the way he'd felt the dead hook into his marks at the Halflight gate. Rhea's words, all those terms, all those promises, going down into Isola's leg like water going into dry ground, and finding nothing there to hold on to. They slid off her. Off the black. Off the dead flesh around it. They slid off like water off oiled cloth.

And they went looking for somewhere else to go.

They found him.

He felt them come up through the floor. Up the legs of the table, across the boards, into the soles of his boots, up through the bones of his legs. They found the marks on his skin. Every name Camille had ever bound into him. And they went *in*. Rhea's funeral terms, every one of them, sliding into him through the doors he'd opened at Halflight, and settling. Here and no further. This is the line. This is the door. Settling into his arm, his wrist, his chest, the place under his breastbone where his brother used to be.

His knees went.

He caught himself on the edge of the table. His bad shoulder screamed. His dead right hand slid on the linen and didn't catch anything. Rhea stopped speaking.

"Silas."

"Keep going."

"Silas, it's going into *you*—"

"I know where it's going. Keep going."

She looked at him across the table, across Isola's leg, and he saw her face do something he hadn't seen it do since they were children. It went afraid.

Above Isola's knee, the black moved another hair's width up.

Rhea put her hands back down, and started again.

---

Every lamp in the building went out.

Not one at a time. Not flickering. All at once, the way the map table had gone black in Dante's office, the way the bathhouse windows had gone bright. One second the lamp over the table was burning, and the next there was nothing, and the whole building went with it. He heard it go. Down through the floor, the lamp in Melena's room on the second floor, and the one in the stairwell, and the one in the cellar by the boiler. Through the wall, the radiator stopped knocking. Out in the street, through the window, the lamp over the door went dark.

Rhea's voice stopped.

In the dark, the dead came closer.

He could feel them. He'd been feeling them since Halflight, hooked into his marks, a crowd at his back, murmuring. Now they weren't at his back. They were in the room. They were all around the table, pressing in, so many and so close that the air had gone thick with them, cold, like the inside of the wash crypt. He couldn't see them. He didn't need to.

They were saying something.

At first it was just murmuring. A hundred voices too soft to make out, rising and falling like water against a wall. Then it changed. It came together. All those voices, all at once, finding the same shape, saying the same thing, over and over, louder, closer, right up against his ear.

*Silas.*

His name. His given name. The one Camille had picked.

*Mercer.*

His father's. The one on the route marks, the one on the ledger, the one on the door of an office that didn't have a sign.

*Graves.*

His mother's. The one on his marks. The one the Ring called him. The one that meant, in Shadowmere, a man who put things in the ground.

*Silas. Mercer. Graves.*

The dead knew all his names. They were saying them the way Isola had said her cousin's on the Halflight floor, each part set down carefully before the next. Like they'd been taught. Like they were calling him by the whole of who he was, so there'd be no part of him left over that could say it hadn't heard.

*Silas. Mercer. Graves.*

In the dark, on the table, under his hand, he felt Isola's breath catch.

Then stop.

Down on the western side of the building, where his mother's oldest threshold used to be, where Remy had stripped the wards that afternoon, something struck the door.
