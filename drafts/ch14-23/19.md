# CHAPTER 19: SILAS

She wasn't breathing.

He had his hand on her. He didn't remember putting it there. His good hand, his left, flat on her chest just below her collarbone, over the strip she was still holding against her heart. He'd put it there when the lamps went out, the way you'd put a hand out in the dark to find a wall. Now he could feel what was under it.

Nothing.

No rise. No fall. Her chest under his palm had gone still the way water goes still. Under the strip, under her coat, under his hand, her heart was doing something, he could feel it, but it was slow and far away and uneven, like somebody knocking on a door from very deep inside a house.

"Rhea."

"I know."

"She's not—"

"I *know*, Silas."

A light came on.

It wasn't a lamp. It was low and orange and moving, and it came from the doorway, and it came from a pair of hands. Melena stood there in the dark of the stairwell with Remy behind her, and she was holding her hands out in front of her, palms up, and they were burning. Not the way they'd burned in Dante's office or at Blue Circle. Low. Careful. Like coals somebody was holding very still so they wouldn't catch. The light came up off her palms and lit the room in a soft red glow, the table and the white linen and the girl lying on it, and Rhea's face, and Silas's hand.

"You needed light," Melena said. Her voice was shaking. "Remy said you needed light."

Nobody answered her. Silas couldn't. He was looking at Isola's leg.

In the orange light, he could see the black clearly. All of it. And it was moving now, really moving, not a hair at a time. The highest branch was climbing the inside of her thigh in a slow, steady line, the way ink climbs paper, and it was nearly at the crease of her hip.

*When it reaches the hip,* Camille had said, *that's the door.*

Down in the building, something struck a door.

Silas felt it in the floor. In the legs of the table. A single blow, heavy and dull, from somewhere below them on the western side. Then another. Then quiet. Then another, closer. It was coming up. One floor at a time, one door at a time, through every threshold Remy had stripped bare that afternoon so Silas could carry a hundred dead men up the stairs on his back. There was nothing left in this building to stop anything.

The dead in the room pressed closer. They were still saying his names. *Silas. Mercer. Graves.* Soft now, almost gentle, the way you'd say a man's name to wake him.

"What is that," Melena whispered. "What's on the stairs."

Nobody answered her.

The next blow hit the door of 3C.

The whole frame jumped. The wood bowed in, and held, and the old chain on the latch rattled against the jamb. Remy pulled Melena away from the doorway and put himself between her and the door without thinking about it. Silas didn't move. He couldn't take his hand off Isola's chest.

The door bowed again.

Through the gap between the door and the frame, where the wood had pulled away from the jamb, he saw it.

It was standing on the landing. It was tall. It had a man's shape, a man's height, broad through the shoulders, and the shoulders were the first thing he saw and the thing he couldn't stop seeing. He knew those shoulders. He'd stood behind them in the map room. He'd watched them through the whisperglass and across his father's desk and in a hundred doorways his whole life. The set of them. The way the left one sat a little lower than the right.

Dante's shoulders.

It didn't have a face. Where a face should've been, there was nothing, a smooth grey dark like the inside of a veil, and across the top of it, where a brow would be, a single mark cut deep.

The short lower hook.

Silas stared at it through the gap in the door. Behind him, the dead had gone quiet.

The shape lifted one arm, slow, and struck the door again.

---

He would remember later that he didn't decide. That was the thing he'd come back to, sitting alone at this same table in the nights after, with her name on his wrist and the dead on his chain. He'd want there to have been a moment where he weighed it. Where he looked at the choices the way Camille had taught him to look at a death tether, three branches and a hip and a door, and picked one.

There wasn't. There was a shape with his father's shoulders at the door, and a girl on his table who'd stopped breathing, and black three inches from her hip, and the dead all around him, waiting.

He knew what it meant. That was the worst part. He'd known since he could talk. He'd heard Camille say it over bodies in the back room, quiet, with her hand flat on the board. He'd heard Dante say it, once, in a voice Silas never heard him use again. *Mine.* It wasn't a word. It was a claim. In the Graves line, it meant protection until burial. It meant no other hand collected this body, not the Synod's, not a Shroud's, not death's. It meant whatever came for her had to come through you.

And it meant if she died anyway, under your hand, with your claim on her, then every dead road inside you kept her. Forever. She'd never get to go wherever the dead were supposed to go. She'd stay in you.

It was the biggest thing a Graves could do to another person. It was the thing you didn't do. You didn't do it without a word from them, and you didn't do it to a stranger, and you didn't do it in a house with your sister standing right there telling you not to.

He looked down at Isola's face in Melena's orange light. Her eyes were closed. Her lips weren't moving anymore. They'd stopped shaping her cousin's name somewhere on the stairs, and he hadn't noticed when.

"Mine," Silas said.

The dead said it with him.

All of them. Every one. Every cold hand hooked under every mark on his skin, every voice in the dark. They said it at the same moment, in his voice, a hundred of them, so that the word came out of him and filled the room and shook the window glass in its frame.

*Mine.*

At the door, the shape stopped.

"No."

Rhea. She'd come around the table. She had her hand on his arm, his left arm, the one with his palm on Isola's chest, and her fingers were digging into him hard enough to bruise.

"No, Silas. You can't. You can't claim her."

"I already did."

"Then *take it back*. You can take it back. It's not closed yet." Her face was very close to his, lit orange from below, and she looked like Camille. She looked like Camille so much that for one second he was six years old at the kitchen table. "You don't know her. She doesn't know you. She can't say yes. You *know* she can't say yes, you're standing there with your hand on her chest because she's not breathing, and you want to put your name on her? You want to put *her* name on *you*? Without her?"

"Si." Remy, from the doorway. His voice was very quiet. "Si, don't."

He didn't look at either of them.

"Take it back," Rhea said again.

"I know who," Silas said.

Rhea's fingers went still on his arm.

He didn't know what he meant by it. He'd said it before he thought it. But he heard it come out of his mouth, and he knew it was true. He knew who. He knew whose name he was asking the dead to hand him. He wasn't claiming a body on a table. He was claiming the woman who'd stood at Pell's counter with her hand in her skirt and told him to watch where he was backing up. Who'd sat on a cold stoop on Bath Row all day so she could follow him to a rail bridge and not come out. Who'd closed her fist over a coin and said *Ask*, and taken black three only after he moved his hand away, and turned around three steps from the Ashvein gate to give him her first name.

Her first name. Not the rest of it. She'd kept the rest. She'd given him exactly what she wanted him to have and kept everything else, and he'd taken that from her and been grateful.

He didn't know her full name. He'd never asked. He'd been waiting for her to give it to him.

The dead gave it to him instead.

He felt it come. Not in his ears. In his marks, the ones on the inside of his left wrist, the oldest ones, the names Camille had bound into him first. He felt the dead reach down into the dark under Shadowmere, down the roads they walked, and find it. Find her. Because Wraithmoor names answered to being held. He'd watched her prove it on the Halflight floor, holding her cousin's name against a Shroud with nothing but her own voice. A Wraithmoor name, held tight enough by anybody, would answer.

He was holding hers tighter than anybody had ever held anything.

*Isola.*

He felt it first. Before he saw it. A burning on the inside of his wrist, between two of the old names, in a space he hadn't known was there. Then he looked down.

Letters.

They were opening on his skin, one at a time, the way a cut opens. Wet and black. Not ink. Something darker than ink, something that shone. The first one opened slow, a long stroke and a dot, an *I*, sitting in the space between two names his mother had put there before he could walk.

*Amara.*

He hadn't known that one. The dead said it, and he felt it go into him, and he knew it now.

*Renae.*

Rae's. The name her godmother had given her in front of witnesses. The one that meant somebody had chosen her.

*Wraithmoor.*

The whole of her. Every name she had, every name her family had written on a wall so the Synod could never take her by striking out just one. Given to him by the dead, without her saying a word, while she lay on his table not breathing.

The first name kept opening. Letter by letter. *I.* *S.* Slow. Wet. Black.

He thought about her at Halflight, three steps from the gate. Turning around. Chin up. *Isola.* Just that. Just the first one, the one her mama picked, the one that was hers before anybody else got a say in it.

*O.*

That was the one he was taking. Not the whole chain. Just the one she'd given him. He was taking the one thing she'd handed him on purpose, and the dead were giving him the rest of her to go with it, and she'd never be able to take any of it back.

*L.*

He knew. He knew exactly what this was. He'd want, later, to tell himself he hadn't understood, that it had all happened too fast, that he'd been out of his mind with the dead in his ears and his father's shoulders at the door. It wouldn't be true. He understood all of it. He understood it better than he'd ever understood anything.

He leaned down. He put his mouth close to her ear. She couldn't hear him. She was past hearing anything. He said it anyway.

"I'm sorry."

He said it quietly. It wasn't for Rhea or for Remy or for the girl in the doorway with fire in her hands. It wasn't for the dead. It was for her. It was the only thing he had to give her that she hadn't already given him first, and he knew it wasn't enough, and he knew it wasn't anywhere close.

He didn't stop.

*A.*

The last letter closed.

---

Isola took a breath.

He felt it under his hand. All at once. Her chest coming up off the table under his palm, deep, so deep, the kind of breath a person takes coming up from the bottom of a cistern. Her whole body arched with it. Her eyes didn't open. Her hand tightened on the strip against her heart.

Then she let it out. And took another.

Down her leg, the black let go of her hip.

He saw it in Melena's light. The highest branch, the one that had been climbing, the one that had been an inch from the door, stopped. It hung there for a long moment on the inside of her thigh, dead and dark. Then it turned. Slowly. It bent away from her hip like a plant bending toward a window, and it turned, and it pointed down her leg, and across the linen, toward his hand.

Toward him.

Through the gap in the door, the shape with his father's shoulders stood very still. Then it stepped back. Silas didn't see where it went. He just saw the landing, empty, and the gap in the door, and nothing in it.

The dead in the room went quiet.

Not gone. They were still there, still hooked into his marks, still crowded close. But they'd stopped saying his names. They'd gotten what they'd come for. They'd gotten a claim spoken in the old words, by a Graves, over a body that would've been theirs in another minute, and they'd heard it, and they'd answered it, and they were satisfied.

Silas looked down at his wrist.

*Isola.* Wet and black and shining, between two names his mother had put there before he could walk. Five letters. Not the rest of it. Just the one.

He felt it pulse. Once. In time with her heart under his other hand.

Then he felt his own heart stop.

It wasn't pain. He'd expected pain. It was quieter than that. It was like something in his chest had reached the end of a long rope and found nothing there, and just let go. He felt it skip, and then not come back. He felt his lungs reach for air and find that they'd forgotten how. He was still standing. He was still looking at his wrist. And everything inside him was going very slow, and very far away, and very quiet, like somebody knocking on a door from deep inside a house.

He knew what it was. He'd been told. Every Graves child was told. Your body took the shape of the ending you touched. He'd put his hand on a dying woman and made her his, and his body had heard him, and done what bodies did.

"Silas," Rhea said, from somewhere.

His knees went. His hand slid off Isola's chest. He felt the linen go out from under his fingers, and the edge of the table go by, and the floor come up, and then he was lying on the boards of his own kitchen with his cheek against the wood and his black right hand flung out beside him and his left wrist up, so that he could see her name on it in Melena's light, shining.

He tried to breathe. Nothing came.

Above him, on the table, he heard Isola take another breath, deep and steady, all on her own.

That was all right, then.

He thought that was all right.
