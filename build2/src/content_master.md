# The Tandem Companion — Content Master

Stage 3 · Authored Companion Copy · Sep 26, 2026 · @Dany

## Part I — How This Companion Works

This Part teaches you to trust a page in under ten minutes. Every later Part assumes you know what a status marker permits, which source wins in which domain, and why a character can't know something just because you do.

### I.1 What the Tandem Companion Is

The Tandem Companion is the working reference for four novels set in one physical world: *3 Shadowmere Lane*, *For Their Entertainment*, *A Dream So Twisted*, and *The Sea Will Keep*. It holds the canon those books have already committed to, the rulings that settled their contradictions, and the open questions they haven't answered yet. It sits beside the manuscript while you draft. It isn't a replacement for it.

The Companion does five jobs. It remembers what's been ruled and drafted so you don't have to reread three generations of files to find out. It distinguishes things a drafter will blur: the Nightmare organization from the ancient entity, Katherine's Frost from Zephyr's, the forensic record of Remy's death from Melena's guilt about it. It connects the books only where a ruling or source connects them. It protects chronology and knowledge, so nobody in 3SL behaves as if they've read ADST. And it catches resurrected ideas, the retired concepts that keep sneaking back because they were once written down with confidence.

It refuses a few jobs on purpose. It won't fill a gap because the gap looks awkward in print. It won't pick the nicer version of a conflict. It won't invent a sigil, a motto, a date, a map coordinate, or a line of scripture to make a page feel finished. It isn't a fan wiki, a marketing synopsis, or a workbook of blank lines waiting for you to do the thinking. When the sources don't know something, the Companion says so plainly, and that sentence is worth more than a confident guess.

### I.2 The Manuscript Outranks the Companion

Drafted prose controls drafted events. If a chapter in the controlling generation shows something happening, it happened, and the Companion records it. If that chapter deliberately changes something the Companion already printed, the Companion changes. A printed page never gets to veto a scene.

That rule has limits that matter as much as the rule. The manuscript controls what it actually shows: POV, scene order, revealed identities, performed magic, relationships, and immediate consequences. It doesn't automatically control national geography, deep history, or magical taxonomy just because a character says something about them. When Silas thinks nobody born in the Underside carries fire, that's a drafted fact about what Silas believes. It becomes world-level canon only if a ruling or the governing worldbuilding agrees. A manuscript can also be wrong by accident. A typo, a stray name from an older generation, or a detail that contradicts a ruling isn't a deliberate change; it's a drafting flag, and the Companion logs it as one instead of rewriting canon around it.

Open and provisional material works the other way. Where the Companion marks something \[?\] OPEN or SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL, drafting is allowed to settle it. When a scene commits to an answer on purpose, that commitment becomes the new record, and the Companion's update protocol (I.6) moves the old version to the Legacy Recovery Index rather than deleting it.

> **GUARDRAIL** — Never bend a scene to match a Companion page. If the scene is right and the page is wrong, fix the page. If the scene contradicts an author ruling (a TCD), stop and decide which one you mean; don't let either quietly win.

Why it matters: the Companion is only trustworthy if it can't become a second, rival manuscript. The moment a printed entry starts dictating prose, the books stop being written and start being transcribed from an index.

### I.3 How Source Authority Works

Authority is domain-specific. A source can settle one kind of fact and have no say over another. The 3SL draft is the last word on what happens in Dante Mercer's office in chapter 3; it isn't the last word on how far Crownlight sits above sea level. The Thalara worldbuilding file governs Thalaran geography; it can't overrule a ruling about the Drowning. Every claim in this book is trusted because of the rung it stands on in its own domain.

**The authority ladder**

| Rung | Source type | What it controls | What it can't do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Explicit author rulings (Canon Decision Log, TCD-001 onward) | Every domain a ruling addresses | Nothing outranks it except a later ruling |
| 2 | Current manuscript pages | What happened on-page: POV, scene chronology, revealed identity, performed magic, relationships, immediate consequences | Settle geography, history, or taxonomy by a character's say-so |
| 3 | Locked constraints (*Novel Constraints*, as corrected by rulings) | Intended premise and undrafted core engine | Override a drafted scene or a later ruling |
| 4 | Current outlines and chapter plans | Undrafted plot, once their generation is confirmed | Overwrite anything already drafted |
| 5 | Current book-specific worldbuilding, character, magic, and institutional files | Geography, institutions, culture, baseline magic, backstory where the manuscript is silent | Contradict a ruling or an on-page event |
| 6 | Shared Tandem lore | Its claims one at a time, only where they survive review | Serve as a blanket master plan |
| 7 | Supporting artifacts (timelines, placement tables, genealogy workbooks, poems, quote maps) | Their narrow domain, after consistency checks | Fill gaps outside that domain |
| 8 | Marketing files | Positioning language | Establish any canon |
| 9 | Legacy drafts and plans | Creative history and recovery leads | Answer a current question |
| 10 | TKC Companion materials | Structural and philosophical models only | Contribute any Tandem canon, ever |

Two rules sit above the ladder. First, the Tandem Source Authority Audit's rulings override conflicting source material wherever they speak. Second, filenames and dates confer nothing. "FINAL," "Official," "New," and a recent year are labels a file picked up along the way. *FTE 2025 FINAL* is Legacy. *New FTE Chapters* predates the modern Remnia execution. A later date on the TSWK blueprint doesn't make it a ruling.

**Worked example: the Beta versus the 2025 FINAL.** *FTE 2025 FINAL* is a complete 47-chapter manuscript. The *FTE Official Beta Reader Copy* stops at chapter 29. A reader who trusts completeness picks the FINAL and gets the ending for free. TCD-001 says no: the Beta is the controlling prose generation, and nothing outside it becomes canon until it's rewritten into that generation. The FINAL still earns its shelf space as planning evidence for the unfinished half, but its simple Dyad/Crossblood explanation, its reveal shortcut, its cause for Atlas's deterioration, and its Tiran-only forfeit are all superseded by TCD-049 through TCD-052. The complete file lost to the incomplete one because the incomplete one is the book being written.

**Current controlling generations at a glance**

| Book | Controls drafted events | Supports undrafted work | Status of the rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3SL | *3 Shadowmere Lane First Draft*, 22 chapters | 32-chapter outline for fixed anchors only; *3SL Character Outlines*; *3SL Full Worldbuilding* | The outline's chapter map no longer controls (TCD-002) |
| FTE | *FTE Official Beta Reader Copy*, through ch. 29 | Chapter 29 fragment and chs. 31–39 (rewrite required); *The Challenges FTE Year*; *49th Chess Tournament Placements* | Every pre-Beta generation is Legacy (TCD-001) |

| TSWK | No manuscript | *Thalara Worldbuilding*; *Azrine and Levi*; July 2026 44-chapter blueprint, provisional by claim | Ending open (TCD-012) |

The Companion treats each as the audited generation because its content matches the audit's description. *The Imperial Lineage* appears in the Project but not in the audit inventory, so it holds no assigned authority until it's classified; nothing in Parts I–III relies on it.

Why it matters: most canon errors in a four-book universe aren't invented facts. They're real facts pulled from the wrong generation or the wrong domain. The ladder is how the Companion answers "which file?" before it answers "what's true?"

### I.4 Reading a Claim: Status Markers

A status marker attaches to a claim, not to a page. One dossier page can hold settled canon, a drafted fact, a plan, and an open question side by side, and each keeps its own label. The marker also controls the grammar. A \[P\] claim written in confident present tense is a canon error even with the \[P\] sitting next to it, because readers believe sentences before they believe badges.

**Status key**

| Marker | Meaning | Permitted wording | Forbidden wording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \[C\] CANON | Established by an author ruling or a controlling source in its domain | Plain declarative present tense, with the TCD or source cited | Hedging that makes settled canon look optional |
| CURRENT DRAFTED TEXT | Established on-page in the controlling manuscript generation | "On-page, …" · "As drafted, …" · "In ch. 7, …" | Treating it as immune to revision |
| SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL | A current plan, bible, blueprint, or artifact that hasn't become drafted canon | "The outline plans…" · "The blueprint proposes…" · "The worldbuilding file describes…" | Present-tense narration of events as though they happened |
| \[P\] POSSIBLE | A live candidate or recovery idea, not approved | "A candidate is…" · "One viable reading…" · "If recovered, …" | "Is," "will," or "does" about the story world |
| \[?\] OPEN | Unresolved; an author decision is needed if and when drafting forces it | State what's fixed, then name exactly what isn't | Filling the gap with a plausible answer |
| \[X\] CONFLICT | Current sources disagree and no ruling settles it | Name both sources and both claims | Picking the nicer version silently |
| \[R\] RESEARCH | Needs outside factual research | State the research question | Presenting a guess as fact |
| LEGACY / SUPERSEDED | Creative history that no longer controls | Past tense, source named: "The 2025 FINAL had…" | Using it to answer a current question |

**How to read a claim: six worked examples**

| Claim as printed | Status | Why it's labeled this way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Katherine Nicholas is the current Tournament's Conqueror. | \[C\] CANON · TCD-034 | An author ruling fixes it, so it takes plain present tense even though the scene that earns it isn't drafted yet. The ruling controls the outcome; the manuscript still has to build the causal path. |
| On-page, Katherine's Examination puts her through severe Fire trauma and still produces only Frost. | CURRENT DRAFTED TEXT · FTE Beta; TCD-058 | The Beta shows it. TCD-058 later explains why it happens (dominant Frost configuration, no ordinary stress trigger), but the event itself is anchored in drafted prose. |
| The July 2026 blueprint plans Azrine's ordination near the end of Book One. | SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL · blueprint, per audit HC-061 | The most detailed TSWK plan proposes it. No ruling has adopted it, so it's written as a plan, never as "Azrine is ordained." |
| The Conduit Trials' final canon name hasn't been chosen. | \[?\] OPEN · TCD-008 | The concept and mechanics are current. The name is a working title, and every page that uses it flags it. |
| The blueprint's chapter 29 credits "Cael Dren" with designing the sacrificial route and First Anchor, while its chapters 38–39 identify twelve-year-old Caelen as the first sacrificed Anchor. | \[X\] CONFLICT · HC-059 | Two passages in one plan disagree. The Companion names both and protects TCD-064's twelve-year-old Caelen without deciding who designed the route. |
| The 2025-era lore treated the Echo Gate as the singular sealed crossing between Remnia and Somnoria. | LEGACY / SUPERSEDED · TCD-027 | Past tense, source named. Its seal, spiral or mirrored glyph, and ancient-crossing imagery may come back only as \[P\]. |

&#91;R\] RESEARCH appears when a scene needs real-world fact the sources can't supply. The clearest current case is the Remy death scene: once the weapon and wound are chosen, the blood-loss timing Melena works against is a research question, not a canon question (TCD-063 leaves weapon and anatomy open).

**Page furniture you'll see throughout**

- *Source —* lines close each section. They're margin provenance in the manuscript and become small-type margin notes in layout.
- **GUARDRAIL** boxes hold locked constraints a drafter could break without noticing.
- **NOT THE SAME AS** callouts separate paired terms readers merge.
- **OPEN DEVELOPMENT** boxes carry four lines: Fixed / Undecided / Why it matters / Decide by.
- **TANDEM CANON DECISION** blocks appear only where a page genuinely can't proceed.
- **See also** lines point to related sections by number.

Why it matters: the status system is the reason this book can be trusted at 2 a.m. in the middle of a chapter. If a claim has no marker and no qualifying grammar, you're meant to be able to rely on it without checking.

### I.5 Knowledge States

No later revelation travels backward into an earlier head. That sounds obvious until the fourth book is drafted and a character in the first one starts making choices that only make sense if she's read the Canon Decision Log. The Companion prevents that by keeping seven lanes of knowledge apart wherever a secret, identity, reveal, or piece of history is involved.

**The seven lanes**

| Lane | What it records | Example of the lane in use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objective truth | What is true in the universe | The gang leader's wound kills Remy (TCD-063). |
| Author knowledge | What Danyelle has ruled or knows, whether or not anyone on-page does | Katherine and Melena are paternal half-sisters through Tobias (TCD-040), long before either sister proves it. |
| Reader knowledge | What a reader knows at a specific point in a specific book | Tracked per chapter in each book's knowledge ledger; never assumed from author knowledge. |
| Character knowledge | What a named character knows, and from when | Melena enters the Challenge Six Duel with substantial suspicion about Katherine and no proof (TCD-050). |
| Character belief | What a character thinks is true when it's wrong or incomplete | Early Reverie believes she's an illegal, unlicensed Dreamwalker with a rare gift; she's also a natural conduit (TCD-029). |
| Institutional / public version | What Houses, the Sanctum, the Synod, Skyhold, schools, or governments teach or record | The Sanctum's likely "Dyad" label for Katherine, which is incomplete (TCD-049). |
| Suppressed / rewritten | What's sealed, censored, misfiled, mythologized, redacted, or lost | House Dren's willing-adult First Anchor mythology versus twelve-year-old Caelen (TCD-064). |

**The six levels of cross-land knowledge.** Knowing another land exists doesn't mean knowing where it is, how its magic works, how to reach it, what its history is, or how the four lands actually relate. TCD-016 separates these into six levels, and none implies another:

1. **Existence** — the land is real.
2. **Location** — where it is.
3. **Functional** — how its people, magic, or institutions work.
4. **Route** — how to get there.
5. **Historical** — what happened between the lands.
6. **True Tandem knowledge** — the objective relationship among all four.

A drafted 3SL example shows why the ladder matters. In chapter 11, Remy jokes that the only place Melena can't reach the blackroot is Somnoria, and Silas hopes she won't ask what Somnoria is. On-page, that establishes that both men know Somnoria by name. It doesn't establish that either knows where it is, how to get there, or anything about dream magic. In chapter 3, Silas reads Melena's fire as proof she's Remnian because "fire belonged to Remnia." That's functional knowledge about one feature of Remnian magic, held as Silas's belief, and it says nothing about what the Synod teaches or what Silas knows of Remnian history. CURRENT DRAFTED TEXT (3SL chs. 3, 11).

**Worked example: two clocks for one missing man.** Elias Lockwood and Professor Alexander Thorne are one person with two public lives, and the two lives go missing on different schedules.

| Lane | Elias Lockwood / Professor Alexander Thorne | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objective truth | Elias voluntarily created and maintained Thorne as an established Somnorian researcher and instructor for years. The Nightmare organization targets Thorne's boundary/Network research and exploits the vulnerability it creates, compromising his dream and magical functioning so that ordinary return to Remnia becomes unsafe or impossible. | \[C\] TCD-032, TCD-062 |
| Author knowledge | The failure-to-return cause is fixed. The exact compromise technique, first crossing, alias credentials, helpers, later captivity, and physical status are not. | \[?\] OPEN |
| Institutional / public version, Remnia | Remnia notices Elias missing first, because he fails to return on pattern. | \[C\] TCD-062 |
| Institutional / public version, Somnoria | Thorne initially remains publicly present but deteriorating; his Somnorian disappearance comes later. | \[C\] TCD-062 |
| Character belief | Breakdown rumors circulate in Somnoria. They reflect a real, patterned compromise, not the whole truth. | \[C\] TCD-062 |
| Character knowledge | Reverie encounters Thorne's collapsing dream. Whether and when she learns Thorne is Elias isn't established here. | Encounter \[C\] TCD-062; identity knowledge \[?\] OPEN |
| Reader knowledge | Set chapter by chapter in the ADST and FTE knowledge ledgers (V.16, IV.22). | Tracked, not assumed |

Why it matters: the two clocks are the whole trick of this plotline. Remnia is already searching while Somnoria still sees Thorne teaching. Collapse the lanes and one country reacts to news it hasn't received.

> **GUARDRAIL** — In-world language survives as perspective, never as cosmology. A Somnorian calling Remnia "another world" is Somnorian framing (TCD-015). House Aether calling the Prism a "cage" is House Aether's interpretation (TCD-038). Caelum calling Melena his "asset" is Caelum's framing (TCD-043).

### I.6 Open Canon, Decisions, and Updates

Open stays open. The Companion doesn't close a question because a page looks unfinished with it, and it doesn't ask for a ruling just because the book reached that topic. It asks only when a page, a scene, or a publication-level commitment can't be written accurately without an answer.

**The OPEN DEVELOPMENT box.** Wherever an open point sits beside settled canon, it gets a four-line box. Here's a live one, reproduced from Part II:

> **OPEN DEVELOPMENT — The Remnia–Underside crossing**
>
> **Fixed:** A current crossing exists near the London Town edge, in the no-man's-land associated with London Town, Old Prosperity, and Alea. It's physical-magical transit, not a portal. The singular Echo Gate is Legacy. (TCD-017, TCD-027)
>
> **Undecided:** Its precise map coordinate, and whether it arrives directly in Shadowmere, Tier 7.
>
> **Why it matters:** Arrival tier decides who controls the Underside end, who can trace a crossing, and how Melena's route from Remnia into Shadowmere reads on-page.
>
> **Decide by:** When Danyelle's Remnia map is reviewed, or earlier if a 3SL scene has to show the arrival point.

The "Decide by" line is the useful one. It names the actual drafting moment that forces the answer. If nothing forces it, the line says "safe to defer," and that's a complete answer.

**The TANDEM CANON DECISION block.** When a page truly can't proceed, the Companion uses this format and nothing looser: the exact issue, the sources creating it, options A through D where the evidence offers them, E for "Something else / I want to explain," one line of implication under each option, and a labeled recommendation only when the evidence strongly favors one path. No more than five at once; one to three is better. A recommendation is never canon until ruled.

> **TANDEM CANON DECISION — SPECIMEN (format only; not asked now)**
>
> **Issue:** Who designed First Shellward's sacrificial route and First Anchor?
>
> **Sources:** July 2026 TSWK blueprint ch. 29 ("Cael Dren" as designer) versus chs. 38–39 (twelve-year-old Caelen as first sacrificed Anchor); TCD-064.
>
> **A. Forged credit.** House record credits a figure who didn't design it. *Implication:* the tablet becomes evidence of rewritten history.
>
> **B. Distinct adult designer.** Cael Dren and Caelen are different people. *Implication:* two names need separate provenance.
>
> **C. Drafting error.** The blueprint conflated names. *Implication:* one passage is corrected; no in-world lie.
>
> **E. Something else / I want to explain.**
>
> **Recommendation:** None. The evidence doesn't strongly favor one option, and HC-059 is deferred to the TSWK draft.

**How a ruling enters the book.** When a new ruling lands or drafted prose deliberately changes something:

1. Log it in the Canon Decision Log with its date, what it supersedes, and its downstream implications.
2. Update the High-Impact Conflict Register if it resolves or opens a conflict.
3. Update every Companion page listed in the ruling's affected-sections field.
4. Move superseded claims to the Legacy Recovery Index rather than deleting them.
5. Re-run the integrity checks on every touched page: no Legacy claim presented as canon, no \[P\] or SUPPORTED claim in declarative story-world tense, no conflict silently harmonized, no knowledge promoted backward, no symmetry assumed across the four lands, no duplicate or contradictory table, no invented canon, no open question phrased as settled, no TKC content imported.

> **GUARDRAIL** — Asking a question assigns no TCD number. The audit's proposed TCD-065 through TCD-074 were never answered or adopted, so none of them is canon and none of those numbers is reserved.

Why it matters: an open question that's clearly marked is safe to draft around. An open question that's been quietly answered by whoever wrote the page last is how three versions of the same backstory end up in print.

### I.7 Using the Companion While Drafting

Start with the book's draft-forward packet, not the front of the Companion. Each dossier ends with pages built to sit open beside the manuscript: III.19 for 3SL, IV.24 for FTE, V.17 for ADST, VI.13 for TSWK. They condense the locked beats, the current knowledge states, and the scene-level open choices, and they cross-reference the full sections behind them.

**Before a scene**

- [ ] Check the packet for any GUARDRAIL that touches the characters or magic in the scene.
- [ ] Check the knowledge ledger for what each POV character knows *at this chapter*, not at the end of the book.
- [ ] Check whether the scene touches a \[C\] ruling. If it does, the outcome is fixed and the scene's job is to earn it.
- [ ] Check whether the scene touches an \[?\] OPEN item. If the open item doesn't affect this scene, keep drafting.

**While drafting**

- An open question that doesn't touch the next scene doesn't stop the next scene.
- If the scene needs an answer to an open item, make the choice in prose and flag it. You don't need a ruling first; you need a record afterward.
- If the scene seems to need something that contradicts a ruling, stop. That's the one case where the draft waits.
- Don't pull a fact from a Legacy file to fill a gap, however well it fits. Check the Legacy Recovery Index; if the idea is listed there as recoverable, it comes back as \[P\] and gets ruled on like anything new.

**After the scene**

- [ ] If the prose settled an open item on purpose, log it through the update protocol (I.6).
- [ ] If the prose changed a knowledge state, update the book's knowledge ledger.
- [ ] If the prose introduced a new name, place, route, or rule, add it to the relevant dossier with its chapter as provenance.

**Navigation conventions.** Sections are numbered Part.section (III.8, VII.5), so insertions don't renumber the book. "See also" lines at each section's foot point outward. Rulings are cited by TCD number, conflicts by HC number, and manuscript facts by book and chapter.

Why it matters: the Companion should cost you seconds mid-scene, not an evening. If using it slows drafting down more than it protects the draft, the page is built wrong, and that's a Companion problem to fix.

*Source — Production Master I.7, A8; Audit, Architecture Handoff.*

See also: III.19, IV.24, V.17, VI.13.

## Part II — The Shared Tandem Spine

This is what holds across all four books before any single story gets its own dossier. Nearly all of it comes straight from author rulings, which is why it can be written with confidence now. Its open slots are mostly cartographic and deep-historical: the Companion knows how the lands relate and how the ancient break worked in outline, and it doesn't yet know coastlines, dates, or the rupture's actual cause.

### II.1 One Physical World

Remnia, Somnoria, Thalara, and the Underside exist in one physical world as separate nations and territories. \[C\] TCD-015. They aren't planets, dimensions, or parallel universes. Every scene that moves a person, an object, or a piece of news between lands is moving it across real distance, around or through real barriers.

The older two-world material said otherwise. The May 2025 *Full Tandem Lore Document* built Remnia and Somnoria as separate worlds joined at a singular Echo Gate. That cosmology is LEGACY / SUPERSEDED. Its shared-origin idea survived in expanded four-land form (II.5), and the Echo Gate survives only as a recovery candidate (II.3).

So why do characters keep saying "world" and "realm"? Because from inside their lives it's accurate. Extreme isolation, magical boundaries, restricted routes, and controlled knowledge make the other lands functionally unreachable for most people, so cultures describe them the way people describe the unreachable. The Companion keeps that language as perspective. It never promotes it to physics.

**Four kinds of reality, kept apart**

| Kind of reality | What's true | Where it's governed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical | One world. Remnia and Somnoria are separate continental landmasses with ocean between them. The Underside descends off Zenithia's side of Remnia. Thalara, primarily underwater, lies farther out in the island/sea region. | \[C\] TCD-015, TCD-025, TCD-026 |
| Political | Separate nations and territories, each with its own authorities. Remnia is a centralized empire with overlapping power centers; Somnoria is governed nationally by its High Council; the Underside's drafted institutions include the Synod; Thalara's are set by its worldbuilding. | \[C\] TCD-021, TCD-041; 3SL draft; *Thalara Worldbuilding* |
| Cultural | Each land's people may call the others separate worlds or realms. That's framing born of isolation, and it's valid as in-world voice. | \[C\] TCD-015 |
| Magical and travel | Boundaries, restricted routes, and controlled passages make crossing rare, costly, or impossible for most people. Routes cross real distance and barriers; they never open onto another dimension. | \[C\] TCD-015, TCD-017 |

> **NOT THE SAME AS** — "Another world" in a character's mouth is not "another world" in the Companion's narration. The first is voice. The second would be a canon error.

Why it matters: the physical fact changes what's possible. If the lands share one world, then news can leak, trade can happen off the books, weather and water connect places, and a route's reach is a question of distance and barriers rather than metaphysics. It also means Convergence (II.12) can never mean continents sliding together.

*Source — TCD-015, TCD-017, TCD-021, TCD-025, TCD-026, TCD-041; Audit inventory note on* Full Tandem Lore Document *(LEGACY by claim).*

See also: II.2, II.4, II.15.

### II.2 Macro Geography

The four lands occupy multiple major landmasses and regions in one world. \[C\] TCD-025. Four placements are fixed:

- **Remnia and Somnoria** are separate continental landmasses with ocean between them.
- **The Underside** attaches to the Zenithia side, or corner, of Remnia's broader geography and descends from there. \[C\] TCD-025, TCD-026
- **Thalara**, primarily underwater, lies farther outward in the island and sea region.

> **GUARDRAIL — What the analogy does not mean.** It doesn't import real borders, coastlines, scale, distances, climate, culture, languages, or history. Routes are magically enabled transit through real physical passages. \[C\] TCD-017. ### II.4 Knowledge of the Other Lands

There's no universal answer to "do people know about the other lands?" Awareness varies by nation, class, institution, profession, network, person, and time. \[C\] TCD-016. A smuggler, a Sanctum archivist, and a miner's kid in the same land can hold three different pictures, and all three can change between chapter 5 and chapter 25.

The six levels from I.5 do the sorting: existence, location, functional, route, historical, and true Tandem knowledge. None implies another. Someone can know Somnoria's name and nothing about where it is. Someone can use a route without knowing what's on the far side of the boundary it crosses. Someone can know a land's history as their own institution teaches it, which may be the least reliable version available.

Four kinds of source feed a character's picture, and the Companion tracks them separately: what their institution teaches or officially claims, what their network passes around as rumor or trade knowledge, what's been suppressed or rewritten, and what they've learned firsthand. An error counts as knowledge for drafting purposes. A character acts on what she believes, and the Companion records the belief with its lane label.

### II.5 Shared Magical Ancestry

All four modern magical traditions ultimately draw from one primordial magical source or substrate that predates the modern division of the Tandem world. \[C\] TCD-035. The Divide didn't create four unrelated magics. Geography, environment, bodies, bloodlines, culture, survival, history, and institutions reshaped one shared power into systems that are now genuinely distinct, with their own access, costs, classifications, and cultural explanations.

Both halves of that ruling carry weight. The shared root means cross-land resonance is possible without anyone inventing a coincidence. The genuine distinctness means a Somnorian dream technique doesn't work in Remnia just because both descend from the same source, and a Thalaran seal cadence isn't a Silencecraft seal with a different accent. Long isolation after the Divide accelerated the specialization. \[C\] TCD-045

### II.7 The Divide

The Divide is a two-stage historical crisis. \[C\] TCD-045. First, a non-deliberate rupture destabilized the primordial magical system and the relations among the lands. Its nature may have been natural, celestial, cosmic, or magical; the ruling fixes that it wasn't deliberate and leaves the cause open. Second, ancient peoples responded. They created, adapted, bound, activated, or repurposed seals, artifacts, routes, living systems, and boundary structures to contain the damage.

The response didn't cause the rupture. It hardened the rupture's consequences into long-term separation. That's the distinction the whole spine rests on: something broke, and then people built walls around the break, and the walls outlasted everyone who remembered why they were built.

### II.8 The Drowning and the First Seal

The Drowning is not the Divide under another name. It's a significantly later, staged Thalaran regional catastrophe, shaped by environmental and magical instability and by political decisions. \[C\] TCD-055. The First Seal is the deliberate final emergency response to it: Thalarans bound the preexisting Leviathan, made an existing Seal Song operational, and sacrificed part of Thalara to stabilize the rest, using or intersecting older Tandem boundary infrastructure as they did.

Three events, kept distinct:

1. **The ancient rupture** that began the Divide. (II.7)
2. **The Drowning**, Thalara's later catastrophe.
3. **The sacrifice decision**, the First Seal, made during the Drowning's final collapse.

### II.12 Convergence

### III.3 The Underside: A Vertical Nation

### III.8 Graveshand, Deathwork, and Severance

Graveshand is inherited Graves-family magic, and it sits outside the formal Six Disciplines. \[C\] TCD-004. Deathwork is the broader category: ending-magic and its effects, which Graveshand can perceive, manipulate, sever, redirect, or create. A Shroud's death tether is Deathwork. Silas's answer to it is Graveshand.

> **NOT THE SAME AS — Graveshand / Deathwork.** Deathwork is what endings do. Graveshand is what the Graves line can do to endings. A Shroud uses Deathwork without Graveshand; Silas uses Graveshand on Deathwork. (TCD-004)

**What Graveshand is, as the draft shows it.** Silas's tattoos aren't decoration. They're Graves marks, death names Camille bound into him: "one for the family, one for the route, one for everything that came through and needed putting back" (ch. 15). Opening Graveshand doesn't just let him touch death. It gives death a route back to him. Once he opens it, every dead thing nearby knows exactly where he stands, and so does anything else listening (ch. 15). Camille taught all three of her children the family rule before she taught them the working: *never call the dead unless you're prepared to answer what comes*. Severance calls louder than anything. (CURRENT DRAFTED TEXT, ch. 15)

**The Halflight Severance, beat by beat (ch. 15)**

1. A Shroud lays a death tether in Isola's leg: three black branches above the ankle, one climbing behind the knee, one sinking toward bone. Silas knows three ways to stop it: cut the limb before the black reaches her hip; kill the Shroud and hope its work dies with it; or use Graveshand, knowing what happens when two death marks meet inside a living body.
2. He splits the half-healed burn Melena left on his palm and drags his blood down the Synod gate: "Welcome me home."
3. Halflight disappears. He stands in the dead routes under the station, old transfer paths, execution roads, buried funeral passages, and the dead who died trying to climb Halflight reach for him.
4. He wraps the Shroud's tether around his bleeding hand. The Shroud's face is made of name strips, and the strip across its forehead carries a Graves mark cut with Dante's hook.
5. "Mine. She came through my gate. Her severance is mine." The dead stop; so does the Shroud.
6. He drives his palm into the mark on its forehead. The Shroud comes apart into name strips. Every name inside it starts talking.
7. The tether snaps. His blood spreads through the gate bars and opens every lock. He crosses into Ashvein under white Synod prayer reading *Unauthorized transfer* and *High Heresy*.

### IV.3 Power and Jurisdiction

Remnia doesn't have one ruler. It has several centers of authority that overlap, compete, and sometimes need each other. \[C\] TCD-041. The Crown is the highest public civil and dynastic authority and directly controls London Town and imperial affairs. Provincial Leaders administer their territories under titles that vary. The Provincial Leadership Board gives the provinces formal representation, and a Conqueror's one-year seat can change it. The Elemental Court is a separate political-magical bloc with influence over several provinces and over elemental policy, but not over London Town. The Sanctum is semi-autonomous in magical regulation, bloodlines, Tournament law, doctrine, security, and historical control. It isn't simply the Crown's agency, and it isn't legally omnipotent over the Crown either.

The practical consequence for drafting: when something goes wrong in FTE, the first question is never "who's in charge?" It's "whose jurisdiction is this, and who else thinks it's theirs?"

### IV.6 The Tournament: Structure and Purpose

The Chess Tournament pits two provinces against each other with living Pieces on a chessboard's positions. This year it's Old Prosperity against London Town. The Empress's opening letter sets the public terms: thirty-two Pieces, "sixteen from each province, eight men and eight women"; the goal is to "capture the opposing King. Achieve Checkmate. Win."; and "to the victor, I offer a seat on the Provincial Leadership Board — a year of power, a year of favor, a year to reshape your province's future." (CURRENT DRAFTED TEXT, front matter)

That's the public half. The private half is canon too: the Sanctum uses selection, assignments, challenges, stress, injuries, alliances, and survival to classify, cultivate, control, exploit, monitor, suppress, or eliminate dangerous people. \[C\] TCD-034. ### IV.8 Conqueror Rules

The Conqueror isn't the King, and isn't whoever lands the last hit. The Conqueror is the surviving Piece whose decisive sanctioned action earns victory, and that Piece holds the winning province's Provincial Leadership Board seat for the next year. \[C\] TCD-034. Under a forfeit, the Conqueror is the surviving Piece whose final sanctioned action or matchup directly produces the decisive state that causes the forfeit.

Katherine Nicholas is this Tournament's Conqueror. \[C\] TCD-034. Atlas and Tiran's confrontation is officially a draw by intervention; Katherine and Melena produce the decisive sequence.

### IV.10 Katherine's Elemental Anatomy

This is the most carefully ruled magic in FTE, and the development chapters get it wrong in instructive ways, so read this before touching any scene where Katherine's element changes.
