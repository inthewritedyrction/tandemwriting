EIGHT QUESTIONS
Run all eight, in order, before deciding what a passage means. Not because it makes you an expert — it does not — but
because most con dent misuse of scripture happens by skipping one of them, and skipping is easy to catch in yourself if
you have a list.
1   ·    W H AT   KIND    OF   TEXT   IS   THIS?


Genre rst, always. It decides what the passage is capable of establishing before any other question is worth asking.
2   ·    WHO      IS   SPEAKING?


The narrator, a character, God as quoted, a rival, a friend who turns out to be wrong. Job's comforters are in the Bible and they are
mistaken, and the book says so.
3   ·    TO   WHOM?


A congregation with a speci c problem, a king, a foreign nation, an individual. Applying an oracle addressed to Babylon to
yourself is a category error with a long history.
4   ·    W H AT   COMES       BEFORE       AND    AFTER?


Read the paragraph, then the chapter. Most weaponized verses are load-bearing only when the sentence on either side has been
removed.
5   ·    W H AT   WORLD        PRODUCED      IT?


Empire, occupation, exile, monarchy, household economy, legal status of women and slaves. The setting is not background; it is
frequently the subject.
6   ·    W H AT   DO    THE    WORDS       DO?


Command, describe, lament, promise, threaten, concede, argue. Compare two translations and see whether they agree about
which.
7   ·    WHO      GAINS   FROM    THIS      READING?


Ask it of every reading, including the one you prefer. A reading that bene ts you is not thereby wrong — it is thereby worth
checking twice.
8   ·    W H AT   DOES    THIS   READING          COST?


And who pays it. This is the question Dominion has never once asked out loud, and the one Eden will eventually be unable to stop
asking.
