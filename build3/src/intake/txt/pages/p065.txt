W H ER E    WE      ARE


In the desert, and it is going badly. The Hebrew name means in the
wilderness; the English name comes from the two censuses that bracket the
book. Both titles are honest: this is a book about counting a population and
about that population falling apart while it waits.
W H AT    H APPE NS


A census, then complaint after complaint: food, leadership, the wisdom of
the whole project. Scouts return terri ed and the people are sentenced to
forty years of wandering. Miriam challenges Moses and is struck with
disease; a rebellion is swallowed by the ground. Five daughters petition
successfully to inherit their father's land.

Legitimate authority and the cost of questioning it. Rebellions here are
punished spectacularly — and yet in the same book ve women bring a legal
challenge to inheritance law and win, with God ruling in their favour. The
text cannot decide whether dissent is faithlessness or petition, and neither
can any empire.
