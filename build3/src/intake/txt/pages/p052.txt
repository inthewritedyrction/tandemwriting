    NOT ALL CONTESTED ALIKE
    The most important distinction in this section. Treating all four as equally open is how a companion becomes useless —
    and how a well-read person ends up unable to say that something is simply false.
    I    ·       SERIOUS         S C H O L A R LY   DISPUTE


    Specialists with the languages, the manuscripts, and the historical training disagree, and each side can state the other's
    evidence. The range of kephal . The authorship of Ephesians. Represent both; do not pick a winner in the companion's own
    voice.
    II       ·       D E N O M I N AT I O N A L   OR    TRADITIONAL            DISAGREEMENT


    Communities dier because of confessional commitments, governance, and inherited practice — not primarily because of
    contested evidence. Who may be ordained. Whether Revelation is read aloud. Name the tradition rather than treating its
    position as the neutral one.
    III          ·    OPEN     THEOLOGICAL             I N T E R P R E TAT I O N


    Questions the text raises and does not close, where evidence runs out and judgment begins. What the conquest narratives say
    about God. Whether Job is answered. These stay open honestly, and they are where a novel does its best work.

    IV           ·   UNSUPPORTED            CLAIM       OR   M I S I N F O R M AT I O N


    Assertions that fail on the evidence: invented etymologies, a word supposedly mistranslated in every Bible, a council that
    decided something it did not discuss, a manuscript claim no one can produce. These are not contested. They are wrong, and
    the companion is allowed to say so — while noting that a character may believe one sincerely and build a life on it.

             THE         SORTING       QUESTION


             Before writing any dispute into a scene: which of the four is it? Category I earns two credible characters. Category IV
             earns one character who is wrong and one who knows it — and that is a dierent scene entirely.
