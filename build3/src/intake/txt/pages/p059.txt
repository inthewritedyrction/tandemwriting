THE METHOD, WORKED
The eight questions from the previous section, answered on this passage, so you can see the shape of a completed run before
doing your own.
 1 · KIND OF TEXT                   Letter, containing a household code — occasional correspondence, not legislation, and a recognizable moral-
                                    instruction form embedded in it.
 2 · SPEAKER                        The letter's author, writing in Paul's name; authorship genuinely disputed. Not a narrator reporting, and not
                                    God quoted directly.
 3 · AUDIENCE                       A congregation — or several — in the Roman province of Asia. Wives, husbands, children, and enslaved
                                    people are all addressed as present and listening.
 4 · BEFORE & AFTER                 Before: mutual submission, addressed to all. After: husbands, then children and parents, then enslaved
                                    people and masters. The wives instruction is the shortest unit in the sequence.
 5 · THE WORLD                      Roman legal households with a male head, an economy that included slavery, a state invested in domestic
                                    order, and a small movement under intermittent suspicion.
 6 · W H AT T H E W O R D S         Instruct, using a participle dependent on the preceding sentence. One contested noun. The husband's

                                    portion is longer and makes the heavier demand.
 7 · WHO GAINS                      From the fragment: whoever holds household authority, and any state that needs households orderly. From
                                    the whole passage: considerably less clear, which is why the fragment is what gets printed.
 8 · W H AT I T C O S T S           Under the fragment reading: a woman's standing depends on a man's character, with no recourse written
                                    into the text. Ask who in Aurelia pays that, and what she is told when she says so.
