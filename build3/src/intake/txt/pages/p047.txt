FOUR TRIGGERS
Comparing English Bibles gets you a long way. These four situations mean stop and nd a real source — a lexicon, a
commentary, a scholar — before it goes into the novel as a claim.
I    ·       THE       ARGUMENT         RESTS      ON     ONE     WORD


If a whole doctrine hangs on the range of a single term, the range is the question, and English cannot answer it. This is the
situation the household verse is in.
II       ·       THE    T R A N S L AT I O N S   SPLIT    ALONG        D E N O M I N AT I O N A L   LINES


When the pattern of who renders it which way matches the pattern of who teaches what, something other than grammar is
operating — and you need to be able to name it precisely rather than gesture at bias.
III          ·   A   FOOTNOTE         S AY S     THE     TEXT   IS   U N C E R TA I N


Take the admission seriously. It means the manuscripts disagree or the syntax will not resolve, and building a scene on the
resolved version is building on a choice.

The most dangerous sentence available to a non-specialist, and the one most often deployed with total con dence by people
on every side. A character may say it. The companion may not, without a source.
