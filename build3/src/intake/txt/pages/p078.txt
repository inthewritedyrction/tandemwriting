Across three crises and, most scholars think, three sets of hands: a kingdom
under Assyrian threat, then the exile, then the disappointing return. One
scroll, three eras, and the seams are visible.

It opens by telling a devout nation that God is sick of its worship because its
hands are full of blood. Then judgment, a throne-room vision, and the great
reversals — swords into farm tools, a child ruler, a banquet for all peoples.
Later a gure appears who is despised, silent, and destroyed on other
people's behalf. It ends with a new earth and a promise aimed squarely at
eunuchs and foreigners.
