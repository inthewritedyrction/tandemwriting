Verse 21 instructs the whole congregation to submit to one another. Verse 22 addresses wives. Several of the earliest and most
heavily weighted Greek witnesses have no verb in verse 22 at all — the instruction to wives depends on the participle
governing verse 21, which makes the two verses one grammatical movement rather than two topics. Other manuscripts
supply a verb, and most English Bibles supply one too, along with a section heading printed between the verses. That heading
is modern editorial apparatus, not scripture.              M S S : P 4 6 · S I N A I T I C U S · VAT I C A N U S

The grammatical link is not in dispute; what it implies is. One reading holds that mutual submission in 21 governs everything
after it, so the household instructions are applications of a shared obligation. Another holds that 21 introduces submission
generally and 22 onward speci es its ordered forms, so mutuality and hierarchy are compatible. Both are argued by serious
scholars, and the grammar alone does not decide between them — anyone who tells you the Greek settles it is overselling, in
either direction.
