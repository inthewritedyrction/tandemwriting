THE SHAPE OF THE OLD
TESTAMENT
Thirty-nine books, and the plot beneath all of them is land: getting it, losing it, and being ruled by somebody
else's empire on it. Learn the six movements below and you will always know roughly where you are
standing, which is most of what reading well requires.
TO R A H
                            Origins, a family, slavery, escape, and a law delivered in a desert to a people who do not
Genesis–Deuteronomy         yet have a country. Everything later in the collection quotes or argues with these ve
                            books, and every empire in your novel's tradition will claim to be enforcing them.

Joshua–Ruth                 collection and ends with a sentence that functions as a political thesis: everyone did as

MONARCHY
                            A people demand a king, are explicitly warned what a king will cost them, and get one
Samuel–Chronicles           anyway. What follows is dynasty, temple, civil war, and a long record of succession by
                            murder. Chronicles retells the same events with the embarrassing parts managed — the
                             rst act of imperial editing in the book itself.
EXILE   &   RETURN
                            The state is destroyed, the population deported, and the survivors must work out how
Ezra–Esther                 to be a people without a country. Then a partial return, a rebuilt wall, a purity crisis
                            about foreign wives, and a queen in a foreign court who never once mentions God.
WISDOM      &   POETRY
                            The interior life of the tradition, and the place where it argues with itself most openly: a
Job–Song of Songs           man on an ash heap accusing God of injustice, a hymnbook that includes rage, a book
                            of orderly proverbs, a book that says order is vapour, and a love poem nobody has ever
                            quite managed to sanitise.
PROPHETS
                            Not fortune-tellers. Political speech, mostly addressed to the powerful, mostly about
Isaiah–Malachi              the treatment of the poor, the widow, the immigrant and the worker, and mostly
                            furious. This is the section every empire is most careful about, and the one your
                            resistance will quote.
