RAHAB · RUTH
Two foreign women who talk their way into the royal genealogy


RAHAB                      Reduced to: her profession, permanently. She is almost never named without it.
Joshua 2 · 6
                           The text: she runs a house on the city wall, which means she hears everything and
                           answers to no husband. When two spies arrive she hides them, lies to the king's men to
                           their faces, and then delivers a political assessment more accurate than anything her own
                           government has produced — she knows the war is lost and says so. Then she negotiates:
                           my whole household, in writing, or no deal. She gets it. The wall falls and her house is the
                           one thing left standing.
                           Dominion: useful only as a conversion story — the fallen woman who chose correctly.
                           Never as a woman who conducted foreign policy from a brothel.
                           TKC: the informant. A woman whose survival depends on knowing which way a war is
                           going before the men in charge do. Breaks: Rahab's intelligence is believed. Ask whether
                           yours would be.

RUTH                       Reduced to: a wedding reading. Loyal, gentle, rewarded with a husband.
Ruth 1–4
                           The text: a Moabite widow with no obligation to anyone, who binds herself to her
                           mother-in-law in the most famous vow of devotion in scripture — spoken by a woman,
                           to a woman. She then works a eld as a foreign labourer under legal protections she is
                           only just entitled to, and on Naomi's explicit coaching goes to a man at night on the
                           threshing oor and asks him, directly, to marry her. She is the one who proposes. The
                           book calls it loyalty, not scandal.
                           Dominion: the model immigrant — the outsider who assimilates completely and is
                           rewarded. The threshing oor is retold as modesty; the vow is reassigned to a husband.
                           TKC: two women running a survival strategy together, with the older one designing it.
                           Breaks: Ruth's gamble works because one man chooses to honour a law he could have
                           ignored.
