Genesis 3.16                     Made into a constitution for the household. Actually a description of consequence in a
                                 poem about exile — a curse, not a command, and read as prescription only by choosing to.
Romans 13.1                      Made into total obedience to the state. Written by a man repeatedly imprisoned by that
                                 state, in a collection full of prophets defying kings and apostles refusing magistrates.
Ephesians 5.22                   Made into household law. Sits directly under an instruction for everyone to submit to one
                                 another, and above a passage three times longer demanding self-sacri ce from husbands.
1 Timothy 2.11–12                Made into a permanent ban on women teaching. One sentence, in the most disputed letter
                                 in the collection, contradicted by named women teaching elsewhere in the same book.
Deuteronomy 28                   Made into a national scoreboard: prosperity proves virtue, disaster proves someone's sin.
                                 The engine of every purge, and the thing Job exists to contradict.
Joshua 7                         Made into the case for collective punishment — one household's disobedience endangers
                                 the nation, therefore surveillance is a mercy and informing is a duty.
Numbers 16                       Made into the standing sermon against dissent: the ground opens under people who
                                 question ordained leadership. Preached annually. Zelophehad's daughters, never.
Judges 21.25                     Made into the founding slogan: without a divinely ordered ruler, everyone does as they
                                 please and women pay for it. Order as the alternative to atrocity.
2 Samuel 7                       Made into the eternal dynasty clause — the legal basis of the throne itself, and the reason
                                 the founder's crimes are a matter for private repentance rather than public account.
