It is not a devotional and it is not a study guide. Nothing in here asks you to
arrive at a settled conclusion about God, and nothing in here will hand you a
single meaning for a passage that has been fought over for two thousand years.
Where a reading is contested, the contest is printed. Where the text is stranger
or more violent or more tender than its reputation, that is printed too. You are
not being catechized; you are being handed summaries, analysis, references
and tools that send you back to the text itself.
Neither is it a character worksheet with the serial numbers led o. The
character sections do not ask what Eden's favorite color is. They ask what she
believes God wants from her, who taught her that, what it would cost her to
be wrong, and which chapter is where the answer changes. Those questions
are the same ones the scripture sections have been rehearsing all along, which
is the point of putting them in one volume.
Above all it is a book you are permitted to ruin. Write in the margins, argue
with the entries, cross out anything the drafting proves false, and date the page
when you do. The manuscript is the authority here, and this companion
serves it. When the two disagree, the manuscript wins and this book gets
amended.
