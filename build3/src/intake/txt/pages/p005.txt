STATUS AND SOURCE
The signals on the facing page tell you what kind of material you are reading. These two systems tell you
something dierent and equally necessary: how settled a claim is, and where it came from. All three run at
once and none of them substitutes for another.

I    ·       CL AIM   S TAT U S   —   INLINE,   IN    THE   SENTENCE    ITSELF



[C]                  Canon. Established by the manuscript or by an approved worldbuilding source, and logged.
                     Nothing is canon because it is good; it is canon because it is logged.
[P]                  Possible. A working idea or reading of your own that has not been approved. Freewriting lands
                     here by default and stays here until you move it.
[?]                  Question. Something the manuscript and the companion have not answered, named as open
                     rather than left to look like an answer.
[R]                  Research. A claim needing biblical, historical, linguistic, or worldbuilding veri cation before it
                     is used as evidence.

II       ·    SOURCE     N O TAT I O N   —   OUTER     MARGIN,     NEVER   INLINE



The outer margin column carries provenance and nothing else: where a statement on that page comes from.
Margins are mirrored, so the column is always on the outside edge — left on a left-hand page, right on a
right-hand page. A status marker is not provenance; a claim can be Canon and still owe you a source.
BIBLICAL
                                         Book, chapter, and verse — Gen 3.16, Eph 5.21–33. The passage the page is
                                         actually about.
MANUSCRIPT
                                         The witness at issue — Sinaiticus, Vaticanus, Masoretic Text, Septuagint, Dead Sea
                                         Scrolls, or a translation's own textual footnote.
LEXICON
                                         Where a word's range is the question — a standard Greek or Hebrew lexicon, cited
                                         by name, never a word-study memory.
C O M M E N TA R Y
                                         A named commentary or critical study standing behind a reading, so the reading
                                         can be traced and checked.
H I S TO R I C A L
                                         Non-biblical evidence — a canon list, a council, a Roman household code, an
                                         ancient Near Eastern parallel.
T KC         SOURCE
                                         Your own les, tagged as they are in the ledger — timeline, Dominions, Trials, the
                                         men, the manuscript, a draft.
✥ A page with a status marker and no source is un nished. Say where it came from, or mark it [R] and move on.
