FORMS INSIDE THE FORMS
Eight genres is the map. These are the units a passage is actually built from, and naming the unit is usually enough to
settle what it can be used for.
 Genealogy               A claim about belonging and legitimacy. Who is included is the argument — Matthew's four women are the
                         whole point of Matthew's list.
 Lament                  Structured complaint, often addressed to God and often unresolved. Its presence in the canon means protest
                         is a sanctioned form of speech.
 Hymn                    Communal praise, often older than the book it sits in. Frequently quoted material — which means it was
                         already familiar.
 Proverb                 A compressed observation. Reliable as a pattern, dangerous as a promise, and lethal as a rebuke to someone
                         suering.
 Parable                 A story built to reposition the hearer. Usually has one hinge; allegorizing every detail is how it gets defused.
 Oracle                  A delivered message with a named addressee, often a foreign nation. Reading one addressed to Babylon as
                         addressed to you is a category error with a long history.
 Legal code              Case law with penalties attached, comparable to other ancient Near Eastern codes. Comparison is where the
                         distinctive parts show.
 Court history           Institutional record written near power. Ask who commissioned it and what it does not mention.

   THE    GENRE   TEST    —    RUN    IT   BEFORE   QUOTING         ANYTHING


   1. What kind of writing is this?                                     4. What is it incapable of establishing?
   2. What is this kind of writing for?                                 5. Am I using it for the thing it can do?
   3. What can it establish?                                            6. If not — is the misuse mine, or my character's?
