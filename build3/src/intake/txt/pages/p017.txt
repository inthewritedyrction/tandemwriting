THE HOUSEHOLD VERSE
A   W O R K E D      E X A M P L E




Take the passage every scriptural empire in history has built its household law on: the instruction to wives
in the letter to Ephesus. Run the eight questions and watch how much of the standard use depends on
stopping at question one.
    "Wives, submit to your husbands" — the fragment as it appears on a wall, in a schoolbook, in a wedding
    rite, and in every Ministry pamphlet.

                                        A letter. Occasional correspondence to one congregation, not statute.
THE   SENTENCE        BEFORE
                                        An instruction to the entire assembly to submit to one another.
                                        Grammatically, the wives clause hangs o it — in the earliest Greek it does not
                                        even carry its own verb. Cut that line and you convert a mutual instruction

THE   PA S S A G E   AFTER
                                        Roughly three times as long, addressed to husbands, and demanding self-
                                        sacri ce to the point of death. The asymmetry runs the opposite way to the one
                                        usually preached.
THE   WORLD
                                        Roman household codes were a standard literary form, and they were far
                                        harsher. Read against its own context the passage is a modi cation of an
                                        existing hierarchy, not the invention of one — which is exactly the fact an
                                        empire must suppress to present it as timeless.
THE   WORDS
                                        The verb rendered submit is voluntary in form, not imperative in the way
                                        English suggests, and the word for head carries a contested range that
                                        translators resolve according to conviction.
WHO    GAINS
                                        Whoever is permitted to quote the fragment and forbidden to read the frame.
                                        That permission is an administrative decision, and administrative decisions
                                        have names attached.
