THE SIXTY-SIX BOOKS
One entry per book, in canonical order, and every entry answers the same six questions so you can compare
across the whole library instead of reading each book in isolation. None of these is a substitute for reading the
book itself. They are orientation — enough to know what you are walking into, what the book argues about,
and which of its pressures your novel can use.
Two of the six questions are yours rather than the Bible's. What it gives TKC is where the raw material gets
named, and what Dominion would do with it is where you decide how the Empire has already processed the
book before any character opens it. Answer those in pencil; they will change as the manuscript changes.
The narrative-heavy books get a full page each, because story is where characters come from and you will
return to them constantly. The shorter prophetic and epistolary books share pages, two to a spread, because
what you need from them is a fast, reliable sense of the argument rather than a scene inventory. The layout is
telling you how much material is in there.
