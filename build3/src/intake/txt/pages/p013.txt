THE SHAPE OF THE NEW
TESTAMENT
Twenty-seven documents, all written inside the Roman Empire by members of a movement with no political
power whatsoever, most of them within about seventy years of a public execution. That situation shapes
everything, including the parts your Empire will most want to use.
GOSPELS
                              Four accounts of one life, and they do not match, which is the most useful thing about
Matthew–John                  them. Mark is urgent and bleak, Matthew is arguing that this is the ful lment of the
                              Law, Luke keeps putting women and poor people in the frame, and John is doing
                              theology at high altitude. Read them side by side and you are watching four
                              communities decide what mattered.
ACTS
                              A small, illegal, property-sharing sect argues its way across the Mediterranean, mostly in
the movement spreads          rented rooms and courtrooms. Worth reading closely for how a movement without
                              institutions makes decisions, and who ends up hosting it: repeatedly, women with
                              houses.
PA U L' S   LETTERS
                              Occasional correspondence, not a systematic theology, and this single fact undoes half
Romans–Philemon               of what gets built on them. Each letter is one side of a conversation with a speci c
                              congregation in a speci c crisis. When a verse is lifted out of that, you are quoting an
                              answer while suppressing the question.
GENERAL       LETTERS
                              Written to scattered communities under pressure. James is blunt about the rich in a way
Hebrews–Jude                  no comfortable institution enjoys reading aloud, and the letters attributed to Peter are
                              largely about how to live faithfully while powerless — a genre your Empire cannot use
                              and your rebels can.
R E V E L AT I O N
                              Resistance literature in code, written for people being persecuted by an empire that
apocalyptic                   called itself divine. The beast is a state, the whore is a capital city, and the whole book is
                              a claim that Rome will not last. Read it as prophecy of the far future and you lose the
                              plot; read it as a banned pamphlet and it becomes the most politically dangerous text in
                              the collection.
    THE     CENTRAL   IRONY   FOR   T KC


    Every document here was written by people with no power, about a man the state executed, for readers the state
    despised. Dominion runs its theology on literature composed by the losing side — the raw material for its own
    undoing is bound into the volume it hands out at induction.
