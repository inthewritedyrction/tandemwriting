 STRUCTURE
                              Nine chapters of genealogy. Then David, at length. Then Solomon and the temple.
                              Then the kings of Judah only, down to the exile, closing with the decree of Cyrus. The
                              northern kingdom is largely not its subject. [C]                                                 T KC : D O M




 ITS SOURCES
                              It draws on Samuel and Kings, often nearly word for word, and also cites records the
                              reader cannot check. It is a book openly built out of other books — which makes it the
                              Bible’s clearest case study in how a tradition edits itself. [C]
 ITS PURPOSE
                              Theological, liturgical, and genealogical, addressed to a community after the exile that
                              needed to know it was continuous with what came before. Framings like “the
                              authorized version” or “state-sponsored scripture” are interpretations of that purpose,
                              not descriptions of it. Use them as arguments you are making, never as facts you are
                              reporting. [P]

W H AT   THE   BOOK    IS   A C T U A L LY   DOING


Proving that a small, occupied province with a rebuilt temple is the same people as the kingdom in the old
stories. Continuity is the argument; the genealogies are the evidence; the temple is the proof. [P]
