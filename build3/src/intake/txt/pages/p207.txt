 THE CAPTIVE WOMAN                  Chapter 21 regulates taking a captive woman as a wife: a month of mourning, a shaved
                                    head, and if she is later rejected she must be freed rather than sold. It is a restriction on a
                                    practice it permits. Whether that is mitigation or legitimation is a genuine dispute, and
                                    both readings are serious. [R]
 DID IT HAPPEN                      Archaeology does not support a sweeping military conquest of the kind Joshua narrates,
                                    and many scholars read these commands as retrospective ideology written long after,
                                    aimed at internal loyalty rather than at actual campaigns. That reading explains the text’s
                                    origin; it does not soften the words on the page. This is Contested, and the companion
                                    prints the dispute without a verdict. [R]
