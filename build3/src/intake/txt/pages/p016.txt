HOW TO READ A PASSAGE IN
CONTEXT
Eight questions, in this order, every time. Run them and you will be reading better than most people who
quote this book professionally, and you will be able to write clergy who are genuinely sophisticated rather
than merely sinister.

I       W H AT   KIND    OF   TEXT           Name the genre before anything else. Law, story, poem, letter, oracle,
                                             apocalypse. This decides what kind of claim it can even make.

II      WHO      IS   SPEAKING               The narrator, a character, God quoted by a character, an opponent
                                             being paraphrased, or a proverb the author is about to demolish. Job's
                                             friends get pages of con dent theology that the book itself rejects.

III     TO   WHOM                            A named congregation, a king, a nation, an enslaved population, a
                                             household. Instructions to the powerful read very dierently when
                                             redirected at the powerless, and that redirection is the most common
                                             abuse there is.

IV      W H AT   COMES     BEFORE            Read the paragraph on either side, then the chapter, then skim the
                                             whole book's argument. A verse used as a wall inscription almost
                                             always has a sentence beside it that complicates it.

V       W H AT   WORLD                       The economy, the legal status of women and slaves, who owned land,
                                             what an honour culture punished. Not to excuse the text, but to see
                                             what it was arguing with — sometimes it is more radical than its
                                             context, sometimes far less.

VI      W H AT   THE    WORDS    DO          Check a second translation on any word carrying weight. If two
                                             respectable versions disagree, you have found a decision, not a fact.

VII     WHO      GAINS                       Ask it of the passage and of every reading of it, including the ones you
                                              nd sympathetic. Interpretations have bene ciaries.

VIII    W H AT   IT   COSTS                  Who has to absorb the consequence if this reading is enforced. That
                                             answer usually names the character in your novel who lives inside the
                                             doctrine.
