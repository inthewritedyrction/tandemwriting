               THE    SYMBOLIC        PERFORMANCES                  ·   4–5,    12


               He lies on one side, then the other, for a counted number of days. He eats a measured siege ration. He shaves his
               head and beard and divides the hair. He digs through a wall and carries out a packed bag at dusk like a refugee.
               Public, humiliating, sustained — performance art commissioned as prophecy. [C]
               THE    D E AT H   OF   HIS     WIFE     ·   24:15–27


               She is called the delight of his eyes. He is told in advance that she will die, and told not to mourn her publicly — no
               weeping, no rites — because his withheld grief is the sign of what is coming to the city. She dies; he does as
               instructed. The text names her only by his relation to her, gives her no words, and uses her death as an illustration.
               Whatever else this book is, it is a book that does that. [C]
