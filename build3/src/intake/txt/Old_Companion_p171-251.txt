HOW TO READ A PERSON
Every pro le in this part runs the same six elds, and the order matters. It starts with what the person has
been reduced to, because that reduction is what you inherited and it will keep steering your instincts until it is
written down and looked at. Then the text itself — only what is on the page, including the parts
inconvenient for everyone.
Then the Empire's use of them, because Dominion has a position on each of these people, and a state that
quotes scripture is always also curating a gallery of approved and cautionary lives. Then the echoes in your
own cast, then — most importantly — where the parallel breaks, because that break is where your character
stops being a reference and starts being a person.

WHAT HE MEANT BY KINGDOM
Start with the word itself, because everything turns on it. He used a political term — kingdom, the same word
used for the empire that occupied his country — and he used it constantly, more than any other phrase. He
was not inventing a private spiritual category. He was making a claim about who actually governs, in a
province where saying that got people cruci ed in rows along the roads.
But the content he poured into that political word is the opposite of what the word normally carries. His
kingdom is described as small, hidden, and growing without permission: a seed, yeast in dough, treasure
buried in a eld. It belongs, he says, to the poor, the grieving, the pushed-around. Entry is granted to
children, foreigners, tax collectors and the visibly disreputable, and it is described as extremely dicult for the
rich — the one demographic every state religion is funded by.
Its politics are inverted on purpose. When his followers argue about rank he tells them the rulers of the
nations lord it over people and it will not be like that among them; whoever wants to be rst goes last. He
washes their feet, which is slave work, and tells them to do the same. He refuses a crown when it is oered,
evades arrest until he chooses not to, and enters the capital on a donkey in what is plainly a staged parody of a

EMPIRES OF THE BIBLE
Five occupations, and what each one wanted from the people it held

Almost the entire Bible is written by people living under somebody else's government. Read the empires as a
menu of methods, because Dominion will be using some combination of them.

EGYPT                     Wants bodies. It extracts work, controls reproduction by decree, and manages unrest by
forced labour             increasing quotas rather than negotiating. Its method is exhaustion — people too tired to
                          organise. The model for a state that governs through the working day.

ASSYRIA                   Wants submission, and advertises the cost of refusing it. Its speciality is population transfer —
terror & deportation      break a people apart, resettle them among strangers, and the identity dissolves within two
                          generations. It sends ocials to shout propaganda at city walls in the local language. Terror as
                          published policy.

BABYLON                   Wants the top of the society, not the bottom. It deports the educated, trains them in its own
elite capture             bureaucracy, renames them, and hands them careers — which is far cheaper than terror and
                          produces administrators who cannot tell whether they were saved or absorbed. The Daniel
                          problem. Your Ministry runs this scheme.

PERSIA                    Wants order, and nds tolerance cheaper than suppression. It funds the rebuilding of the temple,
sponsored religion        licenses local worship, and issues irrevocable decrees — so its violence is procedural rather than
                          passionate. It also runs a court where women are gathered by search and a queen is removed by
                          legislation. An empire whose scripture-friendliness is a governing technique.

ROME                      Wants taxes and quiet, and governs through local clients who do the unpopular work — a client
collaboration &           king, a compliant priesthood, a governor who signs orders he disagrees with. Its executions are
spectacle                 public, slow, and positioned on roads. It calls its emperor divine and its violence peace. The full
                          model: religion, bureaucracy and theatre in one system.
   THE    COMPOSITE       FOR   DOMINION


   Dominion is Babylon's elite capture plus Persia's licensed religion plus Rome's client collaboration — the three
   methods that require the fewest soldiers. Terror is expensive; a career path is not.

WHO GETS TO SAY WHAT IT
MEANS
Authority over a text is never a single thing. It is a set of separate powers, usually held by dierent people, and
a religious state becomes total when one institution collects all of them. Work out which of the seven below
Dominion holds, because the ones it does not hold are where your plot lives.
THE   CANON
                                       Which books count. Usually settled long ago, and therefore invisible.
THE   T R A N S L AT I O N
                                       Which words. The quietest and most powerful lever there is.
THE   LECTIONARY
                                       Which passages are read aloud, on which days, in which order. Most people's entire
                                       scripture is whatever the calendar allows.
THE   C O M M E N TA R Y
                                       What the passage is taken to mean. Handed down as though it were the text.
THE   PULPIT
                                       Who may stand up and speak. The licence, and the withdrawal of it.
THE   COPY
                                       Who owns a physical book, and which edition. Access is doctrine.
LITERACY
                                       Who can read it unaided. The oldest control of all, and the cheapest.
