## ===== 249 · HOW A DOSSIER WORKS =====
 THE CHARACTERS  ·  HOW A DOSSIER WORKS
 HOW A DOSSIER WORKS
 Every dossier opens with a portrait page, then runs in the same order, so you can read across sixteen people and see where the Empire holds and where it is already coming apart. The first nine pages settle who she is on the record. The last seven are where you write. Fill them out of order if you like. A blank line is not a failure; it is a question the manuscript has not answered yet.
 Three conventions. Anything your files already establish is printed , with a small tag naming the source: TL timeline, TR trials, DR3 draft three, DOM dominions, MEN the men. Anything reasoned rather than recorded carries INFERRED — cross it out and write over it, that is what it is there for. Ruled space means nobody has decided yet, including me.
 1 NAME & BIRTH 
Name of record, its meaning and root, who chose it, every alias, and the vitals. 
 2 STANDING 
House, office, what a clerk would file her as, and what she actually does all day. 
 3 CREED 
What she can recite, what she trusts when frightened, what she'd be arrested for. 
 4 MIRRORS 
Three biblical doubles from Volume One — and where each parallel breaks. 
 5 BODY & FEAR 
What the doctrine costs her physically. What she is afraid is actually true. 
 6 BELIEF ARC 
Four stages: what she believes, what cracks it, what she does about it. 
 7 POWER & PEOPLE 
Who she needs, who she can ruin, who can ruin her, who she prays for. 
 8 VOICE 
Lines only she would say, and the words she will not use. Ruled and blank. 
 9 CONTRADICTION 
The thing she believes and violates. The cost. The scene where it lands. 
 10 THE MACHINERY 
Every system pressing on her, on one page, so you never stop drafting to look. 
 11 – 16 THE WORKING PAGES 
Excavation of the scene that made her · ten minutes of her at home with no plot in it · one relationship written from both sides · the scene lab, where a belief meets someone who wants something else · and the spread: what she already does on the page, and where it repeats, costs more, or reverses. Photocopy any of these before filling them; second and third drafts are expected. 
 THE ONE RULE
 No character gets a belief she has never paid for. If you write a conviction onto the creed page, the contradiction page has to name what it cost her — or the conviction is decoration.
 "Write the version she would deny under oath. That one is usually the true one."

## ===== 261 · EDEN · 11 OF 17 · THE MACHINERY AROUND HER =====
 EDEN  ·  11 OF 17 · THE MACHINERY AROUND HER
 THE MACHINERY AROUND HER
 Six systems are pressing on Eden before she says a word in Chapter One. You built all six; this page holds them together so you never have to stop drafting to go and look. Nothing here asks you to write — the next four pages do. Read it, then turn the page while it is still loud.
 THE DESIGNATION, AND THE FLAG TL · DOM 
 At eighteen every Aurem girl is assessed by a Vowed officer and sorted — Unbound track or elevated track — before she has a say in it. Eden scored the highest in the Dominion's history and was flagged the same day: Exceptional intellect. Monitor closely. Pressure: she has been watched by name for three years. Every room she enters, she enters as a file that is already open.
 SELF-NOMINATION — SIX MONTHS AND TEN DOLLARS TR · DR3 
 The clause is real and the process is built to exhaust anyone without institutional backing. She asked Minister Bell to sponsor her first; he asked about her father, asked whether her mother was properly Bound, and prayed that God would close every door not intended for her. She found the clause in small print, paid the fee while the clerk inspected both sides of every bill, and filed six months of paperwork at Celeste's kitchen table. Pressure: the Empire will read her ambition as pride the moment it stops being useful.
 THE SALT CEREMONY, AND CELESTE'S THEOLOGY DOM · TR 
 The night before a Binding, an Aurem grandmother washes the bride's hands in salt water and tells her the truth about marriage: what to hold, what to hide, what not to hand over just because somebody asks. The Empire does not know the ceremony exists. Celeste performed it for Miriam. It did not save her; it gave her something to hold. Pressure: Eden's faith was formed at a kitchen table by a woman who teaches that power is survivable if you keep part of yourself unregistered — and Eden intends to test that at Imperial scale.
 SOLOMON, AND THE DOOR THAT ONLY OPENS ONCE MEN 
 When a common boy's scores come back exceptional, a Crown education officer appears within three months with a scholarship the family may decline — and declining routes him to the worst labor assignments for life. Accepting transfers his autonomy permanently. Solomon's theological reasoning is extraordinary; two informal notes are already filed. Celeste is coaching him on how much of himself to show. Pressure: Eden nominated herself partly to be standing somewhere useful before that officer knocks. She does not know about the notes.
 LEAH, UNDER THE QUOTA MEN · TL 
 Nearly two years a Commonwife with no pregnancy. The Vowed officer who runs the quarterly household assessment has made an informal comment; the husband has made the same one less carefully. Leah comes to Celeste's kitchen twice a week under cover of helping with Solomon, and watches Eden's Covenant Score move on the Sovereign Boulevard screens every evening. Pressure: Eden's win is the only exit anyone in that family can see, and Leah is the reason the clock is running.
 THE EVE PROBLEM TR 
 The Broadcast holds forty percent of the Covenant Score and assigns an antagonist narrative to whichever candidate most threatens a comfortable outcome. Internally they call it the Eve problem: when a woman's intelligence becomes the story instead of her compliance. It does not accuse. It implies. It has worked every previous cycle. Pressure: the thing that got Eden to the Crownward is the thing they will use to take it back.
 Do not answer anything here. The next pages will ask you for one thing at a time and give you the room to be wrong twice before you are right.
