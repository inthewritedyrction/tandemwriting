## ===== 271 · CELESTE · 3 STANDING =====
 CELESTE  ·  3 OF 17 · STANDING
 STANDING
 Two answers to every question here: the clerk's and the true one. Both are printed where your files supply them. The distance between the columns is the whole of this woman.
 ON THE LEDGER
 Unbound, sixty-nine, head of a single-family household in Aurelia's outer district. Never Bound, so never widowed in law. Guardian of record for Eden Selah Osei since 2085. A woman with no husband, no office and no standing beyond what the reclassification granted her in writing. TL 
 IN FACT
 She sits in the row reserved for widows at Temple, and nobody has ever asked her to move. Minister Bell has known her thirty-four years, eaten her sweet potato pie at harvest suppers, and called every winter when the Temple needed coats repaired. He greets her with both hands extended. She answers morning and he keeps smiling, because he knows exactly what she did not say. DR3 
 THE SIX-YEAR FILING
 When Miriam left in 2090 she filed to reclassify the household under her own registration. It took six years to process, because approval required proving the arrangement was permanent — which meant proving on paper that her daughter was not coming back. She put on her one good blouse, went downtown, said what she needed to say, and did not speak for three days after it was approved. TL 
 WHAT SHE SIGNS NOW
 Solomon's vocational consideration form came to the household demanding an adult male authority. Celeste wrote deceased across the appropriate line. It came back with the word circled and a request for the next adult male. Zion, eighteen, qualified. She had not wanted him to sign. He signed anyway. DR3 
 WHAT THIS HOUSEHOLD LIVES ON — WHO EARNS, WHAT IT COVERS, WHAT IT DOESN'T
 THE CANE
 She did not need it last summer. This year she claims people move faster when you carry one. Both reasons are true and she will only ever give you the second. DR3 
 THE COUNTING
 She prays with her thumb against her first knuckle, one tap at the end of each petition. One for mercy. Two for favor. Eden has been reading that hand her whole life. DR3

## ===== 272 · CELESTE · 3 CREED =====
 CELESTE  ·  4 OF 17 · CREED
 CREED
 She is the most religious person in this book and the least Imperial. Those are not in tension for her, and the pages where a reader feels that is the whole engine of the Parish house. What she can recite, what she actually trusts, and what she would be arrested for are three different documents.
 WHAT SHE CAN RECITE
 All of it. Sixty years of Temple attendance, harvest suppers, coat drives, and the widows' row. She knows the approved hymns, the Covenant of Purpose, and every phrase a minister uses when he is about to say no. Fluency is her camouflage. INFERRED 
 WHAT SHE ACTUALLY TRUSTS WHEN FRIGHTENED
 Her own hands. She prays continuously — from the moment they leave the house — and keeps her hands busy so they will not shake. When Eden paid the ten dollars, Celeste did not pray about it. She cleared a shelf. DR3 · TR 
 WHAT SHE WOULD BE ARRESTED FOR
 Three things, all of them ordinary in her house. The Salt Ceremony, which the Empire does not know exists. Burying her dead the old way — red Georgia earth, family-led, no Empire officiating. And the real name of the city, which she says out loud to her granddaughter the night before the Crownward. DOM · TL 
 HER ONE DOCTRINE, STATED PLAINLY
 Keep part of yourself unregistered. That is what the salt water is for, what the second name is for, what morning is instead of an answer. She taught Eden a faith that can survive an institution by not handing it everything. Eden has decided to test that theology at Imperial scale, which Celeste never intended. INFERRED 
 THE ONE PIECE OF IMPERIAL DOCTRINE SHE HALF BELIEVES, AND HATES THAT SHE DOES
 THE SALT CEREMONY
 The night before a Binding, an older woman washes the bride's hands in salt water and tells her the truth about marriage: what to hold, what to hide, what not to hand over just because somebody asks. Celeste performed it for Miriam. It did not save her. It gave her something to hold. DOM 
 THE OLD WAY
 Aurem's outer districts bury their dead family-led, in red earth, with no Empire officiating. This is one of the places the Empire lost completely and still has no idea. DOM

 MIRRORS

 RIZPAH — 2 SAMUEL 21 INFERRED 
 The parallel. A woman who guards bodies the state has decided do not require dignity, and who will not leave until they are buried right. That is the old way in Aurem's outer districts, and Celeste has done it three times. Where it breaks. Rizpah's vigil is public and it shames a king into acting. Celeste's is quiet, family-led, and specifically arranged so that no king ever hears of it.

## ===== 275 · CELESTE · 6 BELIEF ARC =====
 CELESTE  ·  7 OF 17 · BELIEF ARC
 BELIEF ARC
 Four stages. The first two are printed from your files — she has already lived them. The last two are the book, and they are yours: the oldest person in the household is the one most likely to be asked to change her mind, and she has the least practice at it.
 I · WHAT SHE BELIEVED AT THE START TL 
 That refusing the Empire's terms was survivable if you were careful. She stayed Unbound, raised Miriam alone under a designation that was harder and surveilled, and moved between Dominions for years rather than settle where a Binding could find her. She was right for thirty-five years.
 II · WHAT CRACKED IT TL 
 Miriam. Raised by a woman who refused every term the Empire offered, she took the first Binding she was offered at twenty and believed she loved him — and she did. Within four years she was unrecognizable to her own mother. The Salt Ceremony did not save her. Celeste's method failed on the one person it was built for.

 THE MACHINERY AROUND HER
 Five systems pressing on a sixty-nine-year-old woman with no legal standing. Read it, then turn the page while it is still loud. Nothing here asks you to write.
 THE HOUSEHOLD AUTHORITY LINE DR3 
 Every form that matters demands an adult male. She wrote deceased ; it came back with the word circled. Pressure: her authority is real inside the house and evaporates at the counter, and the Empire will keep handing her paper that says so until Zion signs it.
 SOLOMON'S SCORES MEN 
 When a common boy's scores come back exceptional, a Crown education officer appears within three months with a scholarship the family may decline — and declining routes him to the worst labor for life. Two informal notes are already filed. Pressure: she is coaching a thirteen-year-old on how much of his own mind to show, and the clock is not hers.
 LEAH'S QUARTERLY ASSESSMENT MEN · TL 
 Nearly two years a Commonwife with no pregnancy, under Temple review, with a Vowed officer making informal comments and a husband making them less carefully. Leah comes to the kitchen twice a week under cover of helping with Solomon. Pressure: Celeste can feed her and cannot protect her, and both women know which one is happening.
 THE TEMPLE SHE ATTENDS ANYWAY DR3 
 Thirty-four years of harvest suppers and repaired coats bought her a seat in the widows' row and no sponsorship. Bell prayed that God would close every door not intended for her family. Pressure: the institution that knows her best is the one that priced her most accurately.
 WHAT AUREM DOES TO WOMEN LIKE HER DOM 
 Highest Commonwife rate in the Empire, the most elaborate marriage ceremonies, and the most watched Dominion on the Broadcast. Marriage was made to look like the highest honor available here on purpose. Pressure: staying Unbound in Aurem is not a private choice, it is a public statement she has been making for fifty years.
