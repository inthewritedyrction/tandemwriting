  Eden's story. The people and the chapters belong to Volume Two. When a fact about the

  working it out here.

  hand before you build chapters. They're written from your source files, your Final Rulings
  and the rulings you made in the calibration doc. Parts I through V, the scripture research,

  Part VI. The Fall of the United States
  The United States became the Covenant Empire of America over about eighteen years. It
  started with a stadium sermon in 2033 and ended in 2051, when the Crown, the Temple
  and the Broadcast were written into the constitution as the three branches of
  government. Almost every step went through a vote, a referendum or an agreement (TL).
  Nobody in Eden's lifetime remembers the country before. The oldest person in her cast
  was born around 2035, into its last few years.

  VI.3 The Restoration Act
  The Restoration Act of 2041 is the legal root of almost everything in Eden's life. The
  timeline gives four things it did (TL).

       Rights become conditional. They depend on standing, compliance and designation.
       Marriage becomes a legal and theological designation. The Binding, the Commonwife
       quota and the Anointed marriage all descend from this line.
       Women's citizenship is reclassified as dependent on marital status. This is why
       Unbound is a legal category with a file and not a description of someone's life.
       The press is "reorganized for accuracy and unity." Ten years later the same apparatus
       is a branch of government, and today it controls forty percent of a Covenant
       Daughter's score.

  The act passed Congress, so the Empire's founding argument is that nothing illegal
  happened (TL).

  OPEN QUESTION: What does the Act's preamble say? You'll want the actual wording for
  any scene where a character quotes the Restoration.

  Part VII. The Covenant Empire: government and institutions
  The Empire is run by three constitutional bodies: the Crown, the Temple and the
  Broadcast. That arrangement is the Triune Rule of 2051 (TL). Underneath them sit twelve
  Dominions, each under a Governor-Prelate, along with a Registry that holds everyone's
  paper and a Vowed order that watches everyone's daughters. This part covers how those
  pieces fit together and where each one shows up in the book.


  VII.1 The Triune Rule
  In 2051 the Crown, the Temple and the Broadcast were made the three governing bodies
  of the Empire. What had worked informally since 2043 became constitutional, and the
  Broadcast stopped being a network of Coalition media companies and became a branch
  of government (TL).

  The three bodies split the Covenant Trials between them. The Temple runs Phase One and
  the Crown runs Phase Two. Endurance belongs to the terrain. The Covenant Score is
  weighted 30 percent Temple, 30 percent Crown and 40 percent Broadcast (TR). The
  Broadcast's share is never disclosed, because the public would be angry to learn that a
  broadcaster outweighs the Church in choosing the Empire's most powerful woman (TR).

  VII.3 The Crownward
  The Crownward is the capital, built over Washington, D.C. and formally established in
  2043. Elias chose to occupy the old capital instead of moving it, because you don't
  abandon the seat of the thing you claim to restore (CR). Most people in 2104 have never
  seen a photograph of Washington (CR).

  The Potomac is now the Covenant River, and it divides the city (CR).

       The Sovereign Side (west bank) is where power lives: white stone, dark iron and
       formal rows of magnolias. The Crown Palace sits at its highest point. The Temple
       Sanctum stands at a right angle to it, joined by a covered walkway called the Covenant
       Bridge. The Broadcast Citadel sits on the northern edge, placed so its cameras can see
       the Palace and the Sanctum at once. Sovereign Boulevard runs half a mile from the
       Palace gates to the river, lined with Broadcast screens that show the Covenant Scores
       during Trial season. Covenant Row runs parallel to it and holds the twelve Covenant
       Houses, one per Dominion.
       The Administrative Side (east bank) is where the Empire does its actual work. It holds
       the Covenant Court's offices, the Crown's administrative apparatus, staff housing, the
       Vowed training facilities, the registration bureaus and the Registry. It also has market
       streets, Temple-adjacent schools and small restaurants serving food from across the
       Dominions.
       Three bridges cross the river. The Sovereign Bridge is the widest and appears on the
       currency. The Covenant Bridge, the river one, is narrower and used by staff. The Grace
       Bridge is the oldest, never fully renovated, and has blind spots in Broadcast coverage,
       so it's where people go when they don't want to be filmed.

  RESEARCH: In the real city, central Washington sits on the Potomac's east bank, and the
  west bank is Arlington, Virginia. Read literally, the Sovereign Side stands where Arlington
  National Cemetery and the Pentagon are now.

  POSSIBLE: Somebody in the Crownward knows the Crown Palace is built over the old
  national cemetery.

  The Crownward has a rigid social order that nobody writes down and everybody enforces
  (CR). The House of Vane is at the top. Below it is the Anointed Court, about forty families
  whose standing is measured by how close they live to the Palace, and who must attend
  quarterly audiences with the Emperor. Below them is the Administrative Class, the senior
  staff of the Crown, Temple and Broadcast, most of whose wives hold Stewardwife
  designation. The Crownward's daughters who enter the Trials are rarely the most
  celebrated candidates, and they're almost always the most dangerous (CR).

  VII.4 The Temple and the Vowed
  The Temple is both a church and a branch of government. In Eden's world it shows up as
  ministers, Vowed women and doctrine. Its scripture is read from a book called the Book of
  Covenant (DR3), and the Temple Sanctum in the Crownward is its seat (CR).

  Ministers. Senior Minister Bell is the model: a man who signs Binding certificates,
  sponsors Assessment candidates, and prays God will close every door not intended for a
  girl (DR3). The Temple High Prelates sit in the Anointed Court (CR).

  The Vowed. These are women in Temple service, and they run most of the Empire's
  contact with women. A Vowed officer assesses every Aurem girl at eighteen (DOM).
  Vowed officers conduct the quarterly household assessments in Aurem's Commonwife
  households (MEN), run the Regional Assessments (TR), and observe, examine and
  interview the Covenant Daughters (TR). The Crownward Vowed are the Temple's elite.
  They're also the Empire's most efficient intelligence network. The Temple calls it spiritual
  discernment and the Crown uses it as surveillance, and it's never written down anywhere
  the Empire could confiscate it (CR). In Red Earth, Vowed officers attend school
  performances as "pastoral care" (DOM).

  The Vowed designation can be left behind through marriage. Delphine's mother went from
  Vowed to Bonded Wife, and Hannah was Vowed before she became House Mother (TR, FR
  30). Sera Callum was Vowed when Elias married her (TL). Girls train through a Vowed
  education program, which Hannah's daughter Naomi has one year left in (TL).

  The Prelate. She heads the Crownward's Vowed. She's 67, has served three Emperors, and
  is the most institutionally knowledgeable person in the city about how power actually
  works. She assessed Judah's spiritual formation personally. In the first week of the Trial
  she'll reach a conclusion about Eden that she shares with no one, the Emperor included
  (CR). She anoints the Crown Heir's bride (CR). The files call her Prelate Naomi, but FR 33
  renames her so Hannah's daughter can keep the name.

  POSSIBLE: "Prelate Aurelia" is the working name until you choose one (FR, closing note).

  VII.7 The Registry, the law and the paperwork
  The Registry, on the Administrative Side, holds every citizen's Imperial record: birth
  designations, marriage registrations, Covenant Scores, Designation results and death
  records. Only the Crown Palace is guarded more heavily. Whoever controls what the
  Registry says about you controls something more basic than law, faith or the Broadcast
  (CR). Eden will understand this before most people in the story (CR).

  Imperial law is interpreted by the Covenant Court. It keeps offices on the Administrative
  Side and secondary chambers in Blessed Shore, where the law "gets interpreted, refined,
  and weaponized" (CR, DOM).

  In daily life the law arrives as forms.

       Household forms demand an adult male authority. Celeste wrote "deceased" on the
       line, and the form came back with the word circled and a request for the next adult
       male (DR3).

  VII.8 Surveillance
  Cameras and Broadcast screens are everywhere in the seat cities. The Covenant Houses
  are wired in every common area, entrance and garden, but not the bedrooms. Leaving
  bedrooms unwatched is a deliberate choice to give candidates the illusion of privacy,
  which keeps them manageable (CR). Pacific has more cameras per block than any other
  Dominion (DOM). Red Earth embeds its Vowed in community life (DOM).

  Records can be flagged. Eden's file reads "Exceptional intellect. Monitor closely," later
  updated to "Selected for Trial. Monitor closely" (TL). Sera's file was flagged before she
  arrived (TL).

  The gaps are just as much a part of the world: the Grace Bridge (CR), the hollows of
  Ardenne (DOM), the Sable desert (DOM), west Lone's distances (DOM), and Saint
  Meridian's density (DOM). Endurance in Mountain terrain runs mostly without cameras
  (TR).

  Part VIII. Doctrine and lived religion
  The Temple's official religion fits in one line. Private religion lives in kitchens, back rooms,
  deserts and mountains, and it's different in every Dominion. This part covers what the
  Temple teaches, what texts it rests on, how it uses scripture, and what people actually
  practice that the Empire doesn't know about or has decided to tolerate.


  VIII.1 What the Temple teaches
  The official theology of marriage is the center of it: "the husband leads, the wife supports,
  the household reflects divine order in miniature." It's preached every week in every
  Temple, taught from the time children can read, and assumed by every law that governs
  women (CR). See Part IX.

  The rest of the official teaching comes through in what the files show the Temple saying.

  The sermon, Thy Kingdom Come (2033). Six hours long, preached by Elias Vane at The

  IX.4 Commonwife marriage and the quotas
  Commonwife marriage is how the Empire marries off the labor class. Aurem has the
  highest Commonwife rate in the Empire, because the Empire deliberately made marriage
  look like the highest honor available there (DOM). The quotas were introduced under
  Crest in 2062 (TL), after a pilot in Red Earth in 2058 (DOM). They're the reason Red Earth
  Bindings are documented in real time (DOM).

  A Commonwife is expected to conceive within the Covenant timeline. In Aurem, a Vowed
  officer conducts quarterly household assessments in Commonwife homes. Two years
  without a pregnancy brings informal comments first and then Temple review (MEN, TL).

  IX.5 Anointed Wives and the Arrangement
  The first Anointed marriage was Elias Vane's to Sera Callum in 2048. Every Trial since has
  tried to manufacture it again on camera (TL, AM). All thirty-nine Trials place Anointed
  wives, and the Crown contest is a layer on top. One woman goes to the heir. The other
  eleven are married down the elite ladder as designated, and nobody goes home
  unassigned (FR 19).

  The elite husbands are chosen by the oversight committee and ranked by standing, not
  performance. A man can't reject the woman assigned to him, but he can decline the
  assignment altogether, which removes him from the ladder. It has happened twice (FR
  28).

  Among the Anointed Court, the official theology of male headship is mostly performance.
  The women run what they call the Arrangement. Wives manage the information,
  relationships and social intelligence that make their husbands' positions work, and they
  surface the right idea in private so their husbands can present it in public. They never take
  credit or contradict their husbands publicly. Their compensation is influence, access and
  the legal protections of the Anointed designation. New Anointed Wives learn it in their
  first year of marriage (CR). Lyris runs the whole network, which she inherited from the
  Anointed Wife who mentored her (CR). Celeste told Eden about the Arrangement before
  she ever left Aurem (CR).

  INFERENCE: Ladies in Waiting, the Trial's third- and fourth-place finishers, stay in the

  Part X. The Twelve Dominions
  The Dominion Act of 2044 reorganized the states into twelve Dominions by region and
  economic function. The borders roughly follow the old state lines but not exactly, since
  some states were absorbed and some were split (TL). Each Dominion has a seat city
  renamed from an American city, its own Governor-Prelate, and a culture that's sometimes
  Imperial on the surface and sometimes all the way through. The rites of passage are

  X.1 Aurem
  Atlanta had Black wealth, Black culture, Black education and Black memory built so deep
  into the city that the Coalition couldn't flatten it without admitting what it was erasing. So
  the Empire dressed it up and called it restoration. The BeltLine became a ceremonial
  promenade lined with statues and Broadcast screens. Historic neighborhoods became
  Stewardwife districts, and Peachtree Street became Covenant Way. Magnolias were
  planted everywhere because they photograph well and rot slowly. Aurelia is the Empire's
  favorite mirror, Atlanta wearing somebody else's clothes, and the most surveilled and
  showcased territory in the Empire (TL).

  It's humid from May through October. Storms leave the streets smelling of pine and wet
  concrete, and the red clay keeps bleeding through the paving. Black culture went deepest
  underground here and survived most stubbornly. Imperial hymns play on Covenant Way,
  and something else plays in the outer districts. Food is the main vessel of memory, and the
  Empire never thought the kitchen was dangerous enough to watch.

  Aurem has the highest Commonwife rate and the most elaborate marriage ceremonies in
  the Empire, on purpose. It's the Empire's favorite Broadcast Dominion, with fifteen
  Anointed Wives across thirty-eight Trials. Its Covenant Daughter gets forty-seven
  seconds on Sovereign Boulevard in the Opening Procession, more than anyone else, and
  every girl in Aurem knows that number before she can read. An Aurem candidate is
  expected to win. The outer districts watch the scores with the intensity of people who
  never see their names on anything. Death is the place where "the Empire lost completely
  and still has no idea" (see VIII.4).


  X.2 Gulf
  New Orleans was never going to be easy. The first Governor-Prelate lasted fourteen
  months and reported the city "resistant to spiritual reorientation." The Empire settled for
  managing it. Port Seraphine is loyal enough for the Broadcast and layered enough
  underground that the Vowed have learned to look away. Hurricane season takes half the
  year, and the city is "built on water and spite." Gulf is the most culturally intact Dominion:
  music survived here, standing in plain sight, because tourism, rebranded as "cultural
  pilgrimage," earns too much to lose. Gulf candidates arrive fluent in being watched, and
  the scoring system has historically penalized them for warmth, which Crown advisors
  read as performance. A Gulf woman who knows the gap between what she shows and
  what she keeps is one of the most dangerous women in the room.

  X.3 Ardenne
  Appalachia was never fully inside America's idea of itself, and it isn't fully inside the
  Empire's. Ashford is the city the Empire keeps as proof that Ardenne is pacified, and
  everything past the tree line says otherwise. It has real winters, hollows that cut

  well on problem-solving tests. Ardenne rarely sends a Covenant Daughter, and when it
  does she's usually from Ashford and quiet about the gap.


  X.4 Meridian
  The Empire took Philadelphia for its symbolism. The city that held the Declaration of
  Independence now holds the legal architecture of everything the Declaration claimed to
  prevent. Blessed Shore houses the Covenant Court's secondary chambers. Meridian
  absorbed Imperial culture more completely than almost anywhere, because its
  administrators trust process. Its underground is made of lawyers and bureaucrats filing
  precise challenges that go nowhere and documenting them for a future someone is
  preparing. Stewardwives here are expert readers of Binding contracts, trained in Reading
  Circles. Meridian candidates know every rule and where the gaps are, which makes one
  who decides to move almost impossible to stop. Corinne Vale is from Blessed Shore (TR).


  X.5 Vane
  New York was the hardest cultural conquest and the one the Empire brags about most. It

  Vowed officers, family members or community leaders can nominate a woman, or she can
  nominate herself. The Empire frames self-nomination as exceptional faith. In practice the
  process is built to exhaust anyone without institutional support, and most self-
  nominations fail for that reason alone (TR). In Aurem, self-nomination is "independent
  submission": a ten-dollar nonrefundable fee and a registration card printed in red instead
  of bordered in gold (DR3). Eden is the first Aurem candidate in the Trials' history to enter
  that way (TL).

  Eligibility isn't entry. A House Mother can submit a name, but she isn't the only person
  who can (FR 27).


  XI.3 The Regional Assessment and selection
  Each Dominion runs a three-day Regional Assessment under senior Vowed officers, which
  produces a ranked list (TR, TL). In Aurem, results are posted in the Temple atrium on the
  third Monday of June at seven in the morning, and the top five are appointed to the
  Covenant. Their photographs hang in the atrium through the summer, and their families
  get the useful kind of formal review, the kind where unpaid levies disappear (DR3). Some
  sections, like Discernment and Spiritual Fitness, are graded by ministerial review, so a girl
  can know every answer and still fail for a lack of humility (DR3).

  The Governor-Prelate chooses the Covenant Daughter from the top three. In practice he
  picks whoever the Crown has quietly signaled it prefers, then documents it as a choice
  from the top three regardless of the real ranking (TR).


  XI.4 Arrival

  Appointed Officers and her House Mother (TR). Each walks Sovereign Boulevard on
  camera. The walk is choreographed, and candidates are told what pace to keep, where to
  look and how to hold their hands (CR).

  XI.5 The Covenant Houses
  Twelve townhouses line Covenant Row, one per Dominion, close enough to walk to
  Sovereign Boulevard in under five minutes. Cameras cover every common area, entrance
  and garden, but not the bedrooms (CR). The candidates aren't sequestered. They're
  embedded in Crownward public life: Temple services, forums, the approved dining rooms,
  the Administrative Side markets (CR).

  Covenant Daughter per Trial, and House staff serve across many Trials. Hannah's "six
  Trials, twelve girls" counts her whole career across several Houses and posts (FR 26).

  XI.7 Scoring
  The Covenant Score is a composite. The weights are never disclosed: Temple 30 percent,
  Crown 30 percent, Broadcast 40 percent. The Broadcast's share includes narrative-
  priority decisions by the Broadcast Director (TR). Scores update on the Sovereign
  Boulevard screens every evening at seven, in a ritual the Empire named the Evening
  Reckoning (TR).

  Every Trial produces a Broadcast antagonist narrative, and it's assigned to the candidate
  who most threatens a comfortable outcome, not to the eventual winner. When a
  candidate's intelligence becomes the story instead of her compliance, the Broadcast calls
  it internally the Eve problem. The narrative implies and never accuses. It moves the
  Covenant Score without changing a single formal assessment, and it has worked every
  time it's been used (TR).


  XI.8 The Declaration and its rules
  After the Endurance rankings are confirmed, the Crown Heir names his Anointed Wife in a
  public ceremony on Sovereign Boulevard, broadcast live in all twelve Dominions (TR). The
  rules around that moment come from your Final Rulings.

       describes the record, not a rule (FR 20).
       What winning means. Being the choice that costs the Crown nothing. The rankings tell
       the heir which selection is free. Deviation is permitted and expensive, and no heir has
       ever paid it (FR 21).
       Refusal. The recommendation stands by default once the Declaration window closes.

       A candidate's death. Her status goes back to her family, with no compensation and no
