 WHERE ALL OF THIS
CAME FROM
 Every page of this companion carries a narrow source margin on its outer edge, printed in compressed form — author, short title. Those compressed forms are keyed to the full citations on the next two pages. Nothing in the margins is a citation; it is a pointer to one.
 [C] CONFIRMED
 The text says this, or the manuscript evidence shows it. You can check it in any study Bible with a critical apparatus.
 [R] ARGUED
 Responsible scholars disagree. The companion prints the dispute and the strongest case on each side, and does not print a verdict.
 [P] POSSIBLE
 A TKC construction — something about Dominion, the Sanctum, or the manuscript, not a claim about scripture. Test it against the manuscript before use.
 [CANON]
 A Possible that has been ruled on and promoted. Fixed for the manuscript until you rule otherwise.
 ON THE SECONDARY LITERATURE
 The works below are standard reference points, not an endorsement of any one position. Where two of them disagree — and several do, sharply — the companion prints the disagreement rather than picking. Several are technical commentaries that assume Hebrew or Greek; you do not need them to use this book. They are listed so that any claim in it can be traced.

 TEXTS AND COMMENTARIES
 PRIMARY TEXT EDITIONS
 MT
 Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia. Stuttgart: Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft. The standard printed Masoretic text; cite the printing you use.
 LXX
 Rahlfs–Hanhart, Septuaginta. Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft. The Greek translation of the Hebrew scriptures; consulted where it differs from MT.
 DSS
 Dead Sea Scroll manuscripts cited by siglum, e.g. 4QDeut. Consulted only where a scroll reading is materially different.
 LEXICA
 BDB
 Brown, Driver and Briggs, A Hebrew and English Lexicon of the Old Testament. Oxford: Clarendon, 1906. Old, free, and still the one most readers can actually get to.
 HALOT
 Koehler and Baumgartner, The Hebrew and Aramaic Lexicon of the Old Testament. Leiden: Brill. The current scholarly standard; use it over BDB where the two conflict.
 SECONDARY LITERATURE CITED IN THE MARGINS
 MILGROM
 Jacob Milgrom, Leviticus. Anchor Bible 3, 3A, 3B. New York: Doubleday, 1991–2001. Three volumes; the standard technical commentary. Margin notes name the volume by chapter range.
 KLAWANS
 Jonathan Klawans, Impurity and Sin in Ancient Judaism. Oxford University Press, 2000. The distinction between ritual and moral impurity that Dossier III leans on.
 DOUGLAS
 Mary Douglas, Purity and Danger. London: Routledge, 1966. Structural reading of the dietary law; she revised her own position later, and the companion says so.
 OLYAN
 Saul M. Olyan, Disability in the Hebrew Bible. Cambridge University Press, 2008. Behind the blemish material on LV11.
 BERGSMA
 John Sietze Bergsma, The Jubilee from Leviticus to Qumran. Leiden: Brill, 2007. The afterlife of Leviticus 25.
 WEINFELD
 Moshe Weinfeld, Deuteronomy 1–11. Anchor Bible 5. Doubleday, 1991; and Deuteronomy and the Deuteronomic School. Oxford: Clarendon, 1972. The treaty-form comparison in Dossier IV.
 LEVINSON
 Bernard M. Levinson, Deuteronomy and the Hermeneutics of Legal Innovation. Oxford University Press, 1997. How Deuteronomy rewrites earlier law while claiming to preserve it.
 GOLDENBERG
 David M. Goldenberg, The Curse of Ham: Race and Slavery in Early Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. Princeton University Press, 2003. The reception history behind Genesis 9.

## ===== S03 · RECTO · NEW TESTAMENT AND COMPARATIVE =====
 SOURCES AND EDITIONS  ·  3 OF 3 · NEW TESTAMENT AND COMPARATIVE
 GREEK NEW TESTAMENT
 NA28
 Novum Testamentum Graece, 28th revised edition. Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, 2012. Where the companion says a reading is absent from the oldest manuscripts, this is the apparatus it means.
 UBS5
 The Greek New Testament, 5th revised edition. United Bible Societies, 2014. Same text as NA28 with a reader-facing apparatus.
 SECONDARY LITERATURE — PARTS III AND IV
 SANDERS
 E. P. Sanders, The Historical Figure of Jesus. London: Penguin, 1993. A restrained account of what the sources can and cannot establish.
 EHRMAN
 Bart D. Ehrman, The New Testament: A Historical Introduction to the Early Christian Writings. Oxford University Press. Revised frequently — record the edition you own, since page references move.
 GOODACRE
 Mark Goodacre, The Case Against Q. Harrisburg: Trinity Press International, 2002. The minority source proposal set out on G03; cited so the majority view is not the only one on the page.
 COMPARATIVE ANCIENT TEXTS
 COS
 William W. Hallo and K. Lawson Younger, eds., The Context of Scripture. Leiden: Brill, 1997–2002. Source for the flood parallels, the Mesha inscription, and the law-code comparisons.
 SAA 2
 Simo Parpola and Kazuko Watanabe, Neo-Assyrian Treaties and Loyalty Oaths. Helsinki University Press, 1988. The succession-treaty curse list compared with Deuteronomy 28.
 GILGAMESH
 Cited from COS or from a standard translation of your choosing. Any recent scholarly edition will serve for the comparison the companion actually makes.
