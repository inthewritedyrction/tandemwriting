HOW A DOSSIER WORKS

Every dossier opens with a portrait page, then runs in the same order, so you can read across sixteen people and see where the Empire holds and where it is already coming apart. The first nine pages settle who she is on the record. The last seven are where you write. Fill them out of order if you like. A blank line is not a failure; it is a question the manuscript has not answered yet.

Three conventions. Anything your files already establish is printed, with a small tag naming the source: TL timeline, TR trials, DR3 draft three, DOM dominions, MEN the men. Anything reasoned rather than recorded carries INFERRED — cross it out and write over it, that is what it is there for. Ruled space means nobody has decided yet, including me.

11–16THE WORKING PAGES
Excavation of the scene that made her · ten minutes of her at home with no plot in it · one relationship written from both sides · the scene lab, where a belief meets someone who wants something else · and the spread: what she already does on the page, and where it repeats, costs more, or reverses. Photocopy any of these before filling them; second and third drafts are expected.
THE ONE RULE

No character gets a belief she has never paid for. If you write a conviction onto the creed page, the contradiction page has to name what it cost her — or the conviction is decoration.

"Write the version she would deny under oath. That one is usually the true one."
