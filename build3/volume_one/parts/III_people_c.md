- **Seven women prophets.** The Talmud's list of seven (Megillah 14a) includes Miriam, Hannah and Abigail. Eden's mother, her House Mother and her protocol advisor all carry prophets' names. POSSIBLE: Celeste knows the list and has never said so.
- **Hannah.** "Six Trials. Twelve girls. None of them hers" (TL), and the twelve are a whole career (FR 26). The biblical Hannah gives her only child to the sanctuary and brings him a robe every year. Naomi has one year left in Vowed education (TL), and whether Hannah enters her in the Trials turns on what happens to Eden (FR 29). POSSIBLE: Hannah's story is the biblical one run in reverse, a woman deciding whether to give her daughter to the institution she's served.
- **Abigail.** "Pressure in a pretty dress," a hand at the elbow, one soft line (WR 30). The biblical Abigail persuades by kneeling, taking the blame on herself and telling a dangerous man what he'll regret. POSSIBLE: The Arrangement (IX.5) has its oldest written form here.
- **Elias as Elijah.** POSSIBLE: The Temple reads its canonized founder (TL) as the Elijah Malachi promised would return, and named the Crown Heir Judah Elijah to carry it. Elijah's story also includes the collapse after the victory, which the Temple doesn't teach.
- **Esther and Vashti.** The Trials are a state search for young women with a long preparation (TR, II.1), and the Assessment uses "Esther's crown" (DR3). POSSIBLE: The Temple teaches Vashti immediately before Esther as the warning, and never teaches that Esther ends the book issuing a decree in her own name.
- **Mary and consent.** The angel waits for Mary's answer. At an Aurem Binding, "nobody asks if she consents. They ask if she is worthy" (DOM). INTERPRETATION: Every Binding held in a Temple that honors Mary contradicts the story it honors.
- **Huldah.** Officials take a found scroll to a woman for a ruling, and the kingdom acts on what she says. The ledger and the Covenant of Dissent are found documents (TL, FR 41–42). POSSIBLE: Huldah isn't in the Temple's syllabus. OPEN QUESTION: Who in the Empire would be asked to authenticate the Dissent if it surfaced?
- **Tamar's method.** She doesn't accuse. She collects evidence and lets the record speak. POSSIBLE: Eden's method in a world run by the Registry (VII.7), and the limit of it: Tamar had a law on her side that wasn't being enforced, and Eden's law is against her.
- **Mary Magdalene and the Eve narrative.** A reputation assigned centuries after the fact by a sermon and kept after the correction. The Broadcast's Eve narrative "implies and never accuses" (TR). POSSIBLE: The Broadcast keeps a file of the women it's used the narrative on, and their reputations outlast the Trial.
- **Jezebel.** The name became a stereotype used against Black women. Aurelia is built over Atlanta's Black memory (DOM), and Gulf candidates are penalized for warmth (DOM). POSSIBLE: "Jezebel" is the word the Broadcast never says on air and its editors use in the room.
- **The women of Romans 16 and Acts.** Titles like deacon and patron are rendered as domestic words in some translations. POSSIBLE: The Empire's designations do the same thing on purpose, and Stewardwife is the clearest case. Tabitha's widows and the Acts 6 distribution are mutual aid recorded as ministry, like Irongate's Kitchen and Meridian's Reading Circles (DOM).
- **The administrators.** Joseph, who builds the machine, and Pilate, who finds no case and signs anyway. POSSIBLE: The Crown advisor in the Pairing Introduction (TR) is a Pilate.
- **Nathan and the missing prophet.** David's court had one man who could say "You are the man" and live. OPEN QUESTION: Does anyone in Dorian's court have Nathan's office, licensed or not?
- **Samuel 8 and Jotham's fable.** Two anti-monarchy texts in the Empire's own canon. POSSIBLE: Pacific's grandmothers (DOM) know the bramble fable by heart.
- **Ebed-melech.** A foreign palace servant who goes to the king and pulls the prophet out of the cistern. POSSIBLE: Hannah, who knows the back staircases and the camera gaps (TL), is this kind of insider.
- **Jeremiah's scroll.** Burned and dictated again. Dorian removed the Covenant of Dissent (FR 42). POSSIBLE: Someone can still recite it, the way Celeste can recite the Covenant of Purpose (AM).
- **John the Baptist.** The gospels give a personal reason for his death and Josephus gives a political one. INTERPRETATION: The Empire's "sudden medical event" for Azrael and Petra (VI.2) works the same way, a private explanation for a public act.

#### Working questions

1. Which biblical names in the cast were chosen by a character in the story, and which by the Registry? Who named Leah, Zion and Solomon?
2. Does Hannah know she's named for a woman who gave her child to the sanctuary? Does Naomi?
3. What does Abigail say to Eden that the biblical Abigail said to David: that she'll regret a thing she hasn't done yet?
4. Which of the seven women prophets does the Temple teach, and which has it left out?
5. Write the Temple's lesson on Vashti for girls at Designation age, then the version Celeste told Eden.
6. Who in the Empire is a Huldah, someone officials would quietly take a document to? Is it a woman, and does anyone know she's consulted?
7. What evidence is Eden collecting, Tamar-style, and what will she do when the court turns out to be against her?
8. Which Covenant Daughter in earlier Trials got the Mary Magdalene treatment, and where is she now?
9. Does Judah know the end of Elijah's story, the cave and the silence? Who taught it to him?
10. Is there a Nathan in the Crownward? What happens to the first person who says "You are the man" to Dorian?
11. Which unnamed woman in the Empire's records will Eden give a name to?
12. Which biblical woman does Celeste name most often at the kitchen table, and which one won't she talk about?
