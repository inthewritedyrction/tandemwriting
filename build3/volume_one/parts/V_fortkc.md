#### For TKC

- **The composite empire.** POSSIBLE: The Empire uses the cheap methods: Babylon's elite capture, Persia's licensed religion and Rome's local clients. The Imperial Education Track takes exceptional common boys and makes them Crown-adjacent for life (MEN), which is Daniel 1. Governor-Prelates are Persia's licensed religion and Rome's client government in one office (TL, VII.6). Terror is kept for the edges: the Ungoverned, Azrael, the 2061 removal (TL, FR 35–36).
- **The seven powers.** The Empire holds the pulpit (the Temple and its ministers), the lectionary (what's read before the scores, DR3), the commentary (the Assessment's doctrine section, DR3) and much of literacy (the Designation, DOM). OPEN QUESTION: Does the Empire hold the translation and the copy? Is the Book of Covenant a full Bible, and who may own another one (VIII.2, I.7)? The powers it doesn't hold are where the plot can move.
- **The city on a hill.** The Covenant Coalition's platform was "America was great when it was godly. Return to God. Return to greatness" (TL). INTERPRETATION: The Coalition reads Winthrop's image the way later politicians did, as national greatness, and drops his point, which is that a covenant people is watched and judged by its failures. POSSIBLE: Elias's 2033 sermon quotes Winthrop's "choose life" and Deuteronomy 30 together.
- **The Coalition and Christian nationalism.** INTERPRETATION: The Coalition, as the timeline describes it (megachurches, political action groups and militias running candidates at every level, TL), fits Whitehead and Perry's definition of Christian nationalism closely. This is a reading of your own invented history, and real-world use of the term is CONTESTED.
- **The Dominions and Genesis 1:28.** The Dominions are named after a stewardship verse (AM, II.3), and dominionism is the reading of Genesis 1:28 as a mandate for Christian control of public life. POSSIBLE: Whoever named the Dominions held the dominionist reading and chose the word because of it.
- **The Triune Rule and the mountains.** The Seven Mountains name seven spheres. The Triune Rule puts three of them, religion, government and media, into the constitution as the Temple, the Crown and the Broadcast (TL). POSSIBLE: The Coalition's strategists used a version of the mandate, and the Empire's founding generation thought of the Trials as taking the "family" mountain.
- **Reconstructionism and the law.** POSSIBLE: The Covenant Court (VII.7) cites theonomist arguments for Imperial penalties, and quietly declines the parts of the Mosaic law that would bind the Crown (Deuteronomy 17, Leviticus 25, II.3).
- **The Slave Bible and the Book of Covenant.** POSSIBLE: The Book of Covenant is an abridgment on the 1807 model, the full canon on paper and a selection in practice. OPEN QUESTION (VIII.2).
- **Two Christianities.** Douglass set "the slaveholding religion of this land" against "the Christianity of Christ." INTERPRETATION: Part VIII draws the same line between faith the Temple can use and faith that answers to something it doesn't control. Celeste's faith is on Douglass's side of it.
- **Aurem's own history.** The Southern Baptist Convention was founded in Augusta, Georgia, in 1845 over slaveholding missionaries, and apologized in Atlanta in 1995. Aurem's territory includes Georgia and South Carolina, home of Furman's exposition and of Emanuel AME (DOM, X.1). POSSIBLE: Aurelia's outer districts remember the hush harbors, the burned church at Charleston and the walkout at St. George's, and the Temple teaches none of it.
- **Hush harbors.** INTERPRETATION: Worship held out of sight of the authorities, in places the cameras don't reach. The Grace Bridge (CR) and the outer districts' kitchens (DOM) are the Empire's hush harbors.
- **Danvers in the law.** The Temple's official theology of marriage (CR) is the complementarian position of the Danvers Statement, written into civil law and enforced at the counter (I.7, Part IX). INFERENCE: Christians for Biblical Equality's position has no legal standing in the Empire. OPEN QUESTION: Do the egalitarian arguments survive in Meridian's Reading Circles or in Heartland's theological inquiry (DOM)?
