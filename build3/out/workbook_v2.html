FRONT MATTER · WHAT THIS VOLUME IS

What this volume is

Volume One is the world: the scripture the Empire inherited, the history it rewrote, and the institutions, Dominions and Trials it built. This volume is the people who have to live inside all of that. It holds one dossier for every character with weight in the 39th Trial, from the woman at the kitchen table in Aurelia's outer districts to the man on the throne who taught himself never to be surprised.

A dossier here does what an entry in Volume One's people of the text does. It starts from what the files already say, keeps that separate from what's only inferred, and leaves most of the page ruled for you. The difference is that these people are yours. Nothing in a dossier outranks the manuscript, and where your files disagree with each other the page says so out loud instead of quietly picking a winner. Every major character gets a faith and scripture spread. It covers what they believe and who taught it to them, what the Temple teaches about someone like them, the biblical name or story they echo and where the echo breaks, the verses they'd quote and the ones nobody has ever read to them, and the exact pages in Volume One to go back to. The novel's religious line runs between faith the Temple can use and faith that answers to something it doesn't control. Every person in this book stands somewhere on that line, and the faith spread is where you find out where.

"Belief has a cost and a teacher." Rule IV, from Volume One.
What it isn't
It won't replace a cast list or a plot summary, and it won't answer anything for you. Where the files are silent the page is ruled, and the ruled space outranks anything printed near it.

Cross-references give the folio in Volume One, so "Vol. I, 393" means page 393 of The World Dossier, where Hannah, Abigail and Michal are.

FRONT MATTER · HOW A DOSSIER WORKS

How a dossier works

Every dossier opens with a portrait page, a frame for a drawing, a reference image or Canva art, and the person's record underneath it. After that the pages run in the same order for everyone at the same length, so you can read across the cast and see where the Empire holds and where it's already coming apart.

The full dossier, about sixteen pages
  1. The portrait and the record.
  2. The Registry card and every name they answer to.
  3. The years, set against the Empire's calendar.
  4. Standing, the clerk's version and the true one.
  5. Faith and scripture, what they believe and who taught it.
  6. Faith and scripture, the text: the name, the verses, Volume One.
  7. Body and fear, belief arc, power and people, voice and contradiction.
  8. The working pages: the machinery around them, one excavated scene, one ordinary hour, one relationship from both sides, and a scene lab.
The shorter dossiers

Thomas, Zion, Solomon, the House Aurem staff, Senior Minister Bell and the Prelate get about seven pages each, with the faith spread pressed onto a single page. Each Covenant Daughter gets about eight, with a page tracking her through the three phases.

The one rule
No character gets a belief they've never paid for. If the creed page names a conviction, the contradiction page has to name what it cost.
Photocopy first
The working pages expect a second and third draft. Copy them before you fill them.

THE PARISH HOUSEHOLD · HOW TO USE THIS PART

The household as a teaching

Everything Eden carries into the Crownward was assembled in one house, and most of it at one kitchen table. Celeste taught there. Leah comes back to it twice a week to breathe. Zion signs the forms that come to it, and Solomon is being coached at it on how much of his own mind to show. Miriam left it, and Thomas never lived in it (TL, MEN, FR 4–5).

The Empire reads this household as a set of failures on paper: an Unbound woman with no husband, a mother whose registration was deactivated, a father who relocated, a daughter under Temple review. Read the same facts from inside the house and they're a faith that has survived an institution by never handing it everything. The dossiers in this part are written from inside the house. They're also written after the Final Rulings, which changed this family more than any other. Thomas left voluntarily, and the paperwork that said so was accurate (FR 3). Celeste has no proof Miriam is alive (FR 16). Eden grew up as Celeste's child, and the younger three remember arriving into a house that was already running (FR 5). Solomon's father is someone else (FR 0). Any older page that says otherwise is SUPERSEDED.

THE PARISH HOUSEHOLD · CELESTE · STANDING

The clerk's version and the true one

On the ledger
Unbound, sixty-nine, head of a single-family household in the outer district. Never Bound, so never widowed in law. A woman with no husband, no office and no standing beyond what the reclassification granted her in writing (TL).
In the widows' row
She sits in the row reserved for widows at Temple, and nobody has ever asked her to move. Minister Bell has known her thirty-four years, eaten her sweet potato pie at harvest suppers, and called every winter when the Temple needed coats repaired. He greets her with both hands out. She looks at their joined hands and answers "Morning," and he keeps smiling (DR3).
The six-year filing
When Miriam left in 2090 she filed to reclassify the household under her own registration. Approval took six years, because she had to prove the arrangement was permanent, which meant proving her daughter wasn't coming back. She put on her one good blouse, went downtown, and said what she needed to say (TL).
The authority line
A form for Solomon came demanding an adult male authority. She wrote "deceased" on the line. It came back with the word circled and a request for the next adult male, and Zion, at eighteen, signed. She hadn't wanted him to (DR3).
INFERENCE
She has no office, no money and no husband, and she may be the most powerful person in her district.

THE COVENANT DAUGHTERS · HOW TO USE THIS PART

Eleven more people

Every one of these women was raised somewhere specific, sorted by a Designation built for her Dominion, nominated by someone with a reason, and sent to the Crownward carrying more than the Broadcast will ever show. The Writing Rules ask for their own rhythms, so Eden, Ruth, Zara, Delphine, Petra and Abigail don't all sound like Imperial brochures (WR 15). These dossiers are where you find the rhythms.

Each dossier runs the same way. The portrait and the Registry card come first, then her Dominion, a faith and scripture spread, her path through the three phases, Eden and her, and a working page. The phase page prints what the files already give, beat by beat, and leaves the rest ruled. Under the Final Rulings, all thirty-nine Trials place Anointed Wives. One goes to the heir and the other eleven are married down the elite ladder as designated, and nobody goes home unassigned (FR 19). A candidate who dies has her status returned to her family, with no compensation and no punishment (FR 24). Rejection follows a woman "not in the record, in the room" (FR 25).

The Temple teaches that God chose Elias Vane to restore the nation and that his bloodline carries the anointing forward. In the Crownward that isn't a metaphor. Everyone in this part performs for the Broadcast, and the dossiers keep asking what each of them does in the rooms it can't film: the Study, the side rooms at the Anointed Evening, the Binding Night, the Sovereign Garden behind its gates (CR).
