                      ORIENTATION · PART I · HOW THIS COMPANION WORKS



                      WHAT THE TANDEM COMPANION
                      IS
I.1                   Frame the book as a working writer-room reference, not a wiki or
                      workbook.

The Tandem Companion is the working reference for four novels set in one physical world: 3
Shadowmere Lane, For Their Entertainment, A Dream So Twisted, and The Sea Will Keep. It
holds the canon those books have already committed to, the rulings that settled their
contradictions, and the open questions they haven't answered yet. It sits beside the manuscript
while you draft. It isn't a replacement for it.

The Companion does five jobs. It remembers what's been ruled and drafted so you don't have
to reread three generations of files to find out. It distinguishes things a drafter will blur: the
Nightmare organization from the ancient entity, Katherine's Frost from Zephyr's, the forensic
record of Remy's death from Melena's guilt about it. It connects the books only where a ruling
or source connects them. It protects chronology and knowledge, so nobody in 3SL behaves as
if they've read ADST. And it catches resurrected ideas, the retired concepts that keep sneaking
back because they were once written down with confidence.

It refuses a few jobs on purpose. It won't fill a gap because the gap looks awkward in print. It
won't pick the nicer version of a conflict. It won't invent a sigil, a motto, a date, a map
coordinate, or a line of scripture to make a page feel finished. It isn't a fan wiki, a marketing
synopsis, or a workbook of blank lines waiting for you to do the thinking. When the sources
don't know something, the Companion says so plainly, and that sentence is worth more than a
confident guess.

                      RULE · PART I · HOW THIS COMPANION WORKS



                      THE MANUSCRIPT OUTRANKS THE
                      COMPANION
I.2                   State the single rule that keeps the Companion subordinate to the
                      books.

Drafted prose controls drafted events. If a chapter in the controlling generation shows
something happening, it happened, and the Companion records it. If that chapter deliberately
changes something the Companion already printed, the Companion changes. A printed page
never gets to veto a scene.

That rule has limits that matter as much as the rule. The manuscript controls what it actually
shows: POV, scene order, revealed identities, performed magic, relationships, and immediate
consequences. It doesn't automatically control national geography, deep history, or magical
taxonomy just because a character says something about them. When Silas thinks nobody born
in the Underside carries fire, that's a drafted fact about what Silas believes. It becomes world-
level canon only if a ruling or the governing worldbuilding agrees. A manuscript can also be
wrong by accident. A typo, a stray name from an older generation, or a detail that contradicts a
ruling isn't a deliberate change; it's a drafting flag, and the Companion logs it as one instead of
rewriting canon around it.

Open and provisional material works the other way. Where the Companion marks something
 OPEN or  SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL, drafting is allowed to settle it. When a scene commits to an

answer on purpose, that commitment becomes the new record, and the Companion's update
protocol (I.6) moves the old version to the Legacy Recovery Index rather than deleting it.
  GUARDRAIL

  Never bend a scene to match a Companion page. If the scene is right and the page is wrong, fix
  the page. If the scene contradicts an author ruling (a TCD), stop and decide which one you mean;
  don't let either quietly win.


Why it matters: the Companion is only trustworthy if it can't become a second, rival manuscript.
The moment a printed entry starts dictating prose, the books stop being written and start being
transcribed from an index.

                          HOW SOURCE AUTHORITY WORKS
I.3                       Teach the domain-specific ladder so a reader knows why a claim is
                          trusted.

Authority is domain-specific. A source can settle one kind of fact and have no say over another.
The 3SL draft is the last word on what happens in Dante Mercer's office in chapter 3; it isn't the
last word on how far Crownlight sits above sea level. The Thalara worldbuilding file governs
Thalaran geography; it can't overrule a ruling about the Drowning. Every claim in this book is
trusted because of the rung it stands on in its own domain.

Two rules sit above the ladder. First, the Tandem Source Authority Audit's rulings override
conflicting source material wherever they speak. Second, filenames and dates confer nothing.
"FINAL," "Official," "New," and a recent year are labels a file picked up along the way. FTE 2025
FINAL is Legacy. New FTE Chapters predates the modern Remnia execution. A later date on
the TSWK blueprint doesn't make it a ruling.

Worked example: the Beta versus the 2025 FINAL. FTE 2025 FINAL is a complete 47-
chapter manuscript. The FTE Official Beta Reader Copy stops at chapter 29. A reader who
trusts completeness picks the FINAL and gets the ending for free. TCD-001 says no: the Beta is
the controlling prose generation, and nothing outside it becomes canon until it's rewritten into
that generation. The FINAL still earns its shelf space as planning evidence for the unfinished
half, but its simple Dyad/Crossblood explanation, its reveal shortcut, its cause for Atlas's
deterioration, and its Tiran-only forfeit are all superseded by TCD-049 through TCD-052. The
complete file lost to the incomplete one because the incomplete one is the book being written.

                     READING A CLAIM: STATUS
                     MARKERS
I.4                  Teach the eight markers and the wording each allows.

A status marker attaches to a claim, not to a page. One dossier page can hold settled canon, a
drafted fact, a plan, and an open question side by side, and each keeps its own label. The
marker also controls the grammar. A POSSIBLE claim written in confident present tense is a
canon error even with the POSSIBLE sitting next to it, because readers believe sentences
before they believe badges.

I.7                    Make the book fast to use mid-scene.

Start with the book's draft-forward packet, not the front of the Companion. Each dossier ends
with pages built to sit open beside the manuscript: III.19 for 3SL, IV.24† for FTE, V.17† for
ADST, VI.13† for TSWK. They condense the locked beats, the current knowledge states, and the
scene-level open choices, and they cross-reference the full sections behind them.

BEFORE A SCENE

   Check the packet for any GUARDRAIL that touches the characters or magic in the scene.
   Check the knowledge ledger for what each POV character knows at this chapter, not at the
   end of the book.
   Check whether the scene touches a        CANON ruling. If it does, the outcome is fixed and the

   scene's job is to earn it.
   Check whether the scene touches an        OPEN item. If the open item doesn't affect this scene,

   keep drafting.

WHILE DRAFTING

   An open question that doesn't touch the next scene doesn't stop the next scene.
   If the scene needs an answer to an open item, make the choice in prose and flag it. You
   don't need a ruling first; you need a record afterward.
   If the scene seems to need something that contradicts a ruling, stop. That's the one case
   where the draft waits.
   Don't pull a fact from a Legacy file to fill a gap, however well it fits. Check the Legacy
   Recovery Index; if the idea is listed there as recoverable, it comes back as     POSSIBLE and

   gets ruled on like anything new.

                       MACRO GEOGRAPHY
II.2                   Place the lands relative to each other without inventing cartography.

The four lands occupy multiple major landmasses and regions in one world.              CANON TCD-025.

Four placements are fixed:

   Remnia and Somnoria are separate continental landmasses with ocean between them.
   The Underside attaches to the Zenithia side, or corner, of Remnia's broader geography and
   descends from there. CANON TCD-025, TCD-026
   Thalara, primarily underwater, lies farther outward in the island and sea region.
   Relative position is described by a Spain / France / Italy / Greek-islands comparison. That
   comparison controls relative placement only.
  GUARDRAIL

  What the analogy does not mean.
  It doesn't import real borders, coastlines, scale, distances, climate, culture, languages, or history.
  It doesn't make any land a disguised version of a real country. It doesn't settle which way
  anything faces on a map. It tells you roughly where the lands sit in relation to one another, and
  nothing more.


The Underside's placement deserves its own sentence because it's the easiest to get wrong. It
isn't a buried continent lying flat beneath Remnia. It's a vertically tiered nation that begins at or
near the surface off Zenithia's side and descends. Its top tiers, Crownlight and Dawnveil, are
above ground; by Ironcross the descent is fully subterranean. CANON TCD-026. Part III builds
the full cross-section.

II.4                   Kill universal-awareness shortcuts.

There's no universal answer to "do people know about the other lands?" Awareness varies by
nation, class, institution, profession, network, person, and time.         CANON TCD-016. A smuggler, a

Sanctum archivist, and a miner's kid in the same land can hold three different pictures, and all
three can change between chapter 5 and chapter 25.

The six levels from I.5 do the sorting: existence, location, functional, route, historical, and true
Tandem knowledge. None implies another. Someone can know Somnoria's name and nothing
about where it is. Someone can use a route without knowing what's on the far side of the
boundary it crosses. Someone can know a land's history as their own institution teaches it,
which may be the least reliable version available.

Four kinds of source feed a character's picture, and the Companion tracks them separately:
what their institution teaches or officially claims, what their network passes around as rumor or
trade knowledge, what's been suppressed or rewritten, and what they've learned firsthand. An
error counts as knowledge for drafting purposes. A character acts on what she believes, and the
Companion records the belief with its lane label.

                       SHARED MAGICAL ANCESTRY
II.5                   Explain shared deep origin without making magics interchangeable.

All four modern magical traditions ultimately draw from one primordial magical source or
substrate that predates the modern division of the Tandem world. CANON TCD-035. The Divide
didn't create four unrelated magics. Geography, environment, bodies, bloodlines, culture,
survival, history, and institutions reshaped one shared power into systems that are now
genuinely distinct, with their own access, costs, classifications, and cultural explanations.

Both halves of that ruling carry weight. The shared root means cross-land resonance is possible
without anyone inventing a coincidence. The genuine distinctness means a Somnorian dream
technique doesn't work in Remnia just because both descend from the same source, and a
Thalaran seal cadence isn't a Silencecraft seal with a different accent. Long isolation after the
Divide accelerated the specialization.      CANON TCD-045

III.2                 What's definitely true at page one and what pressure drives the book.

As drafted, 3SL runs three engines at once and lets them collide. Each started in a different
family, and by chapter 22 they've fused into one crisis in one loading cut.

Engine 1 — The princess in the safehouse. Mother Rae brings Melena, Remnia's Imperial
Princess and Lucia's daughter, into Dante Mercer's office with no route papers, no belongings,
and a sealed letter from her dead mother. Dante assigns Silas to keep her alive and hidden
until Rae decides where she goes. Melena's Fire can't be controlled: she burns through a
Mercer lock, nearly exposes herself to a Shroud patrol, overdoses on Hush at Blue Circle and
sets the den alight, and wrecks the safehouse on blackroot. Remy becomes the one person who
can bring her back down. (   CURRENT DRAFTED TEXT, chs. 3, 5, 7, 11)


Engine 2 — Asha's counterfeit route. Isola finds a counterfeit Veilmark in her cousin Asha's
bag. Asha has made six sealed deliveries for Blue Circle, paid on a black route strip with a
balance of one hundred eighty Veils and a promise that her name clears when it closes. The
coins are high-grade forgeries built to pass upward, bound with the same compound as the
stolen Dawnveil token that disappeared Jerrick's runners. Silas verifies the coin at Halflight; the
Synod then seals and audits Ashvein's Sixth Ward, strips Micah's Ironcross sponsorship, and
orders Asha to explain Blue Circle at third bell. Nobody from the Synod touches Blue Six, the
dead box the counterfeit actually moves through. ( CURRENT DRAFTED TEXT, chs. 2, 6, 8–10, 12–13)

Engine 3 — Silas and Isola. They collide at Pell's stall, meet properly across the Ironcross line
at Halflight, and part with Silas's direct route tag, black three, in her coat. When a Shroud lays a
death tether in Isola's leg and chases her to Halflight's sealed Ashvein gate, she uses it. Silas
opens Graveshand, severs the claim, unmakes the Shroud, breaks the Synod gate, and carries
her into Shadowmere. Then, to keep her breathing, he claims her under Graves law and binds
her name into his wrist without her consent. The tether that results keeps them both alive and
won't let them separate. ( CURRENT DRAFTED TEXT, chs. 4, 10, 14–18)

III.5                 Map who holds power over movement, law, and violence.

The Synod governs by controlling movement, names, and memory, and almost every drafted
scene is shaped by who else has learned to work around it. As drafted, the Synod's authority
reaches every tier through the Doctrine of Descent (announced on the trams), its enforcement
arms (Shrouds and Quietcasters), and its paperwork (route inspections, household audits, work
chits). Its currency carries its creed: every legal Veilmark bears the Veiled One's face and the
prayer The Veil protects. (   CURRENT DRAFTED TEXT, chs. 1, 2, 9, 12)


The worldbuilding adds a fuller government above what the draft shows: a theocratic hierarchy
under the Order of Silence, with the High Confessor in Crownlight as spiritual and political
head, the Synod as a council of nine elders who approve major decisions and death sentences,
Viceroys over each tier, and Enforcers of Silence as theocratic police.          SUPPORTED / PROVISIONAL.

The draft names the Synod constantly and hasn't yet put the High Confessor, the Viceroys, or
the Order of Silence on the page.

III.8                 The book's magical core, taught precisely.

Graveshand is inherited Graves-family magic, and it sits outside the formal Six Disciplines.
  CANON TCD-004. Deathwork is the broader category: ending-magic and its effects, which

Graveshand can perceive, manipulate, sever, redirect, or create. A Shroud's death tether is
Deathwork. Silas's answer to it is Graveshand.
   NOT THE SAME AS

  Graveshand / Deathwork.
  Deathwork is what endings do. Graveshand is what the Graves line can do to endings. A Shroud
  uses Deathwork without Graveshand; Silas uses Graveshand on Deathwork. (TCD-004)


What Graveshand is, as the draft shows it. Silas's tattoos aren't decoration. They're Graves
marks, death names Camille bound into him: "one for the family, one for the route, one for
everything that came through and needed putting back" (ch. 15). Opening Graveshand doesn't
just let him touch death. It gives death a route back to him. Once he opens it, every dead thing
nearby knows exactly where he stands, and so does anything else listening (ch. 15). Camille
taught all three of her children the family rule before she taught them the working: never call
the dead unless you're prepared to answer what comes. Severance calls louder than anything. (
  CURRENT DRAFTED TEXT, ch. 15)
